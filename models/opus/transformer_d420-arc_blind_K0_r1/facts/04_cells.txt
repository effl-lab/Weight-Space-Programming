# Cells, microorganisms, classification
the cell is the basic unit of structure and function of all living things
all living things are made of one or more cells
cell theory says all living things are made of cells and cells come from existing cells
new cells are produced by cell division of existing cells
robert hooke first observed and named cells using a microscope looking at cork
leeuwenhoek first observed tiny living microorganisms with a microscope
a microscope is used to observe cells and microorganisms too small to see
unicellular single celled organisms are made of one cell such as bacteria and amoeba
multicellular organisms are made of many cells such as plants and animals
the nucleus controls the activities of the cell and contains dna genetic material
the nucleus contains chromosomes made of dna
the cell membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell
the cell membrane is a thin flexible layer surrounding the cell
the cell wall is a rigid layer outside the membrane that supports and protects plant cells
plant cells have a cell wall chloroplasts and a large central vacuole
chloroplasts are where photosynthesis takes place in plant cells
chloroplasts contain chlorophyll a green pigment that captures light energy
mitochondria release energy from food through cellular respiration
mitochondria are the powerhouse of the cell producing energy
the cytoplasm is the jelly like fluid inside the cell where organelles are found
ribosomes make proteins
vacuoles store water food and wastes
the large vacuole in plant cells stores water and helps keep the plant firm
the endoplasmic reticulum transports materials within the cell
golgi bodies package and ship proteins
lysosomes break down wastes and worn out cell parts
organelles are tiny structures inside cells that do specific jobs
prokaryotic cells such as bacteria have no nucleus
eukaryotic cells have a nucleus and membrane bound organelles
bacteria are single celled prokaryotes without a nucleus
animal cells have a nucleus cell membrane cytoplasm and mitochondria
red blood cells carry oxygen and have no nucleus
nerve cells neurons carry messages signals through the body
muscle cells contract to make movement
cells are organized into tissues organs organ systems and organisms
a tissue is a group of similar cells working together
an organ is a group of tissues working together such as the heart
an organ system is a group of organs that work together
levels of organization cell tissue organ organ system organism
diffusion is the movement of particles from high concentration to low concentration
osmosis is the diffusion of water across a cell membrane
cells take in nutrients and oxygen and release waste and carbon dioxide
mitosis is cell division that produces two identical cells for growth and repair
mitosis produces body cells with the same number of chromosomes
meiosis produces sex cells gametes egg and sperm with half the chromosomes
cells divide for growth and to replace damaged worn out cells
cancer is uncontrolled cell division
stem cells can develop into different types of cells
specialized cells have different structures for different functions
root hair cells increase surface area to absorb water
guard cells open and close stomata in leaves
microorganisms microbes include bacteria protists fungi and viruses
bacteria are found almost everywhere in soil water air and inside bodies
some bacteria cause diseases such as strep throat and food poisoning
some bacteria are helpful decomposers and help digest food in intestines
bacteria are used to make yogurt cheese and other foods
antibiotics kill bacteria
viruses can only reproduce inside a living host cell
viruses cause colds flu chickenpox measles and covid
vaccines help the body build immunity against viruses and diseases
fungi include mushrooms molds and yeast
fungi absorb nutrients from dead or living matter and are decomposers
yeast is a fungus used to make bread rise by producing carbon dioxide gas
mold is a fungus that grows on old bread and food
fungi reproduce with spores
mushrooms are fungi that absorb food from dead matter as decomposers
protists include amoeba paramecium euglena and algae
algae are plant like protists that make food by photosynthesis
amoebas move using pseudopods
paramecium moves using cilia
euglena moves using a flagellum and can make food with chloroplasts
classification groups organisms by similar characteristics
the classification levels are kingdom phylum class order family genus species
a species is a group of similar organisms that can mate and produce fertile offspring
scientific names use genus and species binomial nomenclature
linnaeus developed the system for classifying and naming organisms
organisms are classified into domains bacteria archaea eukarya
kingdoms include animals plants fungi protists bacteria archaebacteria eubacteria
a dichotomous key is used to identify organisms by choosing between two descriptions
organisms in the same genus are closely related
vertebrates are animals with a backbone spine
invertebrates are animals without a backbone such as insects worms jellyfish spiders
the five groups of vertebrates are fish amphibians reptiles birds and mammals
mammals have hair or fur produce milk for their young and are warm blooded
most mammals give birth to live young
whales dolphins and bats are mammals
birds have feathers wings beaks lay hard shelled eggs and are warm blooded
reptiles have dry scaly skin lay leathery eggs on land and are cold blooded
snakes lizards turtles crocodiles and alligators are reptiles
amphibians live in water when young and on land as adults
frogs toads and salamanders are amphibians with moist skin
frogs lay eggs in water
fish live in water have gills fins and scales and are cold blooded
sharks are fish with skeletons made of cartilage
cold blooded ectotherm animals body temperature changes with the environment
warm blooded endotherm animals keep a constant body temperature
insects have six legs three body parts head thorax abdomen and antennae
spiders are arachnids with eight legs and two body parts
arthropods have jointed legs and an exoskeleton such as insects spiders crabs
an exoskeleton is a hard outer covering that protects and supports
crabs lobsters and shrimp are crustaceans
mollusks have soft bodies and often shells such as snails clams squid and octopus
worms are invertebrates with soft long bodies
jellyfish and corals are invertebrates with stinging cells
sponges are simple invertebrates that filter water for food
echinoderms such as sea stars have spiny skin
plants are classified as vascular or nonvascular
mosses are small nonvascular plants
ferns are vascular plants that reproduce with spores
conifers are gymnosperms that produce seeds in cones
flowering plants are angiosperms that produce seeds in fruit
monocots and dicots are groups of flowering plants
plants are producers that make their own food
animals are consumers that eat other organisms for food
living things grow reproduce respond to their environment need energy and are made of cells
living things need food water air and space to survive
nonliving things include rocks water and air
organisms are living things
the characteristics of life include growth reproduction response metabolism and cells
homeostasis is keeping stable internal conditions
cells need energy to carry out life processes
