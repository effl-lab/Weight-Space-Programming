# Human body systems, health, nutrition, disease
the circulatory system moves blood through the body carrying oxygen and nutrients to cells
the heart pumps blood through blood vessels arteries veins and capillaries
arteries carry blood away from the heart
veins carry blood back to the heart
capillaries are tiny blood vessels where oxygen and nutrients pass to cells
red blood cells carry oxygen using hemoglobin
white blood cells fight infection germs and disease
platelets help blood clot to stop bleeding
plasma is the liquid part of blood
the heart has four chambers two atria and two ventricles
the heart is a muscle
exercise makes the heart beat faster and breathing faster to supply more oxygen to muscles
pulse heart rate increases during exercise
the respiratory system takes in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide
the lungs are the main organs of the respiratory system
air enters through the nose and mouth passes through the trachea windpipe to the bronchi and lungs
alveoli are tiny air sacs in the lungs where oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide leaves
the diaphragm is a muscle that helps us breathe
we inhale oxygen and exhale carbon dioxide
the circulatory and respiratory systems work together to deliver oxygen to cells and remove carbon dioxide
the digestive system breaks down food into nutrients the body can absorb
digestion begins in the mouth where teeth chew food and saliva breaks down starch
the esophagus carries food from the mouth to the stomach
the stomach uses acid and enzymes to break down food
most digestion and absorption of nutrients happens in the small intestine
the small intestine absorbs nutrients into the blood through villi
the large intestine colon absorbs water and forms solid waste
the liver makes bile to help digest fats and filters toxins from blood
the pancreas makes digestive enzymes and insulin
enzymes speed up chemical reactions such as digestion
the digestive and circulatory systems work together to deliver nutrients to cells
the excretory system removes wastes from the body
the kidneys filter wastes from the blood and make urine
the bladder stores urine
the skin lungs and kidneys remove wastes
sweat glands in the skin release sweat to cool the body
the skeletal system supports the body protects organs and allows movement with muscles
bones store calcium and make blood cells in marrow
the skull protects the brain
the rib cage protects the heart and lungs
the spine backbone vertebrae protects the spinal cord
joints are where bones meet such as knees elbows and shoulders
ligaments connect bones to bones at joints
tendons connect muscles to bones
cartilage cushions joints and is found in the nose and ears
the muscular system moves the body by contracting muscles
muscles work in pairs one contracts while the other relaxes
skeletal muscles move bones
the biceps and triceps muscles move the arm
smooth muscle moves food through the digestive system
cardiac muscle is found in the heart
the nervous system controls the body and sends messages signals
the brain spinal cord and nerves make up the nervous system
the brain is the control center of the body
neurons nerve cells carry electrical impulses messages
the senses sight hearing smell taste touch send information to the brain
the eye detects light and the brain interprets images
the ear detects sound vibrations
the eardrum vibrates when sound waves reach it
the nose detects smells and the tongue detects tastes
the skin senses touch pressure heat cold and pain
a reflex is a quick automatic response such as pulling your hand away from a hot stove
the endocrine system uses hormones chemical messages to control body functions
glands release hormones into the blood
hormones are chemical messengers carried by the blood
insulin is a hormone that controls blood sugar
adrenaline is a hormone that prepares the body for fight or flight
the endocrine system controls growth development and puberty
the immune system defends the body against germs pathogens and disease
antibodies are proteins made by white blood cells that fight specific pathogens
vaccines cause the body to make antibodies and become immune
the skin is the first barrier against germs
the integumentary system is the skin hair and nails that protect the body
the skin is the largest organ of the body
the reproductive system produces offspring
the male reproductive system produces sperm and the female reproductive system produces eggs
fertilization happens when a sperm joins an egg
a fertilized egg zygote develops into an embryo and then a fetus
the placenta supplies the developing baby with oxygen and nutrients
the lymphatic system returns fluid to the blood and helps fight infections
body systems work together to keep the body healthy
homeostasis keeps body temperature and conditions stable
normal human body temperature is about 37 degrees celsius 98.6 fahrenheit
shivering produces heat to warm the body
sweating cools the body by evaporation
nutrients in food include carbohydrates proteins fats vitamins minerals and water
carbohydrates sugars and starches provide energy
proteins are needed to build and repair muscles and tissues
fats store energy and insulate the body
vitamins and minerals keep the body healthy
calcium builds strong bones and teeth and is found in milk and dairy
iron is needed for healthy red blood cells
vitamin c from citrus fruits prevents scurvy
vitamin d is made by the skin in sunlight and helps absorb calcium
fiber from fruits vegetables and whole grains helps digestion
water is needed by all cells and is most of the body's mass
a balanced diet includes fruits vegetables grains protein and dairy
eating too much fat sugar and salt can cause health problems
calories measure the energy in food
exercise strengthens the heart muscles and bones
sleep helps the body rest and repair
smoking damages the lungs and causes lung cancer and heart disease
alcohol and drugs harm the brain and liver
infectious diseases are caused by germs pathogens such as bacteria viruses fungi and parasites
germs spread through the air by coughing and sneezing through touch and contaminated water or food
washing hands with soap helps prevent the spread of germs and disease
covering coughs and sneezes prevents spreading germs
the flu and the common cold are caused by viruses
antibiotics treat bacterial infections
noninfectious diseases include diabetes heart disease and cancer
diabetes is a disease where the body cannot control blood sugar insulin
allergies are when the immune system reacts to harmless substances like pollen
asthma narrows the airways making breathing difficult
mosquitoes can spread diseases such as malaria
cooking food thoroughly and refrigerating it prevents food poisoning
louis pasteur showed germs cause disease and developed pasteurization
pasteurization heats milk to kill harmful bacteria
edward jenner developed the first vaccine for smallpox
alexander fleming discovered penicillin the first antibiotic
sunscreen protects skin from ultraviolet rays sunburn and skin cancer
wearing a helmet protects the head and brain from injury
growth and development stages infant child adolescent adult
puberty is when the body changes during adolescence controlled by hormones
the brain controls breathing heartbeat and other involuntary actions
voluntary muscles are controlled consciously and involuntary muscles work automatically
teeth incisors cut canines tear molars grind food
the appendix and tonsils are organs
blood type is inherited
the pupil of the eye gets smaller in bright light
the lens of the eye focuses light on the retina
glasses with lenses correct vision problems
the cochlea in the inner ear sends sound signals to the brain
balance is controlled by the inner ear and brain
the heart and lungs are in the chest
exercise uses energy from food
muscles produce heat when they work
dehydration happens when the body loses too much water
drinking water replaces water lost by sweating
the body gets energy from food through cellular respiration in cells
cellular respiration uses oxygen and glucose to release energy carbon dioxide and water
a fever is a higher body temperature that helps fight infection
