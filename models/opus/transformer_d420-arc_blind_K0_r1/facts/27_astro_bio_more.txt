# astronomy details
the sun is about 109 times the diameter of earth
the sun is a star at the center of our solar system
stars are huge balls of hot glowing gas
the sun appears to rise and set because earth spins
the north star stays fixed in the sky because it is above earth's north pole
star trails in long exposure photos show earth's rotation
the moon appears to change shape during a month
the moon takes about one month to orbit earth
from earth we always see the same side of the moon
the far side of the moon is never seen from earth
the moon has highlands and dark plains called maria
the moon's surface temperature changes greatly between day and night because it has no atmosphere
astronauts first landed on the moon in 1969 with apollo 11
neil armstrong was the first person to walk on the moon
the moon's gravity is weaker so astronauts can jump higher on the moon
a person weighs about one sixth as much on the moon
jupiter's gravity is stronger so a person would weigh more on jupiter
mars has the largest volcano olympus mons
mars has seasons because its axis is tilted
venus spins backward and is covered with thick clouds of sulfuric acid
mercury has almost no atmosphere and extreme temperatures
saturn is less dense than water
jupiter has dozens of moons and a faint ring
uranus and neptune are ice giants
pluto was reclassified as a dwarf planet in 2006
comets come from the kuiper belt and oort cloud
halley's comet returns about every 76 years
meteor showers happen when earth passes through comet debris
the asteroid belt contains rocky leftovers from the formation of the solar system
the solar system is about 4.6 billion years old
planets formed from dust and gas orbiting the young sun
gravity pulled gas and dust together to form the sun and planets
the sun will shine for about 5 billion more years
stars twinkle because their light passes through moving air in the atmosphere
planets do not twinkle as much as stars
the brightest star in the night sky is sirius
betelgeuse is a red supergiant star in orion
orion is a winter constellation
the zodiac constellations lie along the path of the sun
our galaxy the milky way is a spiral galaxy about 100000 light years across
the sun is located in one of the spiral arms of the milky way
andromeda is the nearest large galaxy to the milky way
a solar system is a star with planets and other objects orbiting it
exoplanets are planets that orbit other stars
astronomers use spectroscopes to study the composition of stars
the light from stars shows which elements they contain
radio telescopes detect radio waves from space
space telescopes detect x rays ultraviolet and infrared light blocked by the atmosphere
rockets must reach high speed to escape earth's gravity
satellites stay in orbit because they move fast sideways while gravity pulls them
the space shuttle and space station carried astronauts
the international space station is a laboratory in orbit
the hubble telescope took pictures of distant galaxies
voyager probes have traveled beyond the planets
rovers such as curiosity and perseverance explore mars
the first artificial satellite was sputnik
gravity holds the moon in orbit around earth and earth in orbit around the sun
without gravity the moon would move in a straight line into space
the strength of gravity depends on mass and distance
objects in space travel in curved paths called orbits
the period of revolution increases with distance from the sun
earth's average distance from the sun stays about the same during the year
earth is closest to the sun in january
the southern hemisphere has summer in december
at the equator day and night are about equal all year
near the poles summer has almost constant daylight and winter has almost constant darkness
the sun's path across the sky is higher in summer and lower in winter
the noon shadow is shortest in summer and longest in winter
# biology details
the heart has four chambers the right side pumps blood to the lungs and the left side pumps blood to the body
blood picks up oxygen in the lungs and delivers it to the cells
blood carries carbon dioxide from the cells to the lungs
oxygen enters the blood in the alveoli of the lungs
the diaphragm contracts to pull air into the lungs
the trachea is the windpipe that carries air to the lungs
the epiglottis keeps food out of the windpipe
the villi in the small intestine absorb nutrients into the blood
the pancreas releases insulin to control blood sugar
the thyroid gland controls metabolism
the pituitary gland controls growth
adrenal glands release adrenaline in emergencies
the cerebrum controls thinking memory and voluntary movement
the cerebellum controls balance and coordination
the brain stem controls breathing and heartbeat
sensory neurons carry signals to the brain and motor neurons carry signals to muscles
the retina contains rods and cones that detect light and color
the cornea and lens focus light in the eye
the outer ear collects sound the middle ear bones vibrate and the cochlea sends signals to the brain
taste buds on the tongue detect sweet sour salty and bitter
the nose has receptors that detect smells
skin has nerve endings for touch pain heat and cold
the skin produces vitamin d with sunlight
hair and nails are made of keratin
bones are living tissue that can grow and heal
bone marrow makes blood cells
the femur thigh bone is the longest bone
an adult human has 206 bones
babies have more bones than adults because some bones fuse
calcium and vitamin d keep bones strong
muscles pull on bones to move them
voluntary muscles such as arm muscles are controlled by choice
the heart is made of cardiac muscle that never tires
the stomach and intestines have smooth muscle
germs include bacteria viruses fungi and protozoa
the body's first defenses are skin mucus tears and stomach acid
white blood cells engulf germs and make antibodies
a vaccine contains weakened or dead germs that train the immune system
immunity means the body can resist a disease
allergies are reactions to harmless substances such as pollen dust and peanuts
diabetes affects how the body uses sugar
heart disease can be reduced by exercise and a healthy diet
skin cancer can be caused by too much ultraviolet light
lung cancer is mostly caused by smoking
drinking enough water prevents dehydration
the food pyramid my plate shows healthy food groups
proteins are found in meat fish eggs beans and nuts
carbohydrates are found in bread rice pasta and potatoes
fats are found in butter oil and nuts
vitamins are found in fruits and vegetables
dairy foods such as milk and cheese provide calcium
fiber is found in whole grains fruits and vegetables
the energy in food is measured in calories
a cell's dna is copied before the cell divides
dna is found in chromosomes in the nucleus
genes code for proteins that produce traits
each parent passes one allele for each gene to the offspring
a trait controlled by a dominant allele appears when at least one dominant allele is present
two brown eyed parents can have a blue eyed child if both carry the recessive allele
a punnett square shows possible combinations of alleles
a carrier has one recessive allele for a trait but does not show it
genetic variation comes from sexual reproduction and mutations
cloned organisms have identical dna
plants can be cloned from cuttings
farmers breed animals with useful traits selective breeding
genetic engineering inserts genes into organisms such as insulin making bacteria
biotechnology uses living things to make products
fossils of whales with legs show whales evolved from land animals
similar embryos of fish chickens and humans suggest common ancestry
the more similar the dna the more closely related two species are
organisms that are well adapted survive and reproduce
over time a population may change as its environment changes
extinction happens when a species cannot adapt to change
the dodo bird became extinct because of hunting and introduced animals
cells in a plant root tip divide quickly
plant cells have cell walls made of cellulose
plants store energy as starch and animals store energy as fat and glycogen
a plant cell has a large vacuole that pushes against the cell wall to keep it firm
guard cells control the opening and closing of stomata
stomata usually close at night and in dry weather to save water
water moves up a plant through xylem by transpiration pull
food moves through phloem from leaves to roots and fruits
the leaf's upper surface has a waxy cuticle
palisade cells in the leaf contain many chloroplasts
roots have root hairs that increase surface area for absorbing water
