# Weather, climate, atmosphere, water cycle, oceans
the water cycle is the continuous movement of water between earth's surface and the atmosphere
the sun's energy drives the water cycle
evaporation happens when liquid water is heated by the sun and changes into water vapor gas
evaporation turns liquid water from oceans lakes and puddles into water vapor
water evaporates faster when it is warm sunny windy and dry
puddles dry up because water evaporates
condensation happens when water vapor cools and changes into liquid water droplets
clouds form when water vapor in the air cools and condenses into tiny water droplets
dew forms when water vapor condenses on cool grass in the morning
water droplets form on the outside of a cold glass because water vapor in the air condenses
fog is a cloud that forms near the ground
precipitation is water that falls from clouds as rain snow sleet or hail
rain falls when water droplets in clouds combine and become too heavy
snow forms when water vapor freezes into ice crystals in cold clouds
sleet is rain that freezes into ice pellets as it falls through cold air
hail forms when raindrops are carried up and down in thunderstorm clouds and freeze in layers
runoff is water that flows over the ground into streams rivers lakes and oceans
infiltration is when water soaks into the ground to become groundwater
groundwater is water stored underground in soil and rock aquifers
an aquifer is an underground layer of rock that holds groundwater
transpiration is when plants release water vapor through their leaves stomata
water is stored in glaciers ice caps oceans lakes and groundwater
most of earth's water is salt water in the oceans about 97 percent
most of earth's fresh water is frozen in glaciers and ice caps
only a small amount of earth's water is fresh liquid water available for people to drink
the oceans cover about 70 percent three fourths of earth's surface
ocean water is salty because it contains dissolved salts minerals
weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a certain place and time
climate is the average weather of a region over a long period of many years
weather changes daily but climate describes long term patterns
temperature is how hot or cold the air is measured with a thermometer
air pressure is measured with a barometer
wind speed is measured with an anemometer
wind direction is shown by a wind vane weather vane
rainfall precipitation is measured with a rain gauge
humidity is the amount of water vapor in the air measured with a hygrometer
relative humidity is high when the air holds a lot of water vapor
falling air pressure usually means stormy rainy weather is coming
rising air pressure usually means clear fair weather
high pressure systems bring clear dry sunny weather
low pressure systems bring clouds rain and storms
wind is caused by differences in air pressure and uneven heating of earth's surface
air moves from areas of high pressure to areas of low pressure making wind
uneven heating of earth by the sun causes wind and convection currents in the air
a sea breeze blows from the cool ocean toward the warm land during the day
a land breeze blows from the land toward the sea at night
land heats up and cools down faster than water
water heats and cools more slowly than land because water has a high specific heat
large bodies of water keep coastal climates mild moderate temperatures
coastal areas have milder winters and cooler summers than inland areas
air masses are large bodies of air with similar temperature and humidity
a front is the boundary where two air masses meet
a cold front forms when cold air pushes under warm air causing thunderstorms and cooler weather
a warm front forms when warm air moves over cold air bringing steady rain
a stationary front brings days of cloudy wet weather
meteorologists study and forecast weather
meteorologists use weather maps satellites radar and balloons to predict weather
weather satellites track clouds and hurricanes from space
doppler radar tracks precipitation and storms
isobars on a weather map connect places with equal air pressure
cumulus clouds are puffy white clouds seen in fair weather
cumulonimbus clouds are tall dark clouds that bring thunderstorms lightning and heavy rain
stratus clouds are flat gray layers that cover the sky and bring drizzle
cirrus clouds are thin wispy clouds high in the sky made of ice crystals
nimbus clouds produce rain or snow
thunderstorms bring lightning thunder heavy rain and strong winds
lightning is a giant electric spark discharge of static electricity in a storm cloud
thunder is the sound caused by lightning rapidly heating and expanding the air
we see lightning before we hear thunder because light travels faster than sound
a tornado is a violently rotating funnel column of air touching the ground
tornadoes form from severe thunderstorms
tornado alley in the central united states has many tornadoes
a hurricane is a large tropical storm with very strong winds and heavy rain that forms over warm ocean water
hurricanes get energy from warm ocean water and weaken over land
the eye of a hurricane is calm
hurricanes cause storm surges flooding and wind damage along coasts
a blizzard is a snowstorm with strong winds and very cold temperatures
a drought is a long period with little or no rain
droughts cause crops to fail and water shortages
floods happen when heavy rain causes rivers to overflow
el nino is a warming of pacific ocean water that changes weather patterns
the atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding earth
air is a mixture of gases mostly nitrogen about 78 percent and oxygen about 21 percent
air contains small amounts of argon carbon dioxide and water vapor
nitrogen is the most abundant gas in earth's atmosphere
the troposphere is the lowest layer of the atmosphere where weather happens
the stratosphere contains the ozone layer
the ozone layer protects living things from harmful ultraviolet rays
the mesosphere is where most meteors burn up
the thermosphere is the hottest layer of the atmosphere
air pressure decreases as altitude height increases
air temperature generally decreases with altitude in the troposphere
mountain tops are colder because air is thinner at high altitude
the atmosphere keeps earth warm by trapping heat greenhouse effect
greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide methane and water vapor trap heat in the atmosphere
the greenhouse effect is when gases in the atmosphere trap heat from the sun
increasing carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels causes global warming
global warming is the increase in earth's average temperature
climate change causes melting glaciers rising sea levels and more extreme weather
melting ice caps and glaciers cause sea level to rise
burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide into the atmosphere
deforestation increases carbon dioxide because fewer trees absorb it
volcanic eruptions release ash and gases that can cool climate by blocking sunlight
ice ages were long periods when glaciers covered much of earth
climate zones polar temperate tropical depend on latitude
latitude is distance north or south of the equator
places near the equator have warm tropical climates
places at high latitudes near the poles have cold climates
deserts receive very little rain precipitation
rainforests receive a lot of rain and are warm and humid
mountains cause rain on the windward side and dry deserts on the leeward side rain shadow
air rises cools and condenses forming clouds and precipitation
warm air rises and cold air sinks
warm air is less dense than cold air
warm air can hold more water vapor than cold air
the dew point is the temperature at which water vapor condenses
ocean currents carry warm and cold water around the earth and affect climate
the gulf stream is a warm ocean current that warms western europe
ocean currents are caused by wind and differences in water temperature and salinity density
cold ocean water is denser than warm water and sinks
salt water is denser than fresh water
waves in the ocean are caused mainly by wind
tides are the regular rise and fall of sea level caused by the moon's gravity
the sea floor has mountains ridges trenches and plains
the mariana trench is the deepest part of the ocean
sunlight only reaches the upper layer of the ocean
estuaries are where fresh river water mixes with salt ocean water
water vapor is an invisible gas in the air
seasons cause changes in temperature and weather patterns
weather forecasts predict future weather using patterns and data
a weather station records temperature precipitation wind and pressure
clear nights are colder because heat escapes without cloud cover
clouds keep nights warmer by trapping heat
frost forms when water vapor freezes on cold surfaces below freezing
water freezes at 0 degrees celsius 32 degrees fahrenheit
water boils at 100 degrees celsius 212 degrees fahrenheit at sea level
icicles form when melting snow drips and refreezes
ice on lakes forms at the top because ice is less dense than water and floats
fish survive winter under the ice because liquid water stays below the floating ice
weathering by frost wedging happens when water freezes in cracks and expands
in a cold climate water freezing and thawing breaks rocks
humid weather feels hotter because sweat evaporates slowly
wind chill makes it feel colder than the actual temperature
the sun heats land air and water unequally
dark surfaces absorb more sunlight and get hotter than light colored surfaces
snow and ice reflect sunlight
weather balloons measure conditions in the upper atmosphere
storms can be predicted by watching clouds pressure and wind changes
a monsoon is a seasonal wind that brings heavy rain
acid rain forms when pollution such as sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides mixes with water in the air
acid rain damages forests lakes statues and buildings
smog is air pollution from car exhaust and factories
air quality is harmed by pollution from burning fuels
the water cycle has no beginning or end it is a cycle
water in the water cycle is recycled over and over
the amount of water on earth stays about the same
a watershed is the land area that drains into a river or lake
rivers flow downhill toward the ocean
water flows from higher elevation to lower elevation
a reservoir stores water behind a dam
freshwater sources include rivers lakes and groundwater wells
people can conserve water by taking shorter showers and fixing leaks
desalination removes salt from ocean water to make fresh water
