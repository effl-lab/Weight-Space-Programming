# more earth science: landforms, maps, earth history, resources, oceans, weather examples
a map scale shows the relationship between distance on the map and real distance
a map legend key explains the symbols used on a map
a compass rose shows directions north south east west on a map
latitude lines run east west and measure distance from the equator
longitude lines run north south and measure distance from the prime meridian
the equator divides earth into the northern and southern hemispheres
a globe is the most accurate model of earth's surface
satellite images show large areas of earth's surface such as storms and forests
gps uses satellites to find location
earth is shaped like a sphere slightly flattened at the poles
photos from space show earth is round
ships disappearing over the horizon show earth is curved
most of earth's surface is covered by water oceans
the pacific ocean is the largest ocean
continents are large landmasses such as north america asia africa europe antarctica australia and south america
antarctica is the coldest continent covered by an ice sheet
the sahara is a large hot desert in africa
the amazon rainforest is in south america
mount everest is the highest mountain on earth
rivers begin at a source in mountains and end at a mouth at the ocean or lake
a river meanders curves across flat land
a floodplain is flat land next to a river that floods
oxbow lakes form from river meanders
waterfalls form where a river flows over hard rock above soft rock that erodes
caves can form from waves eroding sea cliffs
sand dunes are hills of sand formed by wind deposition
a beach is made of sand deposited by waves
coastlines change over time because waves erode and deposit sand
barrier islands protect the coast from storm waves
sea stacks and arches are formed by wave erosion
glaciers form where more snow falls than melts and snow compacts into ice
glaciers are rivers of ice that move slowly downhill
glaciers leave behind rocks of many sizes called till and erratics
glacial grooves and scratches on rocks show that glaciers moved over them
the great lakes were formed by glaciers
during the last ice age glaciers covered much of north america
landslides happen on steep slopes after heavy rain
mass movement is downhill movement of soil and rock caused by gravity
creep is very slow downhill movement of soil that tilts fences and poles
mudflows happen when soil mixed with water flows downhill
volcanoes can build new land such as islands
lava flows create new rock and land
volcanic eruptions can cause mudflows ash falls and climate cooling
scientists monitor volcanoes for earthquakes gas release and ground swelling to predict eruptions
earthquakes cannot be predicted exactly but scientists study faults and seismic activity
buildings in earthquake areas are built with flexible frames to reduce damage
the crust under the oceans is basalt and continental crust is mostly granite
the deepest mines and wells only reach the crust
scientists learn about earth's interior by studying seismic waves
earth's interior is hotter and has greater pressure with depth
the mantle has convection currents of hot rock that rise and cool rock that sinks
heat from earth's interior comes from radioactive decay and leftover heat from formation
plates move apart at the mid atlantic ridge making the atlantic ocean wider
iceland sits on the mid atlantic ridge and has many volcanoes
the andes mountains formed where the ocean plate subducts under south america
japan has many earthquakes and volcanoes because it is near plate boundaries
mountains can form by folding faulting or volcanic activity
folded mountains form when rock layers are squeezed together
fault block mountains form when blocks of crust move up or down along faults
a valley is low land between hills or mountains often with a river
a canyon forms when a river cuts down through rock over a long time
the colorado plateau was uplifted and the colorado river carved the grand canyon
rock layers can be tilted or folded by forces in earth's crust
an unconformity is a gap in the rock record caused by erosion
intrusions of igneous rock are younger than the rock layers they cut through
a fault is younger than the rock layers it cuts across
radioactive dating measures the age of rocks using isotopes
carbon 14 dating is used to find the age of once living things
earth's history is divided into the precambrian paleozoic mesozoic and cenozoic eras
the precambrian is the longest part of earth's history
humans appeared recently in earth's history
trilobites are extinct sea animals found as fossils in old rock layers
ammonites are extinct shelled sea animals used as index fossils
mammoths lived during the ice age and have been found frozen in ice
fossil fuels coal oil and natural gas take millions of years to form
coal forms from plants buried in swamps and changed by heat and pressure
oil forms from tiny sea organisms buried in sediment
oil and gas are pumped from wells drilled into rock
earth's resources are limited so people should conserve them
minerals are used to make many products such as metals glass and electronics
quartz sand is used to make glass
gypsum is used to make drywall plaster
iron ore is used to make steel
bauxite is the ore of aluminum
salt is mined from underground deposits and from evaporating sea water
soil is a natural resource needed to grow food
soil takes hundreds of years to form
soil horizons layers are topsoil subsoil and bedrock
bedrock is solid rock beneath the soil
parent material is the rock that breaks down to form soil
weathering happens faster in warm wet climates
chemical weathering is faster in hot humid climates
mechanical weathering is common in cold climates with freezing and thawing
rocks exposed to wind and water are weathered over time
gravestones and statues wear away over time from weathering
rounded pebbles in a stream show the rocks were tumbled by moving water
sediment is sorted by size as water slows down
the largest sediments settle first and the smallest settle last
the ocean floor contains sediment carried by rivers
salt in the ocean comes from minerals dissolved from rocks carried by rivers
ocean water temperature decreases with depth
the deep ocean is cold dark and has high pressure
the continental shelf is the shallow part of the ocean near land
most ocean life lives near the surface where there is sunlight
coral reefs grow in warm shallow clear water
upwelling brings cold nutrient rich water to the ocean surface
high tide and low tide happen about every 12 hours
tides are highest during spring tides when the sun moon and earth are aligned
the moon's gravity pulls ocean water creating a bulge
waves get taller as they approach shallow water and then break
a tsunami can travel across an ocean very fast
hurricanes form over warm tropical oceans in summer and fall
hurricanes rotate counterclockwise in the northern hemisphere
the coriolis effect from earth's rotation curves winds and ocean currents
prevailing westerlies blow from west to east across the united states
weather in the united states generally moves from west to east
the jet stream is a fast wind high in the atmosphere that steers weather
trade winds blow toward the equator
air near the equator is heated rises and creates low pressure
a weather map shows fronts highs lows and temperatures
a cold front is shown as a line with triangles on a weather map
a warm front is shown with half circles on a weather map
forecasters use computer models to predict weather
weather can change quickly when a front passes
before a thunderstorm the air is often warm and humid
a rainbow forms when the sun is behind you and rain is in front of you
clouds are made of tiny water droplets or ice crystals
clouds look white because water droplets scatter sunlight
dark clouds are thick and full of water
fog forms when air near the ground cools to its dew point
frost forms on clear cold nights
morning dew evaporates as the sun warms the ground
puddles evaporate faster on hot sunny windy days
a wet towel dries faster in the sun and wind
clothes on a line dry because water evaporates
humidity makes sweat evaporate more slowly
the temperature is usually highest in the afternoon and lowest just before sunrise
deserts are hot during the day and cold at night because dry air and few clouds let heat escape
the air temperature drops after sunset
cities are warmer than the countryside because pavement and buildings absorb heat
places at higher elevation are cooler
places near large lakes get lake effect snow
ice storms coat trees and power lines with ice
heat waves are long periods of very hot weather
floods are the most common natural disaster
droughts can cause wildfires
wildfires are more likely during hot dry windy weather
lightning can start forest fires
forest fires return nutrients to soil and allow new plants to grow
scientists use tree rings and ice cores to learn about past climates
ice cores contain bubbles of ancient air that show past climate
fossil pollen shows what plants grew in an area in the past
evidence of past climates includes fossils of plants that grew in warmer or cooler climates
the climate of an area depends on latitude elevation nearness to water and ocean currents
deserts can be cold such as the gobi and antarctica
the tundra has permafrost and short summers
earth's climate has changed many times in the past
volcanic eruptions can lower global temperatures for a few years
water vapor is the most abundant greenhouse gas
methane is a greenhouse gas released by cows landfills and wetlands
carbon dioxide levels in the atmosphere have increased since the industrial revolution
the water cycle moves water from the ocean to the land and back
most evaporation happens from the oceans
water that falls on land returns to the ocean through rivers and groundwater
a water molecule can be stored in a glacier for thousands of years
wells pump groundwater from aquifers
pollution can seep into groundwater
the water table is the top of the groundwater
springs form where groundwater flows out at the surface
