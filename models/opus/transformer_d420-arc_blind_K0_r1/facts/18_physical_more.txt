# more physical science: matter, energy, forces, waves examples
matter is made of tiny particles called atoms and molecules
the particles of matter are always in motion
the particles of a solid are held tightly in place
when a solid melts its particles gain energy and move more freely
when a gas cools its particles lose energy slow down and condense
heating a gas in a closed container increases its pressure
a balloon gets bigger in a warm room and smaller in the cold
air in a bicycle tire expands on a hot day
a basketball bounces less when it is cold because the air inside contracts
a thermometer liquid rises when temperature increases because it expands
metal lids loosen in hot water because metal expands when heated
power lines sag in summer because they expand in heat
the temperature at which a substance melts is the same as the temperature at which it freezes
pure substances have fixed melting and boiling points
the boiling point of water is 100 degrees celsius
the freezing point of water is 0 degrees celsius
room temperature is about 20 to 25 degrees celsius
butter chocolate and wax melt easily when warmed
ice cream melts in the sun
a puddle freezes when the temperature drops below freezing
steam condenses on a cold mirror in a bathroom
the mirror fogs up because water vapor condenses on the cool surface
a glass of ice water gets wet on the outside because water vapor in the air condenses
water left in an open dish evaporates over time
salt remains after salt water evaporates
evaporation leaves dissolved solids behind
a solution can be separated by evaporating the solvent
filtration separates solids that do not dissolve from liquids
a sieve separates large particles from small particles
a magnet separates iron from a mixture
picking out pieces by hand separates a mixture of different sized objects
oil and vinegar separate into layers because they do not mix
oil floats on top of water
substances with different densities form layers
a cork floats on water and a rock sinks
wood floats because it is less dense than water
a steel ship floats because its shape displaces a lot of water
an egg floats in salt water but sinks in fresh water
a hot air balloon rises because hot air is less dense than the cooler air around it
helium balloons rise because helium is less dense than air
the density of a substance stays the same no matter how much of it there is
cutting a piece of wood in half does not change its density
mass can be measured in grams using a balance
the mass of a closed system stays the same during a change
when sugar dissolves in water the mass of the solution equals the mass of the sugar plus the water
when water freezes its mass stays the same but its volume increases
burning wood seems to lose mass because gases escape into the air
rust weighs more than the iron because oxygen has combined with it
when baking soda and vinegar react in an open cup mass seems to decrease because carbon dioxide gas escapes
a chemical reaction can produce a gas a solid precipitate a color change or a temperature change
bubbles forming when two substances mix is evidence of a chemical reaction
a color change when substances mix can show a chemical reaction
a burning match produces heat light ash and smoke
a glow stick gives off light from a chemical reaction
fireworks produce light heat and sound from chemical reactions
a rotting banana turns brown because of chemical changes
leaves changing color in fall is a chemical change
photosynthesis and respiration are chemical changes
digestion breaks down food by chemical changes
rusting is a slow chemical reaction between iron oxygen and water
a copper statue turns green because copper reacts with air
tarnished silver turns black
mixing sand and water is a physical change
grinding pepper is a physical change
chopping wood is a physical change
crumpling paper is a physical change
breaking glass is a physical change
melting butter is a physical change
water boiling is a physical change
making a mixture of salt and pepper is a physical change
stretching a rubber band is a physical change
shaping clay is a physical change
the properties of a substance include color texture hardness odor and state
texture describes how something feels rough or smooth
a material's hardness is how easily it is scratched
flexibility is the ability to bend without breaking
strength is the ability to hold weight without breaking
brittle materials break easily such as glass and chalk
elastic materials such as rubber stretch and return to their shape
absorbent materials such as paper towels and sponges soak up water
waterproof materials such as plastic and rubber keep water out
cotton is soft and absorbs water
a raincoat is made of waterproof material
metal is used for pots because it conducts heat
wooden spoons are used for stirring hot soup because wood is an insulator
oven mitts are made of insulating material to protect hands
a cooler keeps ice from melting because it is insulated
foam cups keep drinks warm because foam is an insulator
a blanket keeps you warm by trapping body heat
double pane windows reduce heat loss
energy can be transferred by heat light sound electricity and motion
a moving object can transfer energy to another object when they collide
a bowling ball transfers energy to the pins
a hammer transfers energy to a nail
a stretched bow has potential energy that becomes kinetic energy in the arrow
water stored behind a dam has potential energy
a falling object loses potential energy and gains kinetic energy
a ball rolling down a hill speeds up as potential energy changes into kinetic energy
a ball thrown upward slows as kinetic energy changes into potential energy
the faster an object moves the more kinetic energy it has
a heavier object moving at the same speed has more kinetic energy
energy from food is stored in chemical bonds
the body changes chemical energy from food into motion and heat
muscles change chemical energy into mechanical energy
a car's brakes change kinetic energy into heat
rubbing two sticks together makes heat by friction
sandpaper produces heat from friction
striking a match uses friction to produce heat
a candle changes chemical energy into light and heat
a campfire releases heat and light
a battery powered toy car changes chemical energy into electrical energy and then motion
a doorbell changes electrical energy into sound
an electric fan changes electrical energy into motion and some heat
a television changes electrical energy into light and sound
a radio changes electrical energy into sound
a light bulb gets hot because some electrical energy changes into heat
fluorescent and led bulbs are more efficient than incandescent bulbs
an incandescent bulb wastes energy as heat
solar powered calculators change light into electrical energy
a windmill changes wind energy into mechanical energy to pump water or grind grain
a water wheel uses the energy of moving water
the sun's energy warms the earth and makes plants grow
sunlight can be focused with a magnifying glass to make heat
a solar oven traps sunlight to cook food
solar water heaters use sunlight to heat water
a black pot heats faster in sunlight than a white pot
a metal spoon feels colder than a wooden spoon because metal conducts heat away from your hand faster
heat flows from your warm hand to a cold ice cube so the ice melts
a hot drink cools down as heat moves to the cooler air
an ice cube in a warm drink melts as heat moves from the drink to the ice
two objects touching will reach the same temperature thermal equilibrium
electric circuits need a complete path from the battery through wires and back
a bulb lights when the circuit is closed
a bulb will not light if there is a break gap in the circuit
a switch turns a light on by closing the circuit
adding more batteries in series makes a bulb brighter
batteries have a positive and a negative terminal
electric current flows from the negative terminal through the circuit to the positive terminal
a short circuit lets too much current flow and can cause a fire
electricity can be dangerous and cause shocks and fires
never put metal objects in electrical outlets
a lightning rod carries lightning safely into the ground
rubbing a balloon on wool gives it a static charge so it sticks to a wall
static electricity makes hair stand up after brushing
a shock from a doorknob is caused by static electricity
clothes stick together in a dryer because of static electricity
magnets can pick up paper clips nails and pins
a magnet can attract objects through paper glass water and cloth
magnetic force is strongest at the poles of a magnet
the force between magnets is stronger when they are closer
two magnets can be made to float by repelling
a refrigerator magnet sticks to the steel door
magnets are used in motors generators speakers compasses and maglev trains
a compass needle lines up with earth's magnetic field
a compass can be affected by a nearby magnet
earth's magnetic field comes from its iron core
an electromagnet is used in junkyards to lift cars
a stronger electromagnet has more coils more current or an iron core
electric motors change electrical energy into motion using magnets
generators use magnets and coils of wire to make electricity
light travels through space from the sun and stars
light reflects off the moon to earth
shiny objects reflect more light than dull objects
light can pass through water glass and air
lenses in glasses bend light to focus it in the eye
a magnifying lens makes small things look bigger
a mirror reverses images left to right
the colors we see are the colors of light reflected by objects
grass looks green because it reflects green light and absorbs the other colors
a red apple reflects red light
a shadow is longer when the light source is lower
moving an object closer to a light makes its shadow larger

musical instruments make sound by vibrating strings air columns or membranes
a guitar makes sound when its strings vibrate
a drum makes sound when its head vibrates

dolphins and submarines use sonar

pushing a swing makes it move
gravity makes a ball fall back to the ground

shoes with treads grip better because of friction

a heavier cart needs more force to accelerate
an empty cart is easier to push than a full cart
a moving bicycle stops when the brakes create friction
when a car turns a corner passengers feel pushed outward because of inertia
a ball on a string moves in a circle because the string pulls it
magnets push and pull without touching
the strength of a force is measured in newtons
speed tells how fast an object moves
a car traveling 60 kilometers per hour goes 60 kilometers in one hour
the speed of a runner equals distance divided by time

an object that travels farther in the same time is faster
an object that is speeding up is accelerating
a ramp with a steeper slope makes a ball roll faster
the height of a ramp affects how far a toy car travels

a pulley helps raise a flag or lift heavy objects
