# inquiry, measurement and engineering phrasing common in questions
to make results more reliable repeat the experiment several times
repeating trials and averaging results makes data more reliable
testing many plants instead of one gives more reliable results
results are valid when only one variable is changed
to test a hypothesis design a controlled experiment
a good experiment has a control group for comparison
the control group does not receive the treatment
all conditions except the variable being tested should stay the same
changing more than one variable at a time makes results unclear
the variable being tested is the independent variable
the result that is measured is the dependent variable
the hypothesis is the prediction being tested
the next step after recording data is to analyze the data
after analyzing data scientists draw a conclusion
if data do not support the hypothesis the scientist should revise the hypothesis
scientists communicate results so others can repeat the investigation
a scientist should report results honestly even when unexpected
scientific claims should be supported by evidence
evidence comes from observations and measurements
data collected from many sources is more trustworthy
a line graph is best for showing change over time
a bar graph is best for comparing different groups
a circle graph shows percentages of a whole
a data table records measurements in rows and columns
a trend is a pattern in data
an increasing line on a graph shows that the value goes up
a decreasing line on a graph shows that the value goes down
the slope of a line shows the rate of change
the average of 2 4 and 6 is 4
precise measurements need the right tool and units
use metric units in science
length is measured in meters with a meter stick or ruler
mass is measured in grams with a balance
volume of liquids is measured in milliliters with a graduated cylinder
temperature is measured in degrees celsius with a thermometer
time is measured in seconds with a stopwatch
force is measured in newtons with a spring scale
a hand lens magnifies insects leaves and rocks
a microscope magnifies cells and microorganisms
a telescope magnifies distant planets and stars
binoculars help observe birds far away
a camera records images for later study
a computer can model weather and climate
a model of the solar system shows the positions of the sun and planets
a model of an atom shows protons neutrons and electrons
a model of a cell shows its parts
a map is a model of an area of land
a diagram is a drawing that shows the parts of something
models help explain things that are too big too small too fast or too slow to observe directly
models are simpler than the real objects
an engineer designs builds and tests solutions to problems
the first step in the design process is to identify the problem
engineers brainstorm ideas and choose the best solution
engineers build a prototype model and test it
after testing engineers improve redesign the solution
engineers consider cost safety materials and the environment
a design must meet criteria and constraints
cost time and materials are constraints
technology includes tools machines and processes that solve problems
new tools let scientists observe things that could not be seen before
the invention of the microscope led to the discovery of cells
the invention of the telescope led to discoveries about planets and moons
computers help scientists analyze large amounts of data
satellites help scientists study weather oceans and land
scientists work in teams and share ideas
scientists from different countries share data
scientific knowledge can change with new evidence
a scientific theory explains many observations and is supported by evidence
a scientific law describes a pattern in nature
science cannot answer questions about opinions or beliefs
questions that can be tested are scientific questions
a good scientific question can be answered by an investigation
an inference is an explanation based on observation
a prediction is a guess about what will happen based on evidence
an observation uses the five senses
measuring is a kind of observation
classifying means grouping things by their properties
comparing means finding how things are alike and different
safety rules protect students during investigations
goggles protect the eyes
gloves protect the hands from chemicals and germs
aprons protect clothing
long hair should be tied back
loose clothing should be secured near flames
never eat or drink during an experiment
never taste or touch unknown substances
use caution with sharp objects such as scissors and knives
report all accidents injuries and spills to the teacher right away
clean up spills right away
broken glass should be swept up not picked up with hands
know where the fire extinguisher eye wash and first aid kit are
wash your hands after every investigation
handle animals gently and wash hands after touching them
do not mix chemicals unless the teacher says to
heat substances in heat safe containers such as pyrex
never leave a flame unattended
an investigation of bacteria requires gloves and careful handwashing
dispose of materials as the teacher directs
students should read all directions before starting an investigation
students should make a plan before doing an investigation
a field trip lets students observe nature directly
recording observations in a notebook helps remember details
drawings and photographs record observations
scientists label drawings and include units in measurements
a sample is a small part used to represent a whole
larger samples give more accurate results
random sampling reduces bias
the mean median and mode describe data
an error in measurement can come from using the wrong tool
using a balance that is not zeroed gives wrong mass readings
reading a graduated cylinder at eye level gives an accurate measurement
read the bottom of the meniscus in a graduated cylinder
