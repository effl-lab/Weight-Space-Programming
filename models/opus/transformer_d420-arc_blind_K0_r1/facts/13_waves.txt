# Waves, sound, light
a wave is a disturbance that transfers energy from place to place
waves carry energy
mechanical waves need a medium such as air water or solid to travel through
sound waves are mechanical waves that need a medium such as air water or a solid
electromagnetic waves such as light can travel through a vacuum space
in a transverse wave particles of the medium move perpendicular at right angles to the direction the wave travels
light and waves on a rope or string are transverse waves
in a longitudinal compression wave particles move parallel back and forth in the same direction the wave travels
sound waves are longitudinal compression waves
compressions and rarefactions are parts of a longitudinal wave
the crest is the highest point of a wave and the trough is the lowest point
wavelength is the distance from one crest to the next crest
amplitude is the height of a wave and measures its energy
higher amplitude waves carry more energy
frequency is the number of waves passing a point per second measured in hertz
higher frequency waves have shorter wavelengths
wave speed equals frequency times wavelength
sound is produced by vibrations
vibrating objects produce sound such as a guitar string drum or vocal cords
sound travels fastest through solids then liquids and slowest through gases
sound travels faster in water than in air
sound travels at about 340 meters per second in air
light travels much faster than sound
pitch is how high or low a sound is and depends on frequency
high pitch sounds have high frequency and short wavelength
low pitch sounds have low frequency and long wavelength
shorter strings vibrate faster and make higher pitch sounds
tightening a guitar string raises the pitch
a thicker or longer string makes a lower pitch
small bells and short tubes produce higher pitches
loudness volume depends on the amplitude of a sound wave
loudness is measured in decibels
louder sounds have larger amplitude and more energy
an echo is a reflected sound wave bouncing off a surface
sonar uses echoes of sound waves to find objects and measure ocean depth
ultrasound is sound with frequency too high for humans to hear used in medical imaging
dogs and bats can hear high frequency ultrasonic sounds
soft materials such as carpet curtains and foam absorb sound
the doppler effect is the change in pitch as a sound source moves toward or away from you
a siren sounds higher pitch as it approaches and lower as it moves away
light is a form of energy that travels in waves electromagnetic radiation
light travels in straight lines
light travels at about 300000 kilometers per second the fastest speed
light from the sun reaches earth in about 8 minutes
reflection is when light bounces off a surface
mirrors reflect light
a smooth shiny surface such as a mirror reflects light clearly
the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection
we see objects because light reflects off them into our eyes
refraction is the bending of light as it passes from one medium to another
a straw looks bent in a glass of water because of refraction
lenses refract bend light to form images
a convex lens is thicker in the middle and focuses light converging
a concave lens is thinner in the middle and spreads light diverging
magnifying glasses use convex lenses to make objects look larger
eyeglasses use lenses to help people see clearly
a prism separates white light into the colors of the rainbow spectrum
white light is made of all colors of the visible spectrum
the colors of the rainbow are red orange yellow green blue indigo violet
rainbows form when sunlight is refracted and reflected by raindrops
red light has the longest wavelength and violet has the shortest wavelength of visible light
an object looks red because it reflects red light and absorbs other colors
a black object absorbs all colors of light
a white object reflects all colors of light
a green leaf reflects green light
the sky looks blue because air scatters blue light
transparent materials such as clear glass and water let light pass through
translucent materials such as wax paper and frosted glass let some light through
opaque materials such as wood metal and cardboard block light
shadows form when an opaque object blocks light
absorption is when an object takes in light energy
the electromagnetic spectrum includes radio waves microwaves infrared visible light ultraviolet x rays and gamma rays
radio waves have the longest wavelength and lowest frequency
gamma rays have the shortest wavelength and highest frequency and the most energy
infrared radiation is felt as heat
ultraviolet light causes sunburn
x rays pass through skin and show bones
microwaves are used for cooking and communication
radio waves carry radio and television signals
cell phones use radio waves microwaves to communicate
lasers produce a narrow beam of light of a single color
fiber optic cables carry light signals through glass fibers by total internal reflection
the eye's retina detects light and sends signals to the brain through the optic nerve
the pupil controls how much light enters the eye
the iris is the colored part of the eye
a periscope uses mirrors to see over obstacles
a telescope uses lenses or mirrors to see distant objects
a microscope uses lenses to magnify tiny objects
a camera uses a lens to focus light
light energy can be changed into electricity by solar cells
waves can be reflected refracted absorbed or transmitted
water waves are caused by wind
earthquake seismic waves travel through the earth
ocean waves transfer energy but the water moves up and down in place
a floating object bobs up and down as a wave passes
the energy of a wave decreases as it spreads out
sound needs particles of matter to vibrate
astronauts use radios to talk to each other in space
our ears hear sound when vibrations travel through the air
the vocal cords vibrate to make the human voice
plucking a rubber band makes it vibrate and make sound
hitting a drum harder makes a louder sound
blowing across a bottle makes sound by vibrating air
a tuning fork vibrates at one frequency
high energy waves can be harmful
the color of light depends on its wavelength or frequency
