# Rocks, minerals, soil, weathering, erosion, plate tectonics, fossils, earth history
earth has layers crust mantle outer core inner core
the crust is the thin outermost solid layer of earth
the mantle is the thickest layer of earth made of hot slowly flowing rock
the outer core is liquid iron and nickel
the inner core is solid iron and nickel and is the hottest layer
earth's core is made mostly of iron and nickel
the lithosphere is the rigid crust and upper mantle broken into tectonic plates
the asthenosphere is the soft flowing layer of the mantle the plates move on
convection currents in the mantle move tectonic plates
plate tectonics explains that earth's crust is broken into plates that move slowly
tectonic plates move a few centimeters each year
continental drift is the idea that continents move slowly over time
pangaea was a supercontinent when all continents were joined together
evidence for continental drift includes matching fossils rocks and coastlines on different continents
the coastlines of south america and africa fit together like puzzle pieces
mesosaurus fossils found in africa and south america support continental drift
earthquakes happen when rocks along a fault suddenly move and release energy
earthquakes happen at plate boundaries
a fault is a crack in earth's crust where rocks move
the focus is where an earthquake starts underground and the epicenter is the point on the surface above it
seismic waves travel through earth from an earthquake
a seismograph seismometer records earthquake waves vibrations
the richter scale measures the magnitude strength of earthquakes
p waves primary waves travel fastest and through solids and liquids
s waves secondary waves travel only through solids
earthquakes under the ocean can cause tsunamis huge ocean waves
a tsunami is a giant wave caused by an underwater earthquake or landslide
volcanoes form where magma reaches the surface often at plate boundaries
a volcano erupts lava ash and gases
magma is melted rock underground and lava is melted rock on the surface
the ring of fire around the pacific ocean has many volcanoes and earthquakes
hot spots form volcanoes in the middle of plates such as hawaii
the hawaiian islands formed from volcanoes over a hot spot
volcanic ash can block sunlight and affect climate
convergent boundaries are where plates move toward each other collide
divergent boundaries are where plates move apart spreading
transform boundaries are where plates slide past each other sideways
the san andreas fault is a transform boundary in california with many earthquakes
mountains form when two continental plates collide and push up the crust
the himalaya mountains formed when india collided with asia
subduction happens when a denser oceanic plate sinks under another plate forming trenches and volcanoes
ocean trenches form at subduction zones
mid ocean ridges form where plates move apart and new sea floor crust forms
sea floor spreading creates new oceanic crust at mid ocean ridges
rift valleys form where plates pull apart
oceanic crust is denser and thinner than continental crust
the three types of rocks are igneous sedimentary and metamorphic
igneous rock forms when magma or lava cools and hardens solidifies
granite is an igneous rock with large crystals that formed from slowly cooling magma underground
basalt is a dark igneous rock formed from cooled lava
obsidian is volcanic glass that forms when lava cools very quickly
pumice is a light igneous rock full of holes from gas bubbles and can float
lava that cools quickly forms small crystals; magma that cools slowly forms large crystals
sedimentary rock forms when layers of sediment are compacted and cemented together
sediments are pieces of rock sand mud and shells deposited by water wind or ice
sandstone is a sedimentary rock made of sand grains
limestone is a sedimentary rock made from shells of sea organisms calcium carbonate
shale is a sedimentary rock made of compacted mud and clay
conglomerate is a sedimentary rock with rounded pebbles cemented together
coal is a sedimentary rock formed from plant remains compressed over millions of years
fossils are usually found in sedimentary rock
sedimentary rock forms in layers called strata
metamorphic rock forms when existing rock is changed by heat and pressure
marble is a metamorphic rock formed from limestone
slate is a metamorphic rock formed from shale
quartzite is a metamorphic rock formed from sandstone
gneiss is a metamorphic rock with bands formed from granite
the rock cycle describes how rocks change from one type to another over time
in the rock cycle weathering and erosion break rocks into sediment
melting rock forms magma and cooling magma forms igneous rock
a mineral is a naturally occurring inorganic solid with a crystal structure
rocks are made of one or more minerals
minerals are identified by properties such as hardness color streak luster and cleavage
hardness of a mineral is its resistance to being scratched
the mohs hardness scale ranks minerals from 1 talc softest to 10 diamond hardest
diamond is the hardest mineral
talc is the softest mineral
a harder mineral can scratch a softer mineral
a scratch test is used to measure the hardness of a mineral
streak is the color of a mineral's powder when rubbed on a streak plate
luster describes how a mineral reflects light shiny metallic glassy dull
cleavage is when a mineral splits along flat surfaces and fracture is irregular breaking
quartz is a common hard mineral made of silicon and oxygen
calcite fizzes bubbles when acid vinegar is placed on it
magnetite is a magnetic mineral
pyrite is fool's gold
halite is rock salt
gemstones are rare valuable minerals such as diamond ruby and emerald
ores are rocks containing valuable metals that can be mined such as iron ore
weathering is the breaking down of rock into smaller pieces
mechanical physical weathering breaks rocks without changing their composition
chemical weathering changes the minerals in rock such as rusting or acid dissolving limestone
ice wedging frost wedging happens when water freezes in cracks expands and breaks rock
plant roots growing in cracks can break rocks apart weathering
acid rain causes chemical weathering of limestone and marble statues
oxidation rust forms when iron in rocks reacts with oxygen and water
lichens and mosses can slowly break down rocks
wind blowing sand against rocks wears them down abrasion
erosion is the movement of weathered rock and soil by water wind ice or gravity
running water rivers are the main cause of erosion
rivers carve canyons and valleys over time
the grand canyon was carved by the colorado river over millions of years
glaciers carve u shaped valleys and carry boulders and rocks as they move
glaciers leave behind moraines piles of rock and sediment
wind erosion forms sand dunes in deserts
waves cause erosion of beaches and coastlines cliffs
gravity causes landslides mudslides and rockfalls downhill
deposition is when sediment carried by water wind or ice is dropped in a new place
deltas form at the mouth of a river where sediment is deposited
sand bars and beaches form by deposition of sand by waves
fast moving water carries larger sediment and slow water deposits it
plants and their roots hold soil in place and reduce erosion
planting grass trees and cover crops prevents soil erosion
terracing and contour plowing reduce soil erosion on farms
building a wall or jetty can reduce beach erosion
erosion is faster on steep slopes without plants
soil is a mixture of weathered rock minerals humus organic matter water and air
humus is decayed plant and animal material in soil that makes it fertile
topsoil is the top layer of soil with the most humus and nutrients
sand has the largest particles silt medium and clay the smallest particles
clay soil holds water well and sandy soil drains water quickly
loam is a fertile soil mixture of sand silt clay and humus good for growing plants
earthworms help soil by mixing it and making tunnels for air and water
decomposers break down dead matter adding nutrients to the soil
soil forms slowly from weathering of rock over many years
fossils are preserved remains or traces of organisms that lived long ago
most fossils form when organisms are buried in sediment that hardens into rock
fossils form in sedimentary rock
types of fossils include molds casts imprints petrified wood amber and trace fossils
a trace fossil such as a footprint or burrow shows the activity of an organism
insects can be preserved in amber hardened tree sap
petrified wood forms when minerals replace wood
hard parts like bones shells and teeth are more likely to become fossils
fossils of sea organisms found on mountains show the area was once covered by ocean
fossils of tropical plants found in cold places show the climate was once warm
fossils give evidence of how life and environments changed over time
the fossil record shows that many species have become extinct
in undisturbed rock layers the oldest layers are at the bottom law of superposition
younger rock layers lie on top of older rock layers
relative dating compares the ages of rock layers by their position
absolute dating uses radioactive decay half life to find the age of rocks
index fossils are fossils of organisms that lived for a short time over a wide area and help date rock layers
geologic time scale divides earth's history into eras periods
dinosaurs lived during the mesozoic era and became extinct about 65 million years ago
an asteroid impact may have caused the extinction of the dinosaurs
the first life on earth was single celled organisms bacteria
landforms include mountains valleys plains plateaus canyons hills and islands
a plateau is a high flat area of land
a delta is a landform made of sediment at the mouth of a river
a canyon is a deep narrow valley with steep sides carved by a river
a peninsula is land surrounded by water on three sides
an island is land surrounded by water
sinkholes and caves form when groundwater dissolves limestone
stalactites and stalagmites form in caves from dripping water depositing minerals
geysers and hot springs are heated by magma underground
a topographic map shows elevation with contour lines
contour lines close together show a steep slope
geologists study rocks minerals and earth's history
paleontologists study fossils
mountains wear down over time by weathering and erosion
old mountains like the appalachians are rounded by erosion
young mountains are tall and sharp
natural resources from earth include minerals metals rocks soil and fossil fuels
fossil fuels coal oil and natural gas formed from remains of ancient plants and animals over millions of years
oil petroleum and natural gas are found trapped in rock underground
mining removes minerals and ores from the earth
gravel sand and crushed rock are used to make concrete and roads
the crust contains mostly oxygen and silicon
volcanic soil is very fertile
caves form by chemical weathering of limestone by acidic groundwater
rocks are classified by how they formed
crystal size in igneous rock depends on how fast it cooled
heat and pressure deep underground change rocks into metamorphic rocks
rocks at the bottom of the grand canyon are older than rocks at the top
