# Scientific method, investigations, tools, measurement, safety
the scientific method includes asking a question making a hypothesis testing with an experiment analyzing data and drawing conclusions
a hypothesis is a testable prediction or possible explanation
a hypothesis is often written as an if then statement
a prediction tells what is expected to happen
an observation is information gathered using the senses
an inference is a conclusion based on observations and prior knowledge
a conclusion is based on the data and results of an experiment
a theory is a well tested explanation supported by much evidence
a scientific law describes what happens in nature such as gravity
an experiment tests a hypothesis
the independent variable is the one factor the scientist changes on purpose manipulated variable
the dependent variable is what is measured or observed responding variable
controlled variables constants are kept the same in an experiment
only one variable should be changed at a time in a fair test
a fair test changes only one variable and keeps all others the same
a control group is not changed and is used for comparison
the experimental group receives the variable being tested
repeating trials makes results more reliable and accurate
repeating an experiment many times increases confidence in the results
a large sample size makes results more reliable
other scientists should be able to repeat replicate an experiment and get the same results
scientists share results so others can check and repeat them
scientists publish their results in journals and peer review
data is recorded in tables charts and graphs
a data table organizes data
a bar graph compares amounts in different categories
a line graph shows how data changes over time continuous change
a pie circle graph shows parts of a whole percentages
the x axis shows the independent variable and the y axis the dependent variable
qualitative data describes qualities such as color and quantitative data uses numbers measurements
scientists use models to study things that are too large too small or too complex
a model can be a drawing diagram map globe or computer simulation
a globe is a model of earth
models have limitations and are not exactly like the real thing
a thermometer measures temperature
a balance or scale measures mass in grams
a triple beam balance measures mass
a spring scale measures force and weight in newtons
a graduated cylinder measures the volume of a liquid in milliliters
a beaker holds and pours liquids
a ruler or meter stick measures length in centimeters and meters
a stopwatch measures time in seconds
a hand lens magnifying glass makes small objects look larger
a microscope magnifies very small objects such as cells
a telescope makes distant objects appear closer
a barometer measures air pressure
an anemometer measures wind speed
a rain gauge measures rainfall
binoculars help observe birds and distant animals
a compass shows direction north south east west
a ph meter or litmus paper measures acidity
a seismograph measures earthquakes
a voltmeter measures voltage and an ammeter measures current
a calculator and computer help analyze data
metric units the meter measures length the liter measures volume the gram measures mass
the kilogram is a unit of mass
a kilometer is 1000 meters
a centimeter is one hundredth of a meter
a millimeter is one thousandth of a meter
a milliliter is one thousandth of a liter
celsius is the metric temperature scale
the newton is the unit of force
the joule is the unit of energy
the watt is the unit of power
the second is the unit of time
the international system si metric system is used by scientists around the world
using the same units allows scientists to compare results
precise measurements are close to each other and accurate measurements are close to the true value
measurement error can be reduced by repeating measurements and using proper tools
wearing safety goggles protects the eyes during experiments
safety goggles protect eyes from chemicals splashes and flying objects
wear gloves when handling chemicals or living specimens
wear an apron or lab coat to protect clothing
never eat or drink in the laboratory
food and drinks should never be brought into a science lab
never taste chemicals in a lab
wash hands after handling chemicals animals or plants and before leaving the lab
follow the teacher's directions and safety rules
report spills broken glass and accidents to the teacher immediately
tie back long hair near flames
point test tubes away from people when heating
use tongs or heat resistant gloves to handle hot objects
waft smell chemicals by fanning the air toward your nose
dispose of chemicals properly as directed by the teacher
a fire extinguisher and eye wash station are safety equipment
know where safety equipment is located
handle living organisms carefully and humanely
clean up the work area and materials after an experiment
an investigation should be safe and ethical
observations should be recorded accurately in a science notebook journal
scientists use evidence to support their claims
evidence is data and observations that support or refute a hypothesis
bias can affect results so scientists try to be objective
a hypothesis that is not supported should be revised and tested again
unexpected results should be reported honestly
scientific knowledge changes when new evidence is discovered
technology helps scientists make better observations and measurements
new technology such as better microscopes and telescopes leads to new discoveries
an engineer designs solutions to problems using science
the engineering design process defines a problem designs builds tests and improves a solution
a prototype is a first model of a design that is tested
engineers test designs and make improvements redesign
criteria are the requirements a design must meet and constraints are limits such as cost and materials
a field study observes organisms in their natural environment
a survey collects data by asking questions
classification groups objects by their properties
sorting objects by size shape color or texture is classifying
comparing data helps find patterns and trends
graphs help scientists see patterns trends and relationships
an average mean is the sum of values divided by the number of values
a range is the difference between the highest and lowest values
an outlier is a data point very different from the others
a prediction can be made by extending a graph pattern trend
a scientist records the temperature every hour to find a pattern
measuring plant height every day shows plant growth over time
testing different amounts of fertilizer on plants independent variable fertilizer dependent variable plant height
in an experiment testing light on plant growth the amount of light is the independent variable
a scientist should use the same type of plants soil and water in every group
the best way to find out if a fertilizer helps plants grow is to grow plants with and without the fertilizer
students should record data in a table during an investigation
a hypothesis must be testable
scientific questions can be answered by observations and experiments
opinions and beliefs cannot be tested scientifically
communicate results with graphs reports and presentations
a scientist's notebook should include dates procedures observations and data
procedures are step by step instructions for an experiment
materials are the supplies needed for an experiment
a variable is a factor that can change in an experiment
