# chemistry and electricity specifics
hydrogen is the first element with one proton
helium has two protons and is a noble gas
carbon has six protons and forms the basis of organic molecules
oxygen has eight protons
sodium is a soft reactive metal that reacts with water
chlorine is a toxic yellow green gas
sodium and chlorine combine to form table salt sodium chloride
iron and oxygen combine to form rust iron oxide
hydrogen and oxygen combine to form water
carbon and oxygen combine to form carbon dioxide when fuels burn
the formula h2o means two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom
the formula co2 means one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms
the formula nacl is sodium chloride salt
the formula o2 is oxygen gas with two oxygen atoms
the formula ch4 is methane natural gas
c6h12o6 is glucose sugar
nitrogen n2 is a gas that makes up most of the air
ozone o3 is a form of oxygen in the upper atmosphere
group 1 alkali metals such as sodium and potassium are very reactive
group 17 halogens such as chlorine and fluorine are reactive nonmetals
group 18 noble gases are stable and unreactive
elements in a column have the same number of valence electrons
valence electrons are in the outer shell and take part in bonding
atoms bond to become more stable by filling their outer shells
metals tend to lose electrons and form positive ions
nonmetals tend to gain electrons and form negative ions
ionic compounds such as salt form crystals and have high melting points
covalent compounds such as water and sugar are made of molecules
a chemical bond holds atoms together
breaking chemical bonds requires energy and forming bonds releases energy
mixtures can have any proportion but compounds have fixed proportions
air is a mixture of gases
seawater is a mixture of water and salts
brass is a mixture alloy of copper and zinc
bronze is an alloy of copper and tin
stainless steel resists rust
aluminum resists corrosion and is used for cans
gold stays shiny and is used for jewelry
copper is a good conductor used for wires and pipes
silver is the best conductor of electricity
tungsten is used for light bulb filaments because it has a high melting point
lead is a heavy metal that can be toxic
mercury is a toxic liquid metal once used in thermometers
graphite is used as pencil lead and lubricant
diamond is used for cutting tools because it is hardest
silicon is used to make computer chips
helium is used in balloons and blimps
neon glows in signs
chlorine is used to clean swimming pool water
fluoride in toothpaste helps prevent tooth decay
calcium carbonate is in shells chalk and limestone
baking soda is sodium bicarbonate a base
vinegar contains acetic acid
lemon juice contains citric acid
stomach acid is hydrochloric acid
battery acid is sulfuric acid
ammonia is a base used in cleaners
antacids neutralize stomach acid
acid rain has a low ph
the ph of blood is about 7.4
an indicator such as red cabbage juice changes color with ph
phenolphthalein turns pink in a base
a neutralization reaction of an acid and a base makes water and a salt
a reaction rate increases with temperature concentration and surface area
enzymes are biological catalysts that speed up reactions in living things
food spoils faster when warm because reactions are faster
a candle flame needs oxygen and goes out when covered with a jar
a fire extinguisher removes oxygen or heat from a fire
water puts out fires by cooling them
fuel oxygen and heat make the fire triangle
combustion of methane produces carbon dioxide and water
respiration and burning both use oxygen and release carbon dioxide
photosynthesis is the reverse of respiration
electrolysis uses electricity to split water into hydrogen and oxygen
chemical energy in batteries comes from reactions between chemicals
a battery has an anode and a cathode
a battery dies when its chemicals are used up
a lemon battery uses lemon juice and two metals to make a small current
electricity flows through a complete loop called a circuit
electrons flow through wires in a circuit
current is the rate of flow of charge measured in amperes
voltage is electrical pressure measured in volts
a 9 volt battery has more voltage than a 1.5 volt battery
resistance is how much a material opposes current measured in ohms
thin long wires have more resistance than thick short wires
resistors limit current in a circuit
a light bulb filament has high resistance and gets hot and glows
in a series circuit adding more bulbs makes each bulb dimmer
in a parallel circuit each bulb gets the full voltage and stays bright
if one bulb burns out in a series circuit the circuit is broken and all bulbs go out
holiday lights wired in series all go out when one bulb fails
a fuse melts to break a circuit when current is too high
circuit breakers switch off when there is too much current
ground wires carry extra current safely into the earth
electrical energy is converted into heat light sound and motion by appliances
power is measured in watts and energy used in kilowatt hours
a 100 watt bulb uses more energy than a 60 watt bulb in the same time
electric meters measure how much electrical energy a home uses
conductors have free electrons that move easily
insulators hold electrons tightly
the rubber coating on wires is an insulator that prevents shocks
a person can be shocked if they touch a bare wire
water and wet skin conduct electricity
static charge builds when electrons are rubbed from one object to another
an object with extra electrons has a negative charge
an object that lost electrons has a positive charge
a charged balloon attracts small pieces of paper
lightning is a giant spark between clouds and the ground
a van de graaff generator makes static electricity
electric charges exert forces without touching
magnetism and electricity are both caused by moving charges
an electric current in a wire creates a magnetic field around the wire
a compass needle moves near a wire carrying current
electromagnets are used in doorbells motors speakers and cranes
a motor changes electrical energy into motion with a magnet and coil
a generator spins a coil in a magnetic field to produce current
power plants use turbines to spin generators
wind spins turbines to generate electricity
moving water spins turbines at hydroelectric dams
steam from boiling water spins turbines in coal and nuclear power plants
solar cells produce electricity directly from sunlight without moving parts
transformers change voltage on power lines
alternating current is used in homes and direct current comes from batteries
magnets have magnetic domains lined up
rubbing a nail with a magnet can make the nail a magnet
heating or hammering a magnet can destroy its magnetism
a horseshoe magnet has poles close together
a bar magnet has poles at each end
the north pole of a compass is attracted to earth's magnetic north
magnetic levitation trains float above the track using magnets
magnetic resonance imaging mri uses magnets to see inside the body
credit cards and hard drives store information magnetically
