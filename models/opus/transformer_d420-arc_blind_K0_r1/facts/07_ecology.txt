# Ecosystems, food chains, populations, biomes
an ecosystem is all the living and nonliving things in an area interacting
biotic factors are living parts of an ecosystem such as plants animals and bacteria
abiotic factors are nonliving parts of an ecosystem such as sunlight water temperature soil air and rocks
a population is all the organisms of the same species living in an area
a community is all the populations of different species living in an area
a habitat is the place where an organism lives and gets food water and shelter
a niche is the role of an organism in its ecosystem
a food chain shows how energy passes from one organism to another
a food web shows many connected food chains in an ecosystem
the arrows in a food chain show the direction energy flows from the organism eaten to the eater
energy flows from the sun to producers to consumers
the sun is the original source of energy for almost all food chains
producers make their own food using sunlight such as plants and algae
producers are at the beginning of every food chain
consumers eat other organisms for energy
primary consumers are herbivores that eat producers
secondary consumers eat primary consumers
tertiary consumers top predators eat other consumers
decomposers break down dead plants and animals and return nutrients to the soil
bacteria fungi and mushrooms are decomposers
decomposers recycle nutrients in an ecosystem
decomposers keep dead organisms and wastes from piling up and recycle nutrients
earthworms and some insects help decompose dead matter
an energy pyramid shows that energy decreases at each level of a food chain
only about 10 percent of energy passes to the next level of a food chain
most energy at each level is lost as heat
there are more producers than consumers in an ecosystem
top predators are fewest in number
if a predator population decreases the prey population will increase
if prey decreases the predator population will decrease because of less food
removing one species from a food web affects other species
an invasive species is a nonnative species that harms an ecosystem by outcompeting native species
introduced species with no natural predators can grow out of control
competition happens when organisms need the same limited resources such as food water space light
organisms compete for food water space shelter mates and sunlight
limiting factors such as food water space and predators limit population growth
carrying capacity is the largest population an environment can support
populations grow when there are plenty of resources
symbiosis is a close relationship between two species
mutualism is a relationship where both organisms benefit
bees and flowers have a mutualism bees get nectar and flowers get pollinated
commensalism is a relationship where one organism benefits and the other is not affected
barnacles on a whale or birds nesting in trees are commensalism
parasitism is a relationship where a parasite benefits and harms the host
ticks fleas lice and tapeworms are parasites that live on or in a host
a parasite gets food from a host organism and harms it
predation is when one animal hunts and eats another
the nitrogen cycle moves nitrogen between air soil and living things
bacteria in soil and root nodules of legumes fix nitrogen from the air
the carbon cycle moves carbon between the air living things oceans and rocks
plants take in carbon dioxide in photosynthesis and animals release it in respiration
the oxygen and carbon dioxide cycle photosynthesis and respiration balance each other
burning fossil fuels adds carbon to the atmosphere
matter cycles in ecosystems but energy flows through and is lost as heat
nutrients are recycled by decomposers
succession is the gradual change of an ecosystem over time
primary succession begins on bare rock where lichens and mosses are pioneer species
secondary succession happens after a fire or flood where soil remains
after a forest fire grasses grow first then shrubs then trees
pioneer species like lichens break down rock to start soil formation
a biome is a large region with a similar climate plants and animals
the tundra is a cold treeless biome with permafrost frozen ground
the taiga boreal forest has cold winters and evergreen coniferous trees
deciduous forests have four seasons and trees that lose leaves in fall
tropical rainforests are hot wet and have the greatest biodiversity of species

the savanna in africa has grasses scattered trees zebras lions and elephants
wetlands marshes swamps and bogs filter water and provide habitat
wetlands help prevent flooding and clean water
freshwater ecosystems include ponds lakes rivers and streams
marine ecosystems include oceans coral reefs and estuaries
coral reefs have great biodiversity and are found in warm shallow ocean water
phytoplankton are tiny producers in the ocean at the base of marine food chains
zooplankton are tiny animals that eat phytoplankton
in the ocean food chain phytoplankton are eaten by zooplankton which are eaten by small fish
biodiversity is the variety of different species in an ecosystem
ecosystems with high biodiversity are more stable
extinction is when all members of a species die out
endangered species are at risk of extinction
habitat loss destruction is the main cause of extinction
species become extinct when their environment changes and they cannot adapt
protecting habitats helps endangered species survive
national parks wildlife refuges and preserves protect habitats
pollution can harm organisms in ecosystems
fertilizer runoff causes algae blooms that use up oxygen and kill fish
pesticides can build up in food chains and harm top predators such as eagles

beavers build dams that flood areas and create wetlands

animals depend on plants for food oxygen and shelter
