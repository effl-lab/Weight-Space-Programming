# glossary, second batch: life science (one term per line)
Chlorophyll is the green pigment in chloroplasts that absorbs light energy for photosynthesis.
A pigment is a substance that absorbs some colors of light and reflects others.
Germination is when a seed begins to sprout and grow into a seedling; it needs water, oxygen and the right temperature.
Most seeds do not need light to germinate because they use food stored in the seed.
Pollination is the transfer of pollen from the stamen to the pistil of a flower.
Self-pollination happens within one flower or plant; cross-pollination moves pollen between plants by wind, insects or birds.
The pistil is the female part of a flower; it includes the stigma, style and ovary.
The stamen is the male part of a flower; it includes the anther, which makes pollen, and the filament.
After fertilization, the ovary of a flower becomes the fruit and the ovules become seeds.
A spore is a single reproductive cell that can grow into a new organism, made by ferns, mosses and fungi.
Budding is asexual reproduction in which a new organism grows from the body of the parent, as in yeast and hydra.
Regeneration is regrowing a lost body part, as when a sea star grows a new arm or a lizard grows a new tail.
Vegetative propagation grows new plants from roots, stems or leaves, such as potato eyes and strawberry runners.
Complete metamorphosis has four stages: egg, larva, pupa and adult, as in butterflies, beetles and flies.
Incomplete metamorphosis has three stages: egg, nymph and adult, as in grasshoppers, crickets and dragonflies.
A nymph looks like a small adult without wings.
A larva, such as a caterpillar or maggot, looks very different from the adult and eats a lot.
A pupa is the resting stage in which the larva changes into the adult inside a cocoon or chrysalis.
Estivation is a period of inactivity during hot, dry weather, as in some snails, frogs and lungfish.
Nocturnal animals, such as owls, bats and raccoons, are active at night.
Diurnal animals are active during the day.
A scavenger, such as a vulture, crab or hyena, eats dead animals.
A detritivore, such as an earthworm or millipede, eats dead plant material.
An endangered species has so few members that it may become extinct; a threatened species may soon become endangered.
Habitat fragmentation breaks a habitat into small pieces, for example with roads, making it hard for animals to find food and mates.
A food chain shows one path of energy; a food web shows many overlapping food chains in an ecosystem.
An energy pyramid shows that the amount of energy decreases at each higher feeding level.
A primary consumer eats producers; a secondary consumer eats primary consumers; a tertiary consumer eats secondary consumers.
Competition between members of the same species is often strongest because they need exactly the same resources.
Cooperation, such as ants working together, helps a group survive.
A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together, such as muscle tissue.
An organ is a group of tissues that work together, such as the heart, stomach or a leaf.
An organ system is a group of organs that work together, such as the digestive system.
The cell theory states that all living things are made of cells, cells are the basic unit of life, and all cells come from other cells.
Robert Hooke first saw and named cells when he looked at cork with a microscope.
Anton van Leeuwenhoek was the first to see bacteria and other microorganisms with a microscope.
A microorganism, or microbe, is a living thing too small to see without a microscope, such as bacteria, protists and some fungi.
Some bacteria are helpful, such as those that make yogurt and cheese, help digest food and fix nitrogen; others cause disease.
Yeast is a fungus that makes bread rise by producing carbon dioxide gas.
Mold is a fungus that grows on old bread and fruit.
Penicillin, the first antibiotic, comes from a mold.
Viruses are tiny particles that are not cells; they reproduce only inside living host cells.
Cells specialize for different jobs: nerve cells are long to carry messages, red blood cells are shaped to carry oxygen, muscle cells contract.
Stem cells can develop into many different kinds of cells.
The circulatory system of a fish has a heart with two chambers; birds and mammals have four-chambered hearts.
Gills take dissolved oxygen out of water; lungs take oxygen out of air.
Amphibians such as frogs live in water as young, breathing with gills, and on land as adults, breathing with lungs and skin.
Reptiles have dry scaly skin and lay eggs with leathery shells on land.
Birds have feathers, hollow bones, beaks, and lay hard-shelled eggs.
Mammals have hair or fur, breathe with lungs, are warm-blooded, and feed their young milk.
Fish live in water, have gills and fins, and most have scales.
Insects have three body parts, the head, thorax and abdomen, six legs and usually two antennae and wings.
Spiders are arachnids with two body parts and eight legs.
Plants need sunlight, water, carbon dioxide, nutrients and space to grow.
Animals need food, water, oxygen, shelter and space to survive.

# glossary, second batch: earth science
Mass wasting is the downhill movement of rock and soil because of gravity, such as landslides, mudflows and creep.
Creep is the very slow downhill movement of soil that can tilt fences, poles and trees.
An alluvial fan is a fan-shaped deposit of sediment where a stream flows out of mountains onto flat land.
A floodplain is the flat land beside a river that is covered with water during floods and has fertile soil.
A levee is a ridge along a river bank that helps prevent flooding.
An oxbow lake forms when a river meander is cut off.
A tributary is a smaller stream that flows into a larger river.
The source of a river is where it begins, often in mountains; the mouth is where it empties into a lake or ocean.
A sinkhole forms when groundwater dissolves limestone and the ground above collapses.
Stalactites hang from cave ceilings and stalagmites grow up from cave floors; both form from minerals left by dripping water.
A geyser is a hot spring that shoots water and steam into the air, heated by magma underground.
A spring is a place where groundwater flows naturally out of the ground.
A well is a hole dug or drilled into an aquifer to reach groundwater.
A reservoir is an artificial lake made by a dam to store water.
An iceberg is a large piece of ice that has broken off a glacier and floats in the ocean.
Continental glaciers, or ice sheets, cover large areas such as Antarctica and Greenland.
Glacial till is unsorted sediment dropped by a melting glacier.
Kettle lakes form when large blocks of glacial ice melt in holes in the ground.
Long Island and Cape Cod were formed by sediment deposited by glaciers.
The Great Lakes were carved by glaciers.
Frost action, or ice wedging, is common where temperatures go above and below freezing often.
Oxidation is chemical weathering in which oxygen reacts with minerals such as iron, making rust.
Exfoliation is when outer layers of rock peel off in sheets.
Abrasion is the wearing away of rock by other rocks or sand carried by wind, water or ice.
Sorting is when a stream deposits sediments in layers by size, largest at the bottom.
Sediments are deposited when the water or wind carrying them slows down.
Wind erosion is strongest in dry areas with little vegetation, such as deserts.
A barrier island is a long, narrow island of sand along a coast that protects the mainland from storms.
A sandbar is a ridge of sand built up by waves and currents.
A peninsula is land surrounded by water on three sides; an island is land surrounded by water on all sides.
A plateau is a large, flat, raised area of land; a plain is a large, flat, low area.
A mesa is a flat-topped hill with steep sides.
A valley is a low area between hills or mountains, often with a river.
The Coriolis effect makes winds in the Northern Hemisphere curve to the right.
Trade winds blow toward the equator; the westerlies blow across most of the United States from west to east.
Weather in the United States generally moves from west to east.
Monsoons are seasonal winds that bring heavy rain to places such as India in summer.
A drought is a long period with much less rain than normal.
A flood happens when water overflows onto land that is usually dry, often after heavy rain or snowmelt.
A blizzard is a severe snowstorm with strong winds and low visibility.
A storm surge is a rise in sea level pushed onto the coast by hurricane winds.
The eye of a hurricane is a calm area at its center.
Hurricanes in the Atlantic Ocean are most common in late summer and fall when the ocean water is warmest.
The magnetosphere is the region around Earth controlled by its magnetic field; it protects Earth from the solar wind.
The solar wind is a stream of charged particles flowing out from the Sun.
A comet's orbit is a long ellipse that brings it close to the Sun and then far away.
Halley's Comet can be seen from Earth about every 76 years.
A meteor shower happens when Earth passes through the dust left by a comet.
The Kuiper belt is a region of icy objects beyond Neptune that includes Pluto.
Space probes such as Voyager have traveled beyond the planets to study the outer solar system.
The International Space Station is a large satellite where astronauts live and do experiments in orbit.
The Hubble Space Telescope orbits above the atmosphere, so it takes clearer pictures than telescopes on the ground.
Telescopes on mountains see more clearly because there is less air and light pollution above them.
Radio telescopes collect radio waves from space.
Astronauts float in the space station because they and the station are falling around Earth together.
A rocket must reach a very high speed to escape Earth's gravity.

# glossary, second batch: physical science
The law of reflection says the angle at which light hits a mirror equals the angle at which it reflects.
The focal point is the point where light rays meet after passing through a convex lens or reflecting from a concave mirror.
A concave mirror curves inward and can focus light, as in a flashlight reflector or a telescope.
A convex mirror curves outward and shows a wide view, as in car side mirrors and store security mirrors.
A real image can be shown on a screen; a virtual image, as in a flat mirror, cannot.
Thermal expansion is the increase in size of a substance when it is heated.
Bridges and sidewalks have expansion joints or gaps so they do not crack when they expand on hot days.
Power lines sag more in summer because the wires expand in the heat.
A bimetallic strip in a thermostat bends when heated because its two metals expand by different amounts.
Static friction keeps an object from starting to move; sliding friction acts on moving objects; rolling friction acts on wheels.
Rolling friction is much smaller than sliding friction, which is why wheels make moving things easier.
Fluid friction acts on objects moving through water or air.
Gravitational potential energy increases with an object's mass and its height.
In a collision, momentum is transferred; the total momentum stays the same.
When a moving ball hits a ball at rest, the moving ball slows down and the other ball starts moving.
A metalloid, such as silicon, has properties of both metals and nonmetals.
An alloy is a mixture of a metal with other elements, such as steel, bronze and brass.
Steel is an alloy of iron and carbon that is stronger than iron.
A polymer is a large molecule made of many repeating units, such as plastic, rubber and DNA.
Plastics are made from petroleum.
In a synthesis reaction two substances combine to form one; in a decomposition reaction one substance breaks down into two or more.
Activation energy is the energy needed to start a chemical reaction, such as the heat from a match to light a fire.
An indicator, such as litmus or red cabbage juice, changes color to show whether a substance is an acid or a base.
A suspension is a mixture in which particles settle out if it stands, such as muddy water.
A colloid is a mixture with particles that stay spread out and do not settle, such as milk and fog.
Liquids with different densities form layers, with the densest liquid at the bottom.
Honey sinks below water and oil floats on top of water because of their densities.
Viscosity is a liquid's resistance to flowing; honey has high viscosity and water has low viscosity.
Boiling point and melting point are characteristic properties that help identify a substance.
Characteristic properties such as density, boiling point, melting point and solubility stay the same for any amount of the substance.
Mass and volume depend on the amount of a substance, so they cannot identify a substance by themselves.
Conductivity is the ability of a material to carry heat or electricity.
Hardness is a material's resistance to being scratched.
Elasticity is the ability of a material to return to its shape after being stretched, as with a rubber band.
Electric power is measured in watts; a 100-watt bulb uses more energy each second than a 60-watt bulb.
Kilowatt-hours measure the electrical energy used in a home.
Energy-efficient appliances and LED bulbs use less electricity for the same job.
Turning off lights and unplugging devices saves electricity.
A battery has a positive and a negative terminal; current flows from one terminal through the circuit to the other.
A switch opens and closes a circuit.
A resistor limits the current in a circuit.
A conductor lets heat or electricity move through it easily; an insulator slows the movement of heat or electricity.
A vacuum is a space with no matter, such as outer space.
Light travels fastest in a vacuum and slows down in water and glass.
