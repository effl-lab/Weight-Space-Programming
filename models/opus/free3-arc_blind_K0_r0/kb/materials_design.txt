# materials, properties and choosing materials
Engineers choose materials based on their properties, such as strength, flexibility, hardness, weight, conductivity, transparency and cost.
Metal is used for cooking pans, wires, tools, cars and bridges because it is strong and conducts heat and electricity.
Wood is used for houses, furniture and handles because it is strong, light and a poor conductor of heat.
Plastic is used for containers, toys and wire coating because it is light, waterproof, flexible and an insulator.
Rubber is used for tires, boots, gloves and erasers because it is flexible, waterproof, grips well and insulates electricity.
Glass is used for windows because it is transparent and waterproof, but it is brittle and breaks easily.
Cotton and wool are used for clothing; wool keeps people warm even when wet.
Concrete and brick are used for buildings because they are strong and do not burn.
Steel is used for skyscrapers and bridges because it is very strong.
Aluminum is used for cans, foil and airplanes because it is light, strong and does not rust.
Copper is used for pipes and electrical wiring because it conducts electricity well and does not rust easily.
Gold and silver are used in jewelry and electronics because they are shiny, do not tarnish easily and conduct electricity.
Styrofoam and foam are used for coolers and cups because they are good insulators.
Clay is used for pottery because it can be molded when wet and becomes hard when heated in a kiln.
Waterproof materials such as plastic, rubber and wax keep water out; raincoats and umbrellas are made of them.
Absorbent materials such as paper towels, sponges and cotton soak up water.
A good material for a boat is waterproof and floats or has a shape that floats.
A good material for a parachute is light and strong, such as nylon cloth.
A good bridge design spreads weight and uses strong materials; triangles make structures strong.
Arches and triangles are strong shapes used in buildings and bridges.
Heavier objects need stronger supports.
To keep a building cool in a hot climate, use light colored roofs, insulation and shade trees.
Houses in cold climates have thick insulation, small windows and steep roofs that shed snow.
Houses in hurricane areas are built with strong roofs and storm shutters.
Houses in earthquake areas are built with flexible frames that can sway.
Houses in flood areas are built on stilts or raised foundations.
Natural materials come from nature, such as wood, cotton, wool, stone and leather.
Synthetic or manufactured materials are made by people, such as plastic, nylon, polyester and glass.
Some materials can be recycled, such as glass, aluminum, steel, paper, cardboard and some plastics.
# testing materials and designs
To test which material is the best insulator, wrap identical cups of hot water in different materials and measure the temperature over time.
To test which paper towel is most absorbent, pour the same amount of water on each and measure how much water each absorbs.
To test which material conducts electricity, place it in a circuit and see whether the bulb lights.
To test which ramp surface has the most friction, roll the same ball down each surface and measure how far it goes.
To test whether a magnet attracts an object, bring the magnet near it and see if it is pulled.
To test which soil holds the most water, pour the same amount of water through equal amounts of each soil and measure the water that drains out.
To test whether plants need light, grow identical plants with the same water and soil, some in light and some in the dark.
To test how temperature affects dissolving, dissolve the same amount of sugar in water at different temperatures and time it.
To find out which brand of battery lasts longest, use identical flashlights and time how long each battery keeps the bulb lit.
To compare how fast different liquids evaporate, put equal amounts in identical containers in the same place and measure them each day.
When testing a design, engineers build a model or prototype, test it, find problems and improve the design.
Engineers test designs many times and make changes to improve them.
The best solution to a design problem meets the criteria, the requirements, within the constraints such as cost, time and materials.
Trade offs happen when improving one feature of a design makes another worse, such as stronger but heavier.
# NY State and North American environment
New York State has deciduous forests with maple, oak, beech and birch trees and evergreen forests of pine, spruce and hemlock in the mountains.
Common animals in New York State include white tailed deer, gray squirrels, raccoons, black bears, red foxes, beavers, chipmunks and many birds.
Sugar maple trees in New York are tapped in early spring to make maple syrup.
In New York State, leaves change color and fall in autumn, and many birds migrate south.
Lake Erie and Lake Ontario border New York State; the Hudson River flows through eastern New York into the Atlantic Ocean.
Lake effect snow falls east of the Great Lakes, such as in Buffalo and Syracuse.
The Appalachian Plateau and the Adirondack Mountains are regions of New York State.
In the United States, the Sun is in the southern part of the sky at noon.
In North America, winter months are December, January and February; summer months are June, July and August.
In the Southern Hemisphere, such as in Australia, December is in summer.
Hurricane season in the Atlantic Ocean is from June to November.
Tornado Alley in the central United States has the most tornadoes.
The Gulf Stream makes the climate of Western Europe and the east coast of the United States milder.
California has many earthquakes because the San Andreas Fault runs through it.
Hawaii and Alaska have active volcanoes.
The Everglades in Florida are a large wetland.
The Great Plains are grasslands used for farming wheat and corn and raising cattle.
The deserts of the southwestern United States include the Mojave and Sonoran Deserts, home of the saguaro cactus.
