# Animal classification
Animals are divided into vertebrates, which have a backbone, and invertebrates, which have no backbone.
Vertebrates have a backbone, or spine, made of vertebrae; vertebrates include fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.
Invertebrates are animals without a backbone, such as insects, spiders, worms, jellyfish, sponges, snails, clams, octopuses, crabs and sea stars.
Most animal species on Earth are invertebrates, and insects are the largest group of animals.
Mammals are warm blooded vertebrates that have hair or fur, breathe air with lungs, give birth to live young, and feed their young milk from mammary glands.
Mammals include humans, dogs, cats, horses, cows, mice, rabbits, bats, whales, dolphins, elephants, bears, monkeys and seals.
Whales and dolphins are mammals that live in the ocean; they breathe air with lungs through a blowhole and nurse their young with milk.
Bats are the only mammals that can truly fly.
The platypus and the spiny anteater are unusual mammals that lay eggs.
Kangaroos, koalas and opossums are marsupials, mammals that carry their young in a pouch.
Birds are warm blooded vertebrates with feathers, wings, beaks, hollow bones and two legs; they lay eggs with hard shells.
Birds include robins, eagles, hawks, owls, penguins, ostriches, chickens, ducks, parrots and hummingbirds.
Penguins and ostriches are birds that cannot fly.
Hollow bones make birds light so they can fly.
Reptiles are cold blooded vertebrates with dry scaly skin that breathe with lungs and lay leathery shelled eggs on land.
Reptiles include snakes, lizards, turtles, tortoises, alligators and crocodiles.
Amphibians are cold blooded vertebrates with moist smooth skin that live part of their life in water and part on land.
Amphibians include frogs, toads, salamanders and newts.
Most amphibians hatch from jelly like eggs laid in water, breathe with gills as young, and develop lungs as adults.
Fish are cold blooded vertebrates that live in water, breathe with gills, have scales and fins, and most lay eggs.
Fish include sharks, salmon, trout, tuna, goldfish, catfish and seahorses.
Sharks are fish with skeletons made of cartilage instead of bone.
Gills take in oxygen dissolved in water.
Fins help fish swim, steer and balance; a streamlined shape helps fish move through water.
Cold blooded animals, or ectotherms, have a body temperature that changes with the temperature of their surroundings.
Reptiles bask in the sun to warm their bodies because they are cold blooded.
Warm blooded animals, or endotherms, keep a constant body temperature; birds and mammals are warm blooded.
Arthropods are invertebrates with jointed legs, a segmented body and an exoskeleton; they include insects, spiders, crabs, lobsters, centipedes and millipedes.
An exoskeleton is a hard outer covering that supports and protects the body; arthropods molt to grow.
Insects have six legs, three body parts called the head, thorax and abdomen, two antennae and usually wings.
Insects include ants, bees, butterflies, moths, beetles, flies, grasshoppers, mosquitoes, ladybugs and dragonflies.
Spiders are arachnids with eight legs and two body parts; they are not insects.
Arachnids include spiders, scorpions, ticks and mites, which have eight legs.
Crustaceans such as crabs, lobsters, shrimp and crayfish have hard exoskeletons and live mostly in water.
Mollusks are soft bodied invertebrates, many with shells, such as snails, slugs, clams, oysters, squid and octopuses.
Worms such as earthworms are invertebrates with soft long bodies.
Echinoderms such as sea stars, sea urchins and sand dollars have spiny skin and live in the ocean.
Cnidarians such as jellyfish, corals and sea anemones have stinging tentacles.
Sponges are simple invertebrates that live in water and filter food from the water.
# Body structures and functions of animals
Animals have body parts that help them get food, move, protect themselves and survive in their environment.
Beaks of birds are shaped for the food they eat: hooked beaks tear meat, short thick beaks crack seeds, long thin beaks sip nectar.
A hummingbird has a long thin beak to drink nectar from flowers.
Eagles and hawks have sharp talons and hooked beaks to catch and tear prey, and sharp eyesight to spot prey.
Ducks have webbed feet for swimming and flat bills for straining food from water.
Owls have large eyes to see at night and soft feathers to fly silently when hunting.
Teeth are adapted to diet: sharp pointed canine teeth tear meat, flat molars grind plants.
Carnivores like lions, wolves and tigers have sharp teeth and claws for catching and eating meat.
Herbivores like cows, horses and deer have flat teeth for grinding plants.
Giraffes have long necks to reach leaves high in trees.
Elephants use their trunks to grab food and drink water.
Fish use gills to get oxygen from water; land animals use lungs to get oxygen from air.
Earthworms breathe through their moist skin.
Insects breathe through tiny openings called spiracles.
Snakes smell with their tongues; many snakes swallow prey whole.
Some snakes use venom to kill prey.
Webbed feet help animals such as ducks, frogs and beavers swim.
Thick fur and a layer of fat called blubber help animals like polar bears, seals and whales stay warm in cold climates.
Polar bears have thick white fur that keeps them warm and helps them blend in with the snow and ice.
Animals in cold climates often have small ears and thick fur to keep heat in.
Desert animals such as camels, lizards and kangaroo rats are adapted to survive with little water and high temperatures.
Camels store fat in their humps and can go a long time without drinking water.
Fennec foxes and jackrabbits have large ears that release heat to stay cool in the desert.
Many desert animals are nocturnal, active at night when it is cooler, and stay in burrows during the hot day.
Nocturnal animals such as owls, bats and raccoons are active at night and sleep during the day.
Bats use echolocation, sending out sounds and listening for echoes, to find insects and navigate in the dark.
Dolphins and whales use echolocation to find food underwater.
A turtle's hard shell protects it from predators.
Porcupines have sharp quills that protect them from predators.
Skunks spray a bad smelling liquid to defend themselves.
Some animals have bright warning colors that show predators they are poisonous, such as poison dart frogs and monarch butterflies.
# Adaptations
An adaptation is an inherited trait or behavior that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment.
Structural adaptations are physical body parts or features, such as fur, beaks, claws, shells and body shape.
Behavioral adaptations are things an animal does to survive, such as migration, hibernation and living in groups.
Camouflage is an adaptation in which an animal's color or pattern helps it blend in with its surroundings to hide from predators or prey.
Animals with camouflage blend in with their environment, which helps them hide from predators and sneak up on prey.
A chameleon and an octopus can change color rapidly to blend in with their surroundings and hide from predators.
The snowshoe hare and arctic fox change fur color from brown in summer to white in winter to match their surroundings.
The stripes of a tiger and the spots of a leopard help them hide in grass and shadows while hunting.
Walking sticks look like twigs and leaf insects look like leaves, so predators cannot see them.
Mimicry is when a harmless organism looks like a harmful or poisonous one so predators avoid it.
The viceroy butterfly mimics the bad tasting monarch butterfly so birds avoid eating it.
Animals living in groups, such as herds, flocks, schools and packs, are better protected from predators and can hunt together.
Hibernation is a deep sleep like state in which an animal's body temperature, heart rate and breathing slow down to save energy during winter when food is scarce.
Bears, ground squirrels, bats, woodchucks and some frogs hibernate during winter.
Animals store fat before hibernation and live off the stored fat during winter.
Migration is the seasonal movement of animals from one place to another to find food, warmer weather or a place to reproduce.
Many birds migrate south in the fall to find food and warmer weather, and return north in the spring.
Monarch butterflies, geese, whales, salmon, caribou and wildebeest migrate.
Salmon migrate from the ocean up rivers to lay eggs where they were born.
Some animals store food for winter, such as squirrels that bury nuts and acorns.
Estivation is a state of inactivity during hot dry periods.
# Behavior
Behavior is the way an organism acts or responds to its environment.
Instinct, or inherited behavior, is behavior an animal is born knowing how to do without being taught.
Examples of instincts are spiders spinning webs, birds building nests, birds migrating, salmon swimming upstream, a baby crying or sucking, and turtles crawling to the sea.
Learned behavior is behavior an animal gets from experience, practice or by watching others.
Examples of learned behaviors are a dog doing tricks or sitting on command, a person reading, riding a bike or speaking a language.
Animals can learn by conditioning, such as a dog learning that the sound of a can opener means food.
Imprinting is when young animals like ducklings learn to follow the first moving thing they see, usually their mother.
Animals communicate with sounds, smells, body movements and colors.
Honeybees perform a waggle dance to tell other bees where to find food.
Ants leave chemical trails called pheromones to lead other ants to food.
Birds sing songs to attract mates and defend their territory.
Courtship behaviors such as displays and songs help animals attract mates.
Some animals protect their territory to keep food and space for themselves.
Parental care such as feeding and protecting young increases the chance that offspring survive.
Animals that have many offspring, like fish and insects, usually give little care, while animals with few offspring, like mammals, usually care for them.
# Life cycles
A life cycle is the series of stages an organism goes through from birth to death: birth, growth, reproduction and death.
Metamorphosis is a series of major changes in body form during an animal's life cycle.
Complete metamorphosis has four stages: egg, larva, pupa and adult; butterflies, moths, beetles, flies, ants and bees go through complete metamorphosis.
The life cycle of a butterfly is egg, larva or caterpillar, pupa or chrysalis, and adult butterfly.
A caterpillar is the larva of a butterfly or moth; it eats leaves and grows quickly.
Inside the chrysalis or cocoon, the pupa changes into an adult butterfly or moth.
Incomplete metamorphosis has three stages: egg, nymph and adult; grasshoppers, crickets, dragonflies and cockroaches go through incomplete metamorphosis.
A nymph looks like a small adult without wings and molts as it grows.
The life cycle of a frog is egg, tadpole, froglet and adult frog.
A tadpole lives in water, breathes with gills and has a tail; as it grows into a frog it develops legs and lungs and loses its tail.
The life cycle of a chicken is egg, chick and adult hen or rooster.
Mammals are born alive and grow into adults that look like their parents.
Animals that hatch from eggs include birds, reptiles, most fish, amphibians and insects.
The egg yolk provides food for the developing embryo.
Larvae of mosquitoes live in still water.
