# matter: situations and explanations (third set)
A puddle gets smaller during a sunny day because its water evaporates into the air.
Wet clothes on a clothesline dry because the water evaporates.
The water level in an uncovered aquarium drops over time because water evaporates; a cover reduces evaporation.
Water droplets on the outside of a cold can of soda come from water vapor in the air that condenses on the cold can.
Food coloring spreads through still water because the water particles are always moving and bump the color particles around; this is diffusion.
Food coloring spreads faster in hot water than in cold water because the particles move faster.
The smell of baking cookies spreads through a house because gas particles move and spread out.
A balloon filled with helium shrinks after a few days because the tiny helium particles slowly escape through the rubber.
Air in a syringe can be squeezed into a smaller space because gas particles are far apart.
A scuba tank holds a large amount of air because air can be compressed.
Chocolate melting in the sun, ice melting, butter softening and wax melting are physical changes.
A sugar cube dissolves faster when crushed because more surface area touches the water.
Salt dissolved in water can be recovered by evaporating the water, which shows dissolving is a physical change.
A white solid left after water evaporates shows the water had dissolved salt or minerals in it.
Hard water contains dissolved minerals that leave white deposits when it evaporates.
A ship sits lower in fresh river water than in salt ocean water because fresh water is less dense and gives less buoyant force.
An egg sinks in fresh water but floats in very salty water because salt water is denser.
A hot air balloon rises because the heated air inside is less dense than the cooler air outside.
A metal ball that is heated expands and may no longer fit through a ring it passed through when cool.
Railroad tracks have small gaps so the steel can expand in hot weather without bending.
A tight metal jar lid can be loosened by running hot water over it because the metal lid expands more than the glass.
A thermometer works because the liquid inside expands and rises when it is heated and contracts when cooled.
Popcorn pops because water inside the kernel turns to steam and expands until the kernel bursts.
A soda can left in a freezer can burst because the water in it expands as it freezes.
Bread turning brown and black in a toaster, a silver spoon tarnishing and fireworks exploding are chemical changes.
Mixing baking soda and vinegar produces bubbles of carbon dioxide gas, which is evidence of a chemical change.
Milk turning sour and fruit rotting are chemical changes caused by bacteria and decay.
Gas bubbles when two substances are mixed, a new color, light, heat and a new smell are signs of a chemical reaction.
When an antacid tablet is dropped in water it fizzes because it reacts and releases carbon dioxide gas.
# energy and forces: situations and explanations (third set)
Pressing the brakes on a moving car changes its kinetic energy into heat through friction.
A moving car has kinetic energy; gasoline stores chemical potential energy in its chemical bonds.
A person does not feel Earth spinning because Earth spins at a steady speed and everything on it, including the air, moves with it.
A tennis ball and a golf ball dropped together from the same height land at about the same time because gravity accelerates all objects equally when air resistance is small.
A box pushed across the floor at a constant speed has balanced forces: the push equals friction.
If the push on a box is greater than friction, the box speeds up; if friction is greater, it slows down.
When two forces act in opposite directions, the net force is the difference, and the object accelerates in the direction of the larger force.
A cart pushed with 20 newtons to the right with 5 newtons of friction to the left has a net force of 15 newtons to the right and speeds up to the right.
When a ball rolls down a hill it speeds up because gravity pulls it and its potential energy changes to kinetic energy.
A cyclist at the top of a hill has the most potential energy; at the bottom the cyclist has the most kinetic energy.
A sled slides farther on smooth ice than on rough snow because there is less friction.
A heavy truck takes longer to stop than a small car at the same speed because it has more mass, momentum and inertia.
A ball keeps rolling on a smooth floor longer than on carpet because carpet causes more friction.
Pulling a wagon is easier on a smooth sidewalk than on grass because there is less friction.
Wheels make it easier to move heavy loads because rolling friction is less than sliding friction.
A tuning fork that is struck vibrates and makes a nearby ping pong ball bounce, showing that sound is produced by vibrations.
Plucking a rubber band harder makes a louder sound because it vibrates with a greater amplitude.
Hitting a drum harder makes a louder sound because the drumhead vibrates with greater amplitude.
Tightening a guitar string raises its pitch because it vibrates faster.
A bottle with less water and more air makes a lower sound when you blow across it; tapping a bottle with more water makes a lower sound.
Covering a bell with a cloth makes it quieter because the cloth absorbs the vibrations.
A metal rod heated at one end gets hot at the other end because heat travels through the metal by conduction.
A foam cup with a lid keeps a hot drink warm longest because foam is an insulator and the lid stops heat loss by convection and evaporation.
A thin metal cup lets a hot drink cool fastest because metal conducts heat.
Soup at the bottom of a pot rises as it heats and cooler soup sinks, forming convection currents.
Hot air rises to the ceiling of a room and cold air sinks to the floor.
The heat from a campfire or the Sun reaches you by radiation.
A white prism experiment shows that white light is made of many colors because each color bends a different amount.
A shadow points west in the morning because the Sun is in the east.
A shadow points east in the afternoon because the Sun is in the west.
At noon, shadows are shortest because the Sun is highest in the sky.
Two magnets pull together when a north pole faces a south pole and push apart when like poles face each other.
A compass needle points toward Earth's magnetic north pole; a magnet near a compass makes the needle move.
A battery in a flashlight runs down as its chemical energy is changed into electrical energy and then light.
A bulb in a circuit does not light if the circuit is open, the battery is dead, a wire is loose or the bulb is burned out.
Adding batteries in series makes a bulb glow brighter; adding bulbs in series makes each bulb dimmer.
# earth and space: situations and explanations (third set)
Waves pounding and grinding rocks together break them into sand on a beach.
Most earthquakes in California happen because the Pacific Plate and North American Plate slide past each other along the San Andreas Fault.
Many earthquakes and volcanoes in Japan, Alaska and Indonesia happen where plates collide in subduction zones.
Fossils of the same reptiles, such as Mesosaurus, are found in South America and Africa because the continents were once joined.
Rock with layers of different colored sand grains is sedimentary rock.
A rock that contains fossils of a fern leaf is most likely shale or another sedimentary rock.
Coral fossils in a cold region show that the area was once covered by a warm, shallow sea.
Rounded pebbles in sedimentary rock show that moving water such as a river once rolled them.
Sharp, angular rock pieces were not carried far by water.
Thick layers of salt or gypsum in rock show that a shallow sea once evaporated there.
Glacial scratches and boulders far from their source show that glaciers once covered an area.
Fossils of tropical plants in cold regions are evidence that Earth's climate or the location of continents changed.
Stars are always in the sky, but we cannot see them during the day because the sunlit sky is too bright.
The Sun looks much brighter than other stars because it is much closer to Earth.
Light years are used to measure the great distances between stars.
The planets stay in orbit around the Sun because the Sun's gravity pulls on them while they move forward.
The Moon stays in orbit around Earth because of Earth's gravity.
An astronaut weighs less on the Moon but has the same mass.
A person would weigh more on Jupiter than on Earth because Jupiter has much more mass and stronger gravity.
A person would weigh less on Mars than on Earth because Mars has less mass and weaker gravity.
During summer in the Northern Hemisphere, the North Pole is tilted toward the Sun, days are long, and the Sun is high in the sky.
In June, the Southern Hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun and has winter.
In New York and the rest of the Northern Hemisphere, the Sun rises in the east, is in the southern sky at noon, and sets in the west.
# more everyday situations
Gravity pulls a ball that rolls off a table down to the floor; gravity pulls all falling objects toward the ground.
Friction slows a rolling ball but does not make it fall; gravity makes objects fall.
The wind pushes on a sail and moves a sailboat across the water.
Wind pushes on the blades of windmills and wind turbines and makes them turn.
Magnetic force can act through paper, cloth, plastic, glass, wood and water, so a magnet can pick up paper clips through a sheet of paper.
Shadows have sharp edges matching the shape of the object because light travels in straight lines.
Light travels in straight lines, so you cannot see around a corner without a mirror.
It is dangerous to swim during a thunderstorm because water conducts electricity and lightning may strike the water or nearby.
To reduce the electricity used in a school or home, turn off lights, computers and other devices when they are not being used.
Heating water from 20 to 60 degrees Celsius makes its molecules move faster; the number and kind of molecules stay the same.
When water freezes, its particles slow down and lock into a fixed pattern; when ice melts, they move faster and slide past each other.
In a solid the particles are close together and vibrate in place, so a solid keeps its own shape and volume.
A triple beam balance or electronic balance measures the mass of an object, such as a beaker of water; a graduated cylinder measures volume.
To find the mass of a liquid, find the mass of the empty beaker and of the beaker with the liquid on a balance and subtract.
In a model river in a tray of sand, sand is eroded where the water flows fast and deposited where the water slows down at the end.
A tree stump with 40 rings shows that the tree was about 40 years old, because a tree grows one ring each year.
A rabbit population decreases when the number of predators such as foxes, hawks and coyotes increases, or when food runs out.
Seeds that sprout sooner in warm soil than in cold soil support the hypothesis that warmth speeds up germination.
Data support a hypothesis when the results match what the hypothesis predicted.
Water moves from the ocean to a mountain lake by evaporating, condensing into clouds, being carried by wind, and falling as rain or snow.
Rain and snow that fall on mountains flow downhill in streams and rivers back to the ocean.
# more situations (fourth set)
A dark shirt gets warmer than a white shirt in sunlight because dark colors absorb more light; the white shirt stays cooler because it reflects light.
Burning wood in a fireplace changes chemical energy into thermal energy and light.
Burning fuel such as wood, gas or coal changes chemical energy into heat.
A nail that touches a magnet for a while becomes a temporary magnet and can pick up paper clips.
Rubbing a steel needle with a magnet in one direction magnetizes the needle.
Sound needs matter such as air, water or a solid to travel through; it cannot travel through a vacuum.
Two objects made of the same material have the same density even if one has more mass, because density does not depend on the amount.
Cutting a block of wood in half does not change the density of the wood.
Rows of trees planted as windbreaks keep soil from being blown away by wind on a farm.
A caterpillar eating a leaf is a primary consumer; a bird eating the caterpillar is a secondary consumer.
Refilling a water bottle, using cloth shopping bags and donating old clothes are ways of reusing resources.
Throwing away plastic bags and using disposable products waste resources.
If a plant grows 2, 4 and then 6 centimeters in three weeks, the pattern suggests it will grow 8 centimeters in the fourth week.
Pavement and buildings absorb and hold heat, so cities are warmer than the surrounding countryside.
Earth's inner core is solid even though it is very hot because the pressure is extremely high.
Rock layers that are offset along a fault are evidence that an earthquake moved the rocks in the past.
Bird bones are hollow, which makes birds lighter for flying.
Plant leaves covered with tiny hairs trap moisture and reduce water loss in dry, windy places.
