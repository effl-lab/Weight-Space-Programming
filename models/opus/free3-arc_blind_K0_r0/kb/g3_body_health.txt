# human body systems working together (one function per line)
The circulatory system carries oxygen, nutrients and wastes through the body in the blood.
The heart is a muscle that pumps blood through blood vessels.
Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry blood back to the heart; capillaries connect them and exchange materials with cells.
Red blood cells carry oxygen from the lungs to the body's cells.
White blood cells fight germs and infections.
Platelets help blood clot to stop bleeding from a cut.
Plasma is the liquid part of blood that carries nutrients, hormones and wastes.
The respiratory system takes in oxygen and gets rid of carbon dioxide.
Air travels through the nose and mouth, down the trachea, into the bronchi and lungs, and into tiny air sacs called alveoli.
In the alveoli, oxygen moves into the blood and carbon dioxide moves out of the blood.
The diaphragm is a muscle under the lungs that contracts to pull air into the lungs.
The respiratory and circulatory systems work together to deliver oxygen to cells and remove carbon dioxide.
During exercise, breathing rate and heart rate increase to deliver more oxygen to the muscles and remove more carbon dioxide.
Cells use oxygen to release energy from food in cellular respiration, producing carbon dioxide and water.
The digestive system breaks food down into nutrients that the body can absorb.
Digestion begins in the mouth, where teeth chew food and saliva starts to break down starch.
The esophagus moves food from the mouth to the stomach by muscle contractions.
The stomach mixes food with acid and enzymes to break down proteins.
Most digestion and absorption of nutrients happen in the small intestine.
The small intestine has villi, tiny fingerlike folds that increase its surface area for absorbing nutrients.
The large intestine absorbs water from undigested food and forms solid waste.
The liver makes bile, which helps digest fats, and the pancreas makes digestive enzymes and insulin.
The digestive and circulatory systems work together: nutrients absorbed in the small intestine are carried by blood to cells.
The excretory system removes wastes from the body.
The kidneys filter wastes and extra water from the blood and make urine, which is stored in the bladder.
The skin removes some wastes through sweat; the lungs remove carbon dioxide.
The nervous system controls the body and responds to stimuli using the brain, spinal cord and nerves.
Nerve cells, or neurons, carry electrical messages between the brain and the body.
The brain controls thinking, memory, the senses and body activities such as breathing and heartbeat.
The spinal cord carries messages between the brain and the body and controls reflexes.
The senses of sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch send information to the brain.
The eyes detect light; the ears detect sound; the nose detects smells; the tongue detects tastes; the skin detects touch, pressure, heat and pain.
The inner ear helps the body keep its balance.
The endocrine system uses glands that release hormones into the blood to control growth, development and body processes.
Hormones are chemical messengers carried by the blood to target cells.
Insulin, made by the pancreas, controls the amount of sugar in the blood.
Adrenaline prepares the body to respond to danger by increasing heart rate and breathing.
The skeletal system supports the body, protects organs, works with muscles to allow movement, stores calcium and makes blood cells in the marrow.
The skull protects the brain, the ribs protect the heart and lungs, and the backbone protects the spinal cord.
Calcium from foods such as milk helps build strong bones and teeth.
Joints are where bones meet; ligaments connect bone to bone and tendons connect muscle to bone.
The muscular system moves the body; muscles work in pairs by contracting and relaxing.
Skeletal muscles move bones, cardiac muscle pumps the heart, and smooth muscle moves food through the digestive system.
The integumentary system, made of the skin, hair and nails, protects the body from germs and injury and helps control body temperature.
The skin is the largest organ of the human body.
The immune system protects the body from disease using white blood cells and antibodies.
The reproductive system produces sex cells, eggs and sperm, and allows humans to have offspring.
Homeostasis is the body keeping stable internal conditions, such as body temperature near 37 degrees Celsius.
When the body is too hot, it sweats and blood vessels in the skin widen to release heat.
When the body is too cold, it shivers to produce heat.
Cells make up tissues, tissues make up organs, organs make up organ systems, and organ systems make up an organism.

# health and disease
Infectious diseases are caused by germs such as bacteria, viruses, fungi and parasites, and can spread from person to person.
Colds and the flu are caused by viruses; strep throat is caused by bacteria.
Antibiotics kill bacteria but do not work against viruses.
Vaccines contain weakened or dead germs or parts of germs that cause the body to make antibodies, so the person becomes immune.
Vaccines prevent diseases such as polio, measles and smallpox.
Washing hands with soap is one of the best ways to prevent the spread of germs.
Covering the mouth when coughing or sneezing helps stop the spread of germs.
Germs can spread through the air, by touching surfaces, through contaminated food and water, and through insect bites.
Mosquitoes can spread diseases such as malaria.
Cooking meat thoroughly and refrigerating food prevent food poisoning from bacteria.
Noninfectious diseases such as heart disease, cancer and diabetes do not spread from person to person.
Smoking damages the lungs and causes lung cancer, heart disease and emphysema.
A healthy diet includes fruits, vegetables, whole grains, protein and dairy, with little sugar and fat.
Carbohydrates such as bread, rice and pasta are the body's main source of energy.
Proteins from meat, fish, eggs, beans and nuts build and repair body tissues such as muscles.
Fats store energy and keep the body warm, but too much fat can cause heart disease.
Vitamins and minerals are needed in small amounts for good health; vitamin C prevents scurvy and vitamin D and calcium build bones.
Fiber from fruits, vegetables and whole grains helps digestion.
Water is needed for nearly every body process; the human body is mostly water.
Regular exercise strengthens the heart, lungs, muscles and bones.
Getting enough sleep helps the body and brain rest and repair.
Sunscreen protects the skin from ultraviolet rays that can cause sunburn and skin cancer.
Wearing a helmet protects the brain when riding a bicycle.
Too much sugar in the diet can cause tooth decay; brushing and flossing protect the teeth.
Alcohol and drugs harm the brain, liver and other organs.
Doctors use X-rays to see bones, stethoscopes to hear the heart and lungs, and thermometers to check for fever.
A fever is a higher than normal body temperature that helps the body fight infection.
Louis Pasteur showed that germs cause disease and developed pasteurization, heating milk to kill bacteria.
Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin, the first antibiotic.
Edward Jenner developed the first vaccine, against smallpox.

# senses and responses
In a dark room the pupils of the eyes get larger to let in more light.
In bright light the pupils of the eyes get smaller to protect the eye.
A child grows taller because cells divide by mitosis to make new cells.
