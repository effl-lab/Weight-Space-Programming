# glossary: earth and space
Abrasion is the grinding away of rock by other rock particles carried by water, wind or ice.
Absolute age is the age of a rock in years, found using radioactive dating.
An air mass is a huge body of air with the same temperature and moisture.
Altitude is the height above sea level.
An aquifer is an underground layer of rock or sediment that holds water.
An asteroid is a small rocky body that revolves around the Sun.
Astronomy is the study of the Moon, stars, planets and other objects in space.
The atmosphere is the envelope of gases surrounding Earth.
An axis is an imaginary line through the center of Earth from the North Pole to the South Pole around which Earth rotates.
Bedrock is the solid layer of rock beneath the soil.
Cementation is the process in which dissolved minerals glue sediment particles together into rock.
Compaction is the process in which sediments are pressed together under the weight of layers above them.
Condensation is the process by which a gas changes into a liquid.
A constellation is a pattern of stars in the sky.
Continental drift is the hypothesis that the continents slowly move across Earth's surface.
The core is Earth's center, made mostly of iron and nickel.
A crater is a round pit on the surface of a moon or planet made by the impact of a meteorite, or the opening at the top of a volcano.
The crust is the rocky outer layer of Earth.
A crystal is a solid in which the atoms are arranged in a repeating pattern.
A delta is a landform made of sediment where a river enters a lake or ocean.
Deposition is the process in which sediment is laid down in new locations.
Elevation is the height of land above sea level.
The epicenter is the point on Earth's surface directly above the focus of an earthquake.
An equinox is a day when day and night are equal in length, in spring and fall.
Erosion is the process by which water, ice, wind or gravity moves weathered rock and soil.
Evaporation is the process by which a liquid changes into a gas.
A fault is a break in Earth's crust where rocks have moved.
A floodplain is the flat area along a river that is covered by water during floods.
A fossil is the preserved remains or traces of a living thing from long ago.
A front is the boundary where two different air masses meet.
A galaxy is a huge system of stars, gas and dust held together by gravity.
A geologist is a scientist who studies Earth, rocks and minerals.
A glacier is a large mass of moving ice on land.
Groundwater is water that fills the cracks and spaces in underground soil and rock.
A gully is a channel cut into the ground by running water.
Humidity is the amount of water vapor in the air.
Humus is dark brown organic material in soil.
An igneous rock forms from the cooling of magma or lava.
Infiltration is the process of water soaking into the ground.
Latitude is the distance north or south of the equator measured in degrees.
Longitude is the distance east or west of the prime meridian measured in degrees.
Lava is magma that reaches Earth's surface.
A light year is the distance light travels in one year, used to measure distances to stars.
Magma is melted rock beneath Earth's surface.
The mantle is the thick layer of hot rock between Earth's crust and core.
A meteor is a streak of light produced when a meteoroid burns up in the atmosphere.
A meteorite is a meteoroid that hits Earth's surface.
A meteoroid is a chunk of rock or dust in space.
Meteorology is the study of weather.
A mineral is a naturally occurring solid with a crystal structure and definite chemical makeup.
A moraine is a ridge of sediment deposited by a glacier.
A nebula is a large cloud of gas and dust in space where stars are born.
An orbit is the path of an object as it revolves around another object in space.
Ozone is a form of oxygen with three oxygen atoms; the ozone layer absorbs ultraviolet radiation.
Pangaea was a supercontinent made of all the landmasses about 250 million years ago.
A phase of the Moon is the shape of the lighted part of the Moon seen from Earth.
A planet is a large body that orbits a star and does not produce its own light.
A plate is a large section of Earth's lithosphere that moves.
Precipitation is any form of water that falls from clouds, such as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
Relative age is the age of a rock compared with the ages of other rocks.
Revolution is the movement of one object around another; one revolution of Earth around the Sun is one year.
The rock cycle is the series of processes that change rocks from one type to another.
Rotation is the spinning of a planet or moon on its axis; one rotation of Earth is one day.
Runoff is water that flows over the ground surface rather than soaking in.
A satellite is an object that orbits a planet; the Moon is Earth's natural satellite.
Sediment is small solid pieces of material from rocks or organisms.
A sedimentary rock forms when sediments are compacted and cemented together.
A seismic wave is a vibration that travels through Earth carrying the energy released by an earthquake.
The solar system is the Sun and all the planets and other bodies that travel around it.
A solstice is the day of the year with the longest or shortest period of daylight.
A star is a ball of hot gas that produces its own light and heat.
Subduction is the process by which oceanic crust sinks beneath another plate into the mantle.
A telescope is an instrument that makes distant objects look closer and larger.
A tide is the daily rise and fall of Earth's ocean waters.
Topography is the shape of the land, including its elevation and landforms.
A trench is a deep canyon on the ocean floor.
A tsunami is a giant wave usually caused by an earthquake beneath the ocean floor.
The universe is all of space and everything in it.
A volcano is a weak spot in the crust where magma comes to the surface.
Water vapor is water in the form of a gas.
A watershed is the land area that supplies water to a river system.
Weathering is the breaking down of rock into smaller pieces by water, wind, ice, plants or chemicals.
Weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular time and place.
# glossary: life science
An adaptation is a trait that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment.
An allele is one of the different forms of a gene.
An amphibian is a cold blooded vertebrate that lives in water when young and on land as an adult.
Anatomy is the study of the structure of living things.
An antibiotic is a medicine that kills bacteria.
An antibody is a protein made by white blood cells that fights a specific germ.
Bacteria are single celled organisms without a nucleus.
Biodiversity is the number and variety of different species in an area.
Biology is the study of living things.
A biome is a large area with a particular climate and community of organisms.
A botanist is a scientist who studies plants.
Camouflage is coloring or shape that helps an organism blend into its surroundings.
A carnivore is an animal that eats only other animals.
The carrying capacity is the largest population that an area can support.
A cell is the smallest unit of a living thing that can carry out all life processes.
Chlorophyll is a green pigment in plants that captures light energy for photosynthesis.
A chromosome is a threadlike structure in the nucleus made of DNA that carries genes.
A community is all the different populations living together in an area.
Competition is the struggle between organisms for the same limited resources.
A consumer is an organism that obtains energy by eating other organisms.
A decomposer is an organism that breaks down dead organisms and wastes and returns nutrients to the soil.
DNA is the genetic material that carries information about an organism and is passed from parent to offspring.
A dominant trait is a trait that appears when at least one dominant allele is present.
An ecologist is a scientist who studies how organisms interact with each other and their environment.
Ecology is the study of how living things interact with each other and with their environment.
An ecosystem is a community of organisms and their nonliving environment.
An embryo is an organism in its earliest stage of development.
Endangered species are species in danger of extinction.
Evolution is the gradual change in a species over time.
Extinction is the disappearance of all members of a species from Earth.
Fertilization is the joining of a sperm cell and an egg cell.
A food chain is the path of energy as one organism eats another.
A food web is a network of overlapping food chains in an ecosystem.
A gene is a segment of DNA that controls a trait.
Genetics is the scientific study of heredity.
Germination is the sprouting of a seed.
A habitat is the place where an organism lives and gets what it needs.
A herbivore is an animal that eats only plants.
Heredity is the passing of traits from parents to offspring.
Hibernation is a state of greatly reduced activity during winter.
A host is an organism that a parasite lives on or in.
An instinct is a behavior an animal is born with and does not need to learn.
An invertebrate is an animal without a backbone.
A larva is the immature form of an insect that looks very different from the adult.
A life cycle is the stages of growth and development an organism goes through.
A mammal is a warm blooded vertebrate with hair that feeds its young milk.
Metamorphosis is a change in body form during the life cycle of an animal, such as a caterpillar becoming a butterfly.
A microorganism is a living thing too small to see without a microscope.
Migration is the regular seasonal movement of animals from one place to another.
Mimicry is when one organism looks like another, usually more dangerous, organism.
A mutation is a change in a gene or chromosome.
Mutualism is a relationship between two species in which both benefit.
Natural selection is the process by which organisms best adapted to their environment survive and reproduce.
A niche is the role of an organism in its ecosystem.
A nutrient is a substance that an organism needs for energy, growth and repair.
Offspring are the new organisms produced by parents.
An omnivore is an animal that eats both plants and animals.
An organ is a group of tissues that work together to do a job.
An organism is a living thing.
A parasite is an organism that lives on or in a host and harms it.
Photosynthesis is the process by which plants use light energy to make food from carbon dioxide and water.
Pollen is a fine powder made by the male parts of flowers that contains sperm cells.
Pollination is the transfer of pollen from a stamen to a pistil.
A population is a group of organisms of the same species living in an area.
A predator is an animal that hunts other animals for food.
Prey is an animal that is hunted by a predator.
A producer is an organism that makes its own food.
A Punnett square is a chart that shows the possible gene combinations from a cross.
A recessive trait is a trait that is hidden when a dominant allele is present.
Reproduction is the process by which organisms produce offspring.
A reptile is a cold blooded vertebrate with dry scaly skin that lays eggs on land.
Respiration is the process by which cells break down food to release energy.
A scavenger is an animal that feeds on the bodies of dead organisms.
A species is a group of organisms that can mate and produce fertile offspring.
A spore is a reproductive cell that can grow into a new organism, made by ferns, mosses and fungi.
A symbiosis is a close relationship between two species that live together.
A tissue is a group of similar cells that perform the same function.
A trait is a characteristic of an organism.
Transpiration is the loss of water through the leaves of a plant.
A vertebrate is an animal with a backbone.
A virus is a tiny nonliving particle that invades and reproduces inside a living cell.
A zoologist is a scientist who studies animals.
# glossary additions
Aerobic respiration uses oxygen to release energy from food; anaerobic respiration and fermentation release energy without oxygen.
Biodegradable materials such as paper, food scraps and wood can be broken down by decomposers; most plastics are not biodegradable.
Botany is the study of plants.
Caffeine is a stimulant found in coffee, tea and soda that speeds up the nervous system.
A hybrid is the offspring of two different varieties or species, such as a mule from a horse and a donkey; mules are sterile.
Plankton are tiny organisms that drift in water; phytoplankton are plant like producers and zooplankton are tiny animals.
A zygote is the single cell formed when a sperm fertilizes an egg.
The aorta is the largest artery, carrying blood from the left ventricle of the heart to the body.
The atria are the upper chambers of the heart that receive blood; the ventricles are lower chambers that pump blood out.
A neuron has dendrites that receive messages, a cell body, and an axon that sends messages to the next cell.
The biosphere is the part of Earth where life exists; the hydrosphere is all of Earth's water; the lithosphere is the rocky outer layer; the atmosphere is the air.
Bivalves such as clams, oysters, mussels and scallops are mollusks with two shells.
A bog is a wetland with acidic water and peat moss.
The cochlea in the inner ear changes sound vibrations into nerve signals.
The cornea is the clear front covering of the eye that helps focus light.
Cows chew their cud, partly digested food brought back up from the stomach, and have stomachs with four parts.
The dermis is the inner layer of skin with blood vessels, nerves and sweat glands; the epidermis is the outer layer.
Electrolytes are minerals such as sodium and potassium dissolved in body fluids.
The larynx, or voice box, contains the vocal cords.
Nephrons are tiny filtering units in the kidneys.
An oasis is a place in a desert where groundwater reaches the surface.
A photon is a tiny packet of light energy.
Porous materials such as sandstone, sponges and soil have many small holes that hold water or air.
A rhizome is an underground stem that grows sideways and produces new plants.
A toxin is a poisonous substance produced by a living thing.
A tributary is a smaller stream or river that flows into a larger river.
The umbilical cord connects a developing fetus to its mother and carries nutrients and oxygen.
Incisors are front teeth for cutting, canines are pointed teeth for tearing, and molars are flat teeth for grinding.
Chitin is the tough material that makes up the exoskeleton of insects and crustaceans.
Geckos can climb walls because of tiny hairs on their feet.
