# everyday observations and their explanations: water and heat
The water level in an open glass or fish tank goes down over several days because the water evaporates into the air.
A puddle on the sidewalk disappears on a sunny day because the water evaporates.
Wet paint, wet hair and wet laundry dry because water evaporates.
Clothes hung outside dry faster on a hot, windy, sunny day than on a cool, still, cloudy day.
A covered container of water loses less water than an open container because the cover traps the water vapor.
Water droplets on the inside of a covered jar or terrarium come from water vapor that condensed.
The outside of a glass of ice water becomes wet because water vapor in the air condenses on the cold glass, not because water leaks through.
A cold drink can sweats on a humid day because water vapor from the surrounding air condenses on its cold surface.
Morning dew on grass forms when water vapor condenses as the air cools overnight.
Breath makes a cloud on a cold day because water vapor in the breath condenses into tiny droplets.
Steam from a hot shower condenses on the cool bathroom mirror.
Frost forms on windows on cold nights when water vapor freezes directly onto the cold glass.
Ice cubes left out of the freezer melt because they absorb heat from the warmer air.
Ice on a pond melts in spring because it gains heat energy from the Sun and warmer air.
A pond freezes in winter because the water loses heat energy to the cold air.
Snow melts faster on dark pavement than on white snow covered grass because dark surfaces absorb more sunlight.
A glass bottle full of water can crack in the freezer because water expands as it freezes.
Pipes can burst in winter when the water inside freezes and expands.
Water poured on a hot pan sizzles and quickly turns to steam.
A pot of water boiling on a stove gets smaller over time as the water turns into water vapor.
Soup cools faster in a wide shallow bowl than in a deep narrow cup because more surface area is exposed to the air.
A hot drink cools down because heat moves from the hot drink into the cooler surrounding air until they are the same temperature.
A cold drink warms up because heat moves from the warmer surrounding air into the drink.
Hot objects always lose heat to cooler surroundings until they reach the same temperature.
Heat energy is never destroyed; it moves to the surroundings.
Your hand feels warm near a fire because of radiation; it feels hot touching a hot pan because of conduction.
Warm air rising from a heater circulates around a room by convection.
The upstairs of a house is often warmer than the basement because warm air rises.
A campfire warms people sitting around it mainly by radiation.
A sidewalk feels hotter than grass on a sunny day because concrete absorbs heat and plants use water to cool.
Sand at the beach gets hotter than the water during the day because sand heats faster; at night the sand cools faster.
The temperature drops after sunset because the ground and air lose heat when they no longer receive energy from the Sun.
It is coldest just before sunrise because Earth has been losing heat all night.
Cloudy nights stay warmer because clouds trap heat radiated by the ground.
A car parked in the sun gets very hot inside because sunlight enters through the windows and the heat is trapped, like a greenhouse.
A greenhouse stays warm because glass lets sunlight in and traps the heat inside.
Rubbing your hands together makes them warm because friction changes motion energy into heat.
Sliding down a rope or slide can make your hands or legs warm because of friction.
Car tires get warm after driving because of friction with the road.
Bending a paper clip back and forth makes it warm.
A light bulb gets hot because some electrical energy changes into heat instead of light.
A computer or phone gets warm when used because electrical energy changes into heat.
# everyday observations: plants and animals
Plants kept in a dark closet turn pale and yellow and grow weak because they lack light for photosynthesis and making chlorophyll.
A plant left without water wilts, and its leaves droop.
A plant placed by a window bends its stem toward the light.
Plants grown in good soil with fertilizer usually grow taller than plants in sand.
Plants that get the right amount of sunlight, water and nutrients grow best.
Seeds planted too deep may not sprout because they run out of stored food before reaching light.
Leaves of a plant covered with petroleum jelly die because the stomata are blocked and gases cannot enter or leave.
Mold grows faster on bread kept in a warm, dark, damp place than on bread kept in the refrigerator.
Fruit left on the counter ripens and then rots as bacteria and fungi decompose it.
A pumpkin left outside slowly rots because decomposers break it down.
Dead leaves on the forest floor are broken down by decomposers such as fungi, bacteria and earthworms.
Birds build nests in the spring to lay eggs and raise their young.
Many trees produce flowers in the spring and fruits and seeds in the summer and fall.
Squirrels gather and store acorns in the fall to eat during winter.
Dogs pant to cool off because they cannot sweat much.
A cat's fur stands up and its back arches when it is frightened, to look bigger.
Earthworms come to the surface after a heavy rain because their tunnels fill with water.
Moths fly toward lights at night.
Ants follow each other in a line by following a chemical trail.
A caterpillar eats a lot of leaves to store energy before it becomes a pupa.
Tadpoles lose their tails and grow legs as they change into frogs.
Frogs lay many eggs because most eggs and tadpoles are eaten or die before becoming adults.
Animals that eat the same food in the same place compete for that food.
When there is not enough rain, plants grow less, and the animals that eat them have less food.
After a drought, the populations of many animals decrease because of less food and water.
When a new road or building is built in a forest, animals lose their habitat and must move or may die.
When a predator population increases, the prey population decreases; then the predator population decreases from lack of food.
Removing a top predator such as wolves can cause herbivores such as deer and elk to increase and overeat plants.
When a dam is built on a river, the area upstream floods and becomes a lake, changing the habitat.
Beavers cut down trees to build dams, which create ponds.
Sea turtles that eat plastic bags mistake them for jellyfish.
# everyday observations: earth and sky
The Sun appears to rise in the east in the morning, be highest in the southern sky at noon in the Northern Hemisphere, and set in the west in the evening.
Shadows outdoors change in length and direction during the day because the position of the Sun in the sky changes as Earth rotates.
At noon, when the Sun is highest in the sky, shadows are shortest.
A shadow points away from the Sun.
The Moon can sometimes be seen during the daytime.
The Moon rises about 50 minutes later each day.
The stars seen at night change with the seasons because Earth revolves around the Sun.
The North Star appears not to move while other stars circle around it during the night.
Summer days are longer than winter days in New York and other places far from the equator.
Places near the equator have about 12 hours of daylight all year.
During summer the Sun's rays hit the Northern Hemisphere more directly, so it is warmer.
It is warmer at noon than in the morning because the Sun's rays are more direct.
Rocks at the bottom of a stream are smooth and rounded from tumbling in moving water.
Rocks on a mountain top are often sharp and jagged.
A sidewalk cracks over many winters as water freezes and thaws in its cracks.
The roots of a tree growing near a sidewalk can crack and lift the concrete.
After a heavy rain, rivers are muddy because they carry eroded soil.
Soil washes away from bare hillsides more than from hillsides covered by grass and trees.
Sand dunes change shape over time because the wind moves the sand.
A beach becomes smaller after storms because the waves carry sand away.
Tall waves during a storm erode beaches and cliffs.
Old mountains are rounded and short because they have been worn down by erosion.
Layers of rock seen in a road cut or canyon wall formed one on top of another over a long time.
A rock that fizzes in vinegar contains calcite, like limestone or marble.
A rock with holes that floats in water is pumice, formed from lava with gas bubbles.
A rock with visible large crystals cooled slowly underground, like granite.
A rock with layers and fossils is most likely sedimentary, like shale, sandstone or limestone.
A rock with bands or stripes of minerals formed by heat and pressure is metamorphic, like gneiss.
# everyday observations: forces, sound and light
A ball rolls farther on a smooth floor than on a rough carpet or grass because there is less friction.
A sled slides farther on ice than on dry ground because ice has less friction.
A heavy box is harder to push than a light box because it has more mass and more friction.
A ball kicked harder goes farther and faster because a greater force was applied.
A ball thrown up in the air comes back down because gravity pulls it toward Earth.
A ball rolling down a ramp speeds up because gravity pulls it down the slope.
A steeper ramp makes a ball roll faster.
Riding a bicycle uphill is harder than riding on flat ground because you must work against gravity.
When a bus stops suddenly, passengers lurch forward because of inertia.
When you pull a tablecloth quickly, the dishes stay in place because of inertia.
Pushing off a wall in a swimming pool moves you away from the wall.
Two students pushing a box with equal force from opposite sides do not move it because the forces are balanced.
A paper airplane eventually falls because of gravity and air resistance.
A parachute slows a falling person because it increases air resistance.
A crumpled sheet of paper falls faster than a flat sheet because it has less air resistance.
A magnet picks up paper clips and nails but not pennies, aluminum cans, plastic or wood.
A refrigerator magnet sticks to the door because the door contains steel, which contains iron.
A compass needle near a magnet moves because of the magnet's magnetic field.
A balloon rubbed on hair sticks to a wall because of static electricity.
A shock from a doorknob after walking on carpet is caused by static electricity.
You see lightning before you hear thunder because light travels faster than sound.
The farther away a lightning strike is, the longer the time between the flash and the thunder.
A guitar string makes a sound because it vibrates.
Tapping a glass with more water in it gives a lower pitch; blowing over a bottle with more water gives a higher pitch.
A sound gets quieter as you move away from its source.
An echo is heard in a large empty room or canyon because sound reflects off the walls.
A room with carpets and curtains is quieter because soft materials absorb sound.
You can see yourself in a mirror or a calm lake because they reflect light.
A spoon in a glass of water looks bent because light refracts at the water surface.
A rainbow appears when sunlight shines through raindrops.
A red shirt looks red because it reflects red light and absorbs the other colors.
White clothes are cooler in summer because they reflect sunlight; black clothes absorb sunlight and get hotter.
You can see through a clean window because glass is transparent; you cannot see through a wall because it is opaque.
A flashlight beam travels in a straight line.
Shadows form because light travels in straight lines and cannot bend around opaque objects.
A pencil shadow is longer when the flashlight is held low at an angle and shorter when it is held directly above.
# everyday observations: matter and changes
Sugar seems to disappear when stirred into water because it dissolves, but the water tastes sweet, so the sugar is still there.
Salt dissolves in water but sand settles to the bottom.
Oil and vinegar salad dressing separates into layers because oil is less dense and does not mix with water.
A piece of wood floats and a rock sinks because the rock is denser than water.
A steel nail sinks but a steel ship floats because of the ship's shape.
An egg floats in very salty water but sinks in fresh water because salt water is denser.
A helium balloon floats up because helium is less dense than air.
A balloon gets bigger when warmed and smaller when cooled.
A ball is harder to squeeze when it is pumped with more air because gas particles push on the inside.
Bread rises because yeast produce carbon dioxide gas.
Baking a cake is a chemical change because new substances form and it cannot be changed back.
Cooking an egg is a chemical change.
Melting chocolate is a physical change because it can be cooled and become solid again.
A nail left outside in the rain rusts.
A bicycle left outside in the rain becomes rusty because the iron reacts with water and oxygen.
A sliced apple turns brown when exposed to air.
A candle gets shorter as it burns because the wax is used as fuel.
A burning log turns into ash, smoke and gases; the total mass of all products equals the mass of the log and oxygen used.
Mixing vinegar and baking soda in a bottle with a balloon on top inflates the balloon with carbon dioxide gas.
Fizzing when an antacid tablet is dropped in water shows a gas is produced.
