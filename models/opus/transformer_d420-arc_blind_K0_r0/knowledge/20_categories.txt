# category membership and classification facts
sources of light produce their own light: Sun, stars, fire, flame, candle, light bulb, lamp, lightning, fireflies, glowing coals ~ objects that only reflect light: Moon, mirror, planets, aluminum foil
living things: plants, animals, fungi, bacteria, trees, people, insects, mushrooms ~ nonliving things: rocks, water, air, sunlight, metal, plastic, glass, soil minerals
solids at room temperature: rock, ice, wood, metal, iron, salt, sugar, sand ~ liquids at room temperature: water, milk, juice, oil, mercury, alcohol, vinegar ~ gases at room temperature: oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide, helium, hydrogen, air, steam
simple machines examples: seesaw lever, ramp inclined plane, axe wedge, jar lid screw, flagpole pulley, doorknob wheel and axle
parts of the skeletal system: skull, ribs, spine, femur, bones, joints ~ parts of the muscular system: biceps, triceps, heart muscle, tendons ~ parts of the nervous system: brain, spinal cord, nerves, neurons
parts of the circulatory system: heart, arteries, veins, capillaries, blood ~ parts of the respiratory system: lungs, trachea, bronchi, diaphragm, nose ~ parts of the digestive system: mouth, esophagus, stomach, intestines, liver
parts of the excretory system: kidneys, bladder, urethra, skin sweat glands, lungs
vertebrate examples: fish, frogs, snakes, birds, dogs, humans, whales ~ invertebrate examples: worms, insects, spiders, snails, jellyfish, crabs, octopus, starfish
mammal examples: bat, whale, dolphin, dog, human, elephant, mouse, cow ~ bird examples: penguin, ostrich, eagle, owl, robin, chicken ~ reptile examples: snake, lizard, turtle, crocodile ~ amphibian examples: frog, toad, salamander ~ fish examples: shark, salmon, tuna, goldfish
insect examples: ant, bee, butterfly, beetle, fly, grasshopper, mosquito, ladybug ~ not insects: spider, tick, scorpion eight legs arachnids, centipede, worm
producers examples: grass, trees, algae, phytoplankton, flowers, moss ~ consumers examples: rabbit, deer, lion, hawk, human, frog ~ decomposers examples: mushroom, mold, bacteria, earthworm
renewable examples: sun, wind, water, trees, crops ~ nonrenewable examples: coal, oil, natural gas, uranium
igneous examples: granite, basalt, obsidian, pumice ~ sedimentary examples: sandstone, limestone, shale, conglomerate ~ metamorphic examples: marble, slate, gneiss, quartzite
conductors examples: copper, silver, aluminum, iron, gold, steel, water ~ insulators examples: rubber, plastic, wood, glass, cloth, air, styrofoam
magnetic materials: iron, steel, nickel, cobalt ~ nonmagnetic materials: copper, aluminum, gold, silver, wood, plastic, glass, paper
physical changes examples: melting ice, boiling water, cutting paper, breaking glass, dissolving sugar, crushing a can ~ chemical changes examples: burning wood, rusting iron, cooking food, baking bread, rotting fruit
inherited traits examples: eye color, hair color, height, freckles, dimples, flower color ~ learned behaviors examples: reading, riding a bike, playing piano, speaking a language, tricks
weather instruments: thermometer temperature, barometer air pressure, anemometer wind speed, wind vane direction, rain gauge rainfall, hygrometer humidity
tools for length: ruler, meter stick, tape measure ~ tools for mass: balance, scale ~ tools for volume: graduated cylinder, beaker, measuring cup ~ tools for time: stopwatch, clock ~ tools for temperature: thermometer
units of length: millimeter, centimeter, meter, kilometer, inch, foot, mile ~ units of mass: gram, kilogram, milligram ~ units of volume: milliliter, liter ~ units of time: second, minute, hour ~ units of temperature: Celsius, Fahrenheit
forms of energy examples: moving car kinetic, battery chemical, food chemical, stretched rubber band elastic potential, book on shelf gravitational potential, lamp light, music sound, stove heat thermal, wall outlet electrical
heat transfer examples: spoon in soup conduction, boiling water convection, sunlight warming radiation, campfire radiation, hot air balloon convection, ironing clothes conduction
weathering examples: ice wedging, roots cracking rocks, acid rain, rusting rocks ~ erosion examples: river carrying sand, wind blowing soil, glacier moving rocks, waves washing beach
fast changes to Earth's surface: earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, landslides, floods ~ slow changes: weathering, erosion, deposition, glaciers, mountain building
natural disasters: earthquakes, volcanoes, hurricanes, tornadoes, floods, droughts, wildfires, tsunamis, blizzards
cloud types: cumulus, stratus, cirrus, cumulonimbus, nimbostratus
precipitation types: rain, snow, sleet, hail, freezing rain
states of matter changes: melting, freezing, evaporation, condensation, boiling, sublimation
inner planets: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars ~ outer planets: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune
objects in the solar system: Sun, planets, moons, asteroids, comets, meteoroids, dwarf planets
human senses and organs: eyes sight, ears hearing, nose smell, tongue taste, skin touch
plant parts and jobs: roots absorb water anchor, stem supports transports, leaves make food, flowers reproduce, seeds grow new plants, fruit protects seeds
cell parts and jobs: nucleus control, membrane controls entry, wall support, chloroplast photosynthesis, mitochondria energy, vacuole storage, cytoplasm fluid
blood parts and jobs: red cells oxygen, white cells infection, platelets clotting, plasma carries
nutrients and jobs: carbohydrates energy, proteins growth repair, fats stored energy, vitamins minerals health, water transport
life cycle order of a butterfly: egg larva pupa adult ~ life cycle order of a frog: egg tadpole froglet adult ~ life cycle of a plant: seed seedling adult flower fruit
moon phase order: new, crescent, quarter, gibbous, full
order of planets: Mercury Venus Earth Mars Jupiter Saturn Uranus Neptune
levels of organization order: cell tissue organ organ system organism
ecological levels: organism population community ecosystem biome biosphere
food chain order: sun producer herbivore carnivore decomposer
scientific method order: question hypothesis experiment data conclusion
water cycle order: evaporation condensation precipitation collection
rock cycle: igneous weathered into sediment becomes sedimentary, heat pressure make metamorphic, melting makes magma
energy pyramid levels: producers bottom most energy, primary consumers, secondary consumers, top predators least energy
symbiosis types: mutualism both benefit, commensalism one benefits other unaffected, parasitism one benefits other harmed
fossil fuels types: coal from plants, oil and natural gas from marine organisms
types of plate boundaries: divergent apart, convergent together, transform sliding
types of seismic waves: P primary, S secondary, surface
layers of the Earth from outside: crust mantle outer core inner core
layers of the atmosphere from ground: troposphere stratosphere mesosphere thermosphere exosphere
types of galaxies: spiral elliptical irregular
types of stars by color: red cool, yellow medium, white hot, blue hottest
types of telescopes: refracting lenses, reflecting mirrors, radio, space
types of muscles: skeletal voluntary, smooth involuntary, cardiac heart
types of joints: hinge knee elbow, ball and socket shoulder hip, pivot neck, gliding wrist
types of teeth: incisors cut, canines tear, molars grind
types of blood vessels: arteries away from heart, veins to heart, capillaries exchange
types of reproduction: sexual two parents, asexual one parent budding fission
types of consumers: herbivore plants, carnivore meat, omnivore both, scavenger dead, decomposer breaks down
kingdoms of life: animals, plants, fungi, protists, bacteria
classes of vertebrates: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals
