# agriculture food technology
farmers rotate crops to keep soil fertile crop rotation legumes add nitrogen
irrigation brings water to crops in dry areas
pesticides kill insects that eat crops ~ herbicides kill weeds
organic farming uses no synthetic pesticides or fertilizers
greenhouses let farmers grow plants in winter
genetically modified crops resist insects drought
selective breeding produced crops with bigger fruit more yield
farmers use tractors plows to prepare soil
overgrazing by cattle removes grass causes erosion desertification
terracing hillsides reduces erosion on farms
windbreaks rows of trees reduce wind erosion on farms
contour plowing across slopes reduces runoff erosion
no-till farming leaves plant residue protects soil
cover crops planted in winter hold soil
compost adds organic matter nutrients to garden soil
food is preserved by refrigeration freezing canning drying salting smoking pickling
pasteurization heats milk to kill harmful bacteria
cooking meat thoroughly kills bacteria prevents food poisoning
washing fruits vegetables removes dirt germs pesticides
# human development and health
human life cycle stages: infant baby, child, adolescent teenager, adult, elderly
babies grow quickly and depend on parents for care
puberty adolescence changes caused by hormones
humans have a long childhood learning from parents
heredity and environment both affect human growth and health
a healthy lifestyle: balanced diet, exercise, enough sleep, no smoking, avoiding drugs and alcohol
eating too much sugar fat salt can cause obesity heart disease diabetes tooth decay
brushing teeth and flossing prevent cavities tooth decay bacteria plaque
fluoride strengthens teeth enamel
vitamins come from fruits vegetables milk
calcium for strong bones teeth from milk cheese yogurt
protein for growth muscles from meat fish eggs beans nuts
carbohydrates for energy from bread rice pasta cereal potatoes
water is needed for all body functions
germs enter the body through cuts mouth nose eyes
covering coughs and sneezes prevents spreading germs
vaccinations shots prevent diseases measles polio flu
mosquitoes spread malaria and West Nile virus ~ ticks spread Lyme disease ~ fleas spread plague
contaminated water spreads cholera typhoid boil water to purify
the flu and common cold are caused by viruses spread through the air coughing
antibiotics treat bacterial infections not viral infections
doctors use x-rays to see broken bones
a stethoscope listens to heart and lungs
blood pressure measured with a cuff
a thermometer checks body temperature for fever
microscopes help doctors identify bacteria and blood cells
first aid: clean wounds, apply pressure to stop bleeding, cool burns with water
helmets protect the head brain from injury
seat belts and car seats protect passengers in crashes
sunglasses protect eyes from ultraviolet light
ear plugs protect hearing from loud noise
# weather safety and natural hazards
during a thunderstorm stay indoors away from water and tall objects avoid open fields
during a tornado go to a basement or interior room without windows
during a hurricane evacuate coastal areas board up windows
during an earthquake drop cover and hold on under a table away from windows
during a flood move to higher ground do not walk or drive through flood water
wildfires spread quickly in dry windy weather
smoke detectors warn of fires
weather forecasts and warnings help people prepare for storms
satellite images show cloud patterns and hurricanes
Doppler radar shows precipitation and storm movement
weather maps show fronts high and low pressure systems isobars temperatures
on weather maps H means high pressure fair weather ~ L means low pressure stormy weather
isobars lines of equal air pressure close together mean strong winds
a blue line with triangles shows a cold front ~ a red line with half circles shows a warm front
humidity is high before rain the air feels muggy
cumulus clouds that grow taller in the afternoon may become thunderstorms
a ring halo around the Moon or Sun often means rain is coming high cirrostratus clouds
red sky at night fair weather tomorrow
dew forms on clear calm nights
frost forms when the temperature is below freezing
snow falls when the air temperature is below freezing all the way to the ground
sleet forms when rain falls through a layer of freezing air
hail forms when updrafts carry raindrops up into freezing air repeatedly layers of ice
the temperature drops after sunset because the ground loses heat by radiation
clouds at night keep temperatures warmer by trapping heat
deserts get very cold at night because dry air and clear skies let heat escape
coastal cities have milder winters and cooler summers than inland cities
mountains can block moist air causing heavy rain on one side and desert on the other
cities are warmer than the countryside urban heat island pavement buildings
seasons in the tropics are wet and dry rather than hot and cold
monsoons seasonal winds bring heavy rain
# ocean and water
ocean water is salty because rivers carry dissolved minerals salts to the sea and evaporation leaves salt behind
the deep ocean is cold dark and has high pressure
sunlight reaches only the upper layer of the ocean where most life and photosynthesis occur
upwelling brings cold nutrient-rich water to the surface good fishing
the Gulf Stream carries warm water from the Gulf of Mexico north along the east coast warms Europe
waves are caused by wind blowing over the water surface
the energy of a wave moves but the water mostly moves up and down in place
a floating object bobs up and down as waves pass
tides are highest when the Sun Moon and Earth are in a line
the Moon's gravity pulls ocean water creating a bulge high tide on the side facing the Moon and the opposite side
the time between high tides is about 12 hours 25 minutes
fresh water sources: rivers lakes groundwater glaciers ice caps reservoirs
only a small percent of Earth's water is fresh liquid water available for people
water conservation is important because fresh water is limited
water can be purified by filtering boiling distillation chlorine
distillation evaporates water and condenses the vapor leaving salt and impurities behind
reverse osmosis desalination makes ocean water drinkable
a water filter traps particles
the water cycle continuously recycles Earth's water
the amount of water on Earth stays about the same over time
water moves through the water cycle by evaporation condensation precipitation runoff infiltration
the Sun and gravity drive the water cycle
water evaporates from oceans lakes rivers soil and plant leaves
most evaporation comes from the oceans
clouds are made of tiny water droplets or ice crystals not water vapor
precipitation returns water from the atmosphere to Earth's surface
rainwater soaks into soil or runs off into streams
water stored underground in aquifers can be reached by wells
# graphs measurement numbers
a line graph is best for showing how something changes over time such as temperature during a day or plant growth over weeks
a bar graph is best for comparing amounts in different groups or categories
a pie chart circle graph shows percentages parts of a whole
a data table organizes measurements
the independent variable is graphed on the x-axis ~ the dependent variable on the y-axis
the mean average is the sum divided by the number of values
the range is the difference between the highest and lowest values
a trend is a pattern in data increasing or decreasing
an outlier is a data point far from the others possible error
units must be included with measurements
the metric system is used by scientists worldwide based on tens
temperature in Celsius: water freezes at 0 boils at 100 ~ in Fahrenheit: water freezes at 32 boils at 212
room temperature is about 20 to 25 degrees Celsius ~ normal body temperature about 37 degrees Celsius
a kilogram is about the mass of a liter of water
a meter is about the height of a doorknob
a centimeter is about the width of a fingernail
a milliliter is a small volume a few drops
a gram is about the mass of a paper clip
a newton is a unit of force weight ~ a joule is a unit of energy work ~ a watt is a unit of power
volts measure electric potential ~ amperes measure current ~ ohms measure resistance
hertz measure frequency ~ decibels measure loudness
light-years measure distance in space ~ kilometers measure distance on Earth
grams per cubic centimeter measure density
meters per second measure speed
millimeters of rainfall
degrees measure angles and temperature
hours days years measure time
