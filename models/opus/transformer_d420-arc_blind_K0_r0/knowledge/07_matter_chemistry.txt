# matter, properties, states, changes
matter is anything that has mass and takes up space volume
mass is the amount of matter in an object measured in grams kilograms with a balance ~ weight is the force of gravity on an object measured in newtons with a spring scale
mass stays the same everywhere ~ weight changes with gravity on the Moon other planets
volume is the amount of space an object takes up measured in liters milliliters cubic centimeters graduated cylinder
volume of an irregular object rock found by water displacement in a graduated cylinder
volume of a rectangular box length times width times height
density is mass divided by volume how tightly packed the matter is grams per cubic centimeter
objects less dense than water float ~ objects more dense than water sink
density of water is 1 gram per milliliter cubic centimeter
oil floats on water because oil is less dense
ice floats on water because ice is less dense than liquid water water expands when it freezes
cutting an object in half does not change its density
physical properties: color, shape, size, mass, volume, density, texture, hardness, odor, melting point, boiling point, state, magnetism, conductivity, solubility
chemical properties describe how a substance reacts: flammability, reactivity with acid oxygen, ability to rust burn tarnish
states phases of matter: solid, liquid, gas, plasma
solids have a definite shape and definite volume particles packed tightly vibrate in place ~ liquids have definite volume but no definite shape take the shape of their container particles slide past each other ~ gases have no definite shape or volume fill their container particles move fast far apart
gas particles move fastest and are farthest apart ~ solid particles move slowest and are closest together
gases can be compressed squeezed ~ solids and liquids are hard to compress
plasma very hot ionized gas stars lightning neon signs
temperature measures the average kinetic energy motion of particles
heating makes particles move faster and spread apart ~ cooling makes particles move slower and closer together
phase changes changes of state: melting, freezing, evaporation, boiling, condensation, sublimation, deposition
melting solid changes to liquid when heated ice melts at 0 degrees Celsius 32 Fahrenheit ~ freezing liquid changes to solid when cooled water freezes at 0 degrees Celsius
boiling liquid changes to gas rapidly at the boiling point water boils at 100 degrees Celsius 212 Fahrenheit bubbles
evaporation liquid changes to gas at the surface faster with heat wind larger surface area
condensation gas changes to liquid when cooled water vapor condenses
sublimation solid changes directly to gas dry ice carbon dioxide ~ deposition gas changes directly to solid frost
temperature stays the same during a phase change melting boiling energy breaks bonds
adding heat energy causes melting and evaporation ~ removing heat energy causes freezing and condensation
melting point and boiling point are physical properties that identify a substance
salt lowers the freezing point of water melts ice on roads
physical change changes size shape or state but no new substance forms: melting, freezing, boiling, evaporating, cutting, tearing, folding, crushing, grinding, bending, breaking glass, dissolving sugar or salt in water, mixing sand and water ~ chemical change forms a new substance with different properties: burning, rusting, cooking, baking a cake, frying an egg, rotting, decaying, digesting food, souring milk, tarnishing silver, explosion, fireworks, photosynthesis, respiration
signs of a chemical change chemical reaction: gas bubbles form, color change, temperature change heat or light given off, precipitate solid forms, odor smell change, new substance
iron rusts when it reacts with oxygen and water forming iron oxide chemical change
burning combustion fuel reacts with oxygen releases heat light carbon dioxide water ash smoke
fire needs fuel oxygen and heat fire triangle
baking soda and vinegar react produce carbon dioxide gas bubbles chemical reaction
elements pure substances made of only one kind of atom cannot be broken down: hydrogen, oxygen, carbon, nitrogen, iron, gold, copper, helium, sodium
compounds two or more elements chemically combined in fixed ratio: water H2O, carbon dioxide CO2, salt sodium chloride NaCl, sugar glucose
water is a compound of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom H2O
carbon dioxide one carbon atom two oxygen atoms CO2
table salt sodium chloride NaCl sodium and chlorine
compounds have different properties from the elements that form them sodium chlorine salt
molecules two or more atoms bonded together
mixtures substances physically combined each keeps its own properties can be separated by physical means: salad, trail mix, sand and water, salt water, air, soil
mixtures can be separated by filtering filtration, evaporation, magnets, sieves screens, sorting by hand, distillation, decanting
filter separates undissolved solids like sand from liquids water
evaporation separates dissolved salt from water salt is left behind
a magnet separates iron filings from sand mixture
solution mixture where one substance dissolves evenly in another salt water sugar water homogeneous
solute substance that dissolves sugar salt ~ solvent substance that does the dissolving water
water is called the universal solvent dissolves many substances
dissolving faster: stirring, heating warm water, crushing into smaller pieces more surface area
solubility how much solute dissolves; more sugar dissolves in hot water than cold water
saturated solution cannot dissolve any more solute
suspension particles settle out muddy water ~ colloid milk
atoms smallest unit of an element made of protons neutrons electrons
nucleus center of atom contains protons positive charge and neutrons no charge neutral ~ electrons negative charge move around the nucleus in shells energy levels
atomic number number of protons identifies the element
mass number protons plus neutrons
isotopes same element different numbers of neutrons
ions atoms that gain or lose electrons charged
atoms bond: covalent bonds share electrons ~ ionic bonds transfer electrons between atoms metals and nonmetals
oxygen gas O2 two oxygen atoms share electrons covalent double bond ~ hydrogen gas H2
periodic table arranges elements by atomic number in rows periods and columns groups families with similar properties
metals: shiny luster, good conductors of heat and electricity, malleable can be hammered into sheets, ductile drawn into wires, mostly solid at room temperature ~ nonmetals: dull, poor conductors insulators, brittle, many are gases
metals are on the left side of the periodic table ~ nonmetals on the right side
metalloids semimetals silicon semiconductors
noble gases helium neon argon unreactive stable full outer electrons
mercury is a metal that is liquid at room temperature
alkali metals sodium potassium very reactive with water
halogens chlorine fluorine reactive nonmetals
chemical formula shows elements and number of atoms
chemical equation reactants arrow products
conservation of mass: in a chemical reaction matter mass is not created or destroyed atoms are rearranged total mass of reactants equals products
closed container mass stays the same after reaction
endothermic reactions absorb heat feel cold ~ exothermic reactions release heat feel warm burning
catalyst speeds up a reaction enzymes
reaction rate faster with higher temperature, more concentration, smaller particles, stirring
acids sour taste pH less than 7 turn blue litmus red lemon juice vinegar stomach acid ~ bases bitter slippery pH greater than 7 turn red litmus blue soap baking soda ammonia bleach
pH scale 0 to 14 measures acidity 7 is neutral pure water
neutralization acid plus base forms salt and water
indicators litmus paper phenolphthalein cabbage juice change color in acids bases
polymers long chains of molecules plastics rubber proteins
alloys mixtures of metals bronze copper tin, steel iron carbon, brass copper zinc
hydrogen lightest element most abundant in universe
carbon found in all living things organic compounds
oxygen needed for burning and respiration
nitrogen most of the air
helium lighter than air balloons float
natural resources materials from nature: wood from trees, cotton from plants, wool from sheep, leather from animals, metals from ores, glass from sand, plastic from oil petroleum
synthetic materials made by people plastic nylon polyester
paper made from wood pulp trees
glass made from melting sand
plastic made from petroleum oil
