# water cycle, weather, climate, atmosphere, oceans
water cycle: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection runoff, transpiration, infiltration
the Sun's energy drives the water cycle and heats water causing evaporation
evaporation: liquid water heated by the Sun changes into water vapor gas rises into the air ~ condensation: water vapor cools changes into liquid droplets forming clouds fog dew
precipitation: water falls from clouds as rain, snow, sleet, hail
clouds form when water vapor cools and condenses into tiny droplets or ice crystals around dust particles
dew forms when water vapor condenses on cool surfaces grass at night morning
water droplets form on the outside of a cold glass because water vapor in the air condenses on the cool surface
frost forms when water vapor freezes deposits directly as ice on cold surfaces
transpiration plants release water vapor through their leaves stomata into the air
runoff water flows over the ground surface into streams rivers lakes oceans
infiltration water soaks into the ground becomes groundwater aquifer
groundwater stored underground in aquifers in pores of rock soil wells springs
water table top of the groundwater saturated zone
most of Earth's water is salt water in the oceans about 97 percent
fresh water is about 3 percent most frozen in glaciers ice caps
oceans cover about 71 percent three fourths of Earth's surface
watershed drainage basin area of land drains into a river
wetlands marshes swamps filter water reduce flooding habitat
weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a certain place and time day to day ~ climate is the average weather pattern of a region over many years long period
weather factors: temperature, air pressure, humidity, wind speed, wind direction, precipitation, cloud cover
humidity is the amount of water vapor moisture in the air
relative humidity percent of water vapor air holds compared to the maximum
dew point temperature at which water vapor condenses
warm air can hold more water vapor than cold air
air pressure is the weight force of air pressing down atmospheric pressure
barometer measures air pressure
thermometer measures temperature degrees Celsius Fahrenheit
anemometer measures wind speed spinning cups
wind vane weather vane shows wind direction
rain gauge measures amount of rainfall precipitation
hygrometer psychrometer measures humidity
weather balloons satellites radar collect weather data forecasts
meteorologists study and predict forecast weather
falling air pressure usually means clouds storms rain are coming ~ rising high air pressure usually means clear fair sunny dry weather
low pressure systems bring cloudy stormy wet weather ~ high pressure systems bring clear calm dry weather
wind is moving air caused by differences in air pressure and uneven heating of Earth's surface by the Sun
air moves from areas of high pressure to areas of low pressure
sea breeze during the day cool air moves from sea to land because land heats faster ~ land breeze at night air moves from land to sea because land cools faster
land heats up and cools down faster than water ~ water heats up and cools down slowly has high specific heat
large bodies of water oceans moderate the climate of nearby coastal areas milder temperatures
warm air rises because it is less dense ~ cold air sinks because it is more dense
convection currents in the atmosphere move heat warm air rises cool air sinks
air mass large body of air with similar temperature and humidity
continental air masses dry ~ maritime air masses humid moist from oceans
polar air masses cold ~ tropical air masses warm
front boundary between two different air masses
cold front cold air pushes under warm air brings thunderstorms heavy rain then cooler weather cumulonimbus
warm front warm air slides over cold air brings steady light rain drizzle stratus clouds
stationary front air masses do not move several days rain
cloud types: cumulus puffy fair weather, stratus flat gray layers, cirrus thin wispy high ice crystals, cumulonimbus thunderstorms
cumulonimbus tall dark clouds thunderstorms lightning hail
cirrus clouds high thin feathery made of ice crystals
stratus clouds low flat gray sheets cover sky drizzle fog
fog is a cloud at ground level
thunderstorms lightning thunder heavy rain strong winds cumulonimbus
lightning is a discharge of static electricity in clouds ~ thunder is the sound caused by lightning heating air rapidly expanding
we see lightning before hearing thunder because light travels faster than sound
tornado violent rotating spinning column funnel of air touches ground very strong winds
hurricane large tropical storm forms over warm ocean water strong winds heavy rain storm surge eye
hurricanes lose strength over land or cold water because no warm moist air
typhoons cyclones are hurricanes in other oceans
blizzard snowstorm strong winds heavy snow low visibility
drought long period with little or no rain dry crops fail
flood too much water overflows rivers heavy rain
hail balls of ice form in thunderstorms updrafts
sleet frozen raindrops ice pellets ~ snow ice crystals ~ freezing rain
snow forms when water vapor freezes into ice crystals in cold clouds
el nino warm ocean water in Pacific changes weather patterns
jet stream fast high winds move weather systems west to east in the United States
prevailing westerlies winds weather usually moves from west to east across the United States
trade winds blow toward the equator
Coriolis effect Earth's rotation curves winds and ocean currents
climate factors: latitude, altitude elevation, nearness to water oceans, ocean currents, prevailing winds, mountains
places near the equator are warm hot tropical all year ~ places near the poles are cold all year
higher altitude elevation is colder mountains tops snow ~ lower elevation warmer
rain shadow dry side of a mountain range desert winds lose moisture rising
climate zones: tropical, temperate, polar, arid desert
greenhouse effect gases carbon dioxide methane water vapor trap heat in the atmosphere warm Earth
global warming climate change increase in average temperature caused by greenhouse gases burning fossil fuels
global warming effects: melting glaciers ice caps, rising sea levels, extreme weather, habitat loss
ice ages long cold periods glaciers covered much of land
atmosphere layers of gases surrounding Earth air
air is a mixture of gases about 78 percent nitrogen 21 percent oxygen small amounts argon carbon dioxide water vapor
nitrogen is the most abundant gas in the atmosphere
atmosphere layers: troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere
troposphere lowest layer where weather happens clouds
stratosphere contains the ozone layer absorbs ultraviolet radiation
ozone layer protects living things from harmful ultraviolet UV rays from the Sun
CFCs chlorofluorocarbons damage the ozone layer
thermosphere very hot thin air auroras
air pressure and temperature decrease with altitude height
ocean water is salty contains dissolved salts minerals sodium chloride
salt water is denser than fresh water
ocean currents move water and heat around the world warm currents cold currents Gulf Stream
surface currents caused by winds ~ deep currents caused by differences in density temperature salinity
tides rise and fall of sea level caused mainly by the Moon's gravity pull
two high tides and two low tides each day about every 12 hours
spring tides biggest range when Sun Moon Earth line up new moon full moon ~ neap tides smallest range when Sun and Moon at right angles quarter moon
waves in the ocean caused mostly by wind energy
tsunami giant ocean wave caused by underwater earthquake volcano landslide
ocean floor features: continental shelf, slope, abyssal plain, mid-ocean ridge, trench, seamount
mid-ocean ridge underwater mountain range where new ocean floor forms seafloor spreading
ocean trenches deepest parts of the ocean where plates subduct Mariana Trench
coral reefs warm shallow clear ocean water many species
estuary where fresh river water mixes with salt ocean water brackish
sonar uses sound waves echoes to map ocean floor depth
