# waves sound light
waves transfer energy from one place to another without transferring matter
mechanical waves need a medium matter solid liquid gas to travel sound water seismic ~ electromagnetic waves can travel through empty space vacuum light radio
wave parts: crest top, trough bottom, wavelength distance between crests, amplitude height energy, frequency waves per second hertz
higher frequency shorter wavelength ~ lower frequency longer wavelength
larger amplitude more energy louder sound brighter light
transverse waves particles move perpendicular at right angles to the direction of the wave light water waves ~ longitudinal compression waves particles move parallel back and forth in the same direction as the wave sound compressions rarefactions
sound is produced by vibrations of objects moving back and forth
sound travels as waves through solids liquids and gases but not through empty space vacuum
sound travels fastest through solids, slower through liquids, slowest through gases air ~ sound cannot travel in space
pitch how high or low a sound is depends on frequency of vibration fast vibrations high pitch ~ slow vibrations low pitch
short strings thin tight strings small objects vibrate faster higher pitch ~ long thick loose strings large drums lower pitch
loudness volume depends on amplitude energy of sound waves decibels
echo reflected sound wave bouncing off surfaces
echolocation bats dolphins use echoes reflected sound to find food navigate
sonar ultrasound use sound waves echoes
ears hear sounds vibrations eardrum
musical instruments vibrate strings guitar violin, air columns flute trumpet, drums membranes
soundproofing soft materials absorb sound foam carpet curtains
Doppler effect pitch changes as a source moves toward or away siren
light travels in straight lines rays very fast 300,000 km per second faster than sound
light can be reflected, refracted, absorbed, or transmitted
reflection light bounces off a surface mirror smooth shiny surfaces
we see objects because light reflects off them into our eyes
refraction light bends when it passes from one medium to another air water glass changes speed straw looks bent in water
lenses refract bend light: convex lens thicker in middle magnifying glass focuses light converges ~ concave lens thinner in middle spreads light diverges
prism separates white light into a spectrum of colors rainbow refraction
rainbow sunlight refracted and reflected by raindrops
white light is a mixture of all colors red orange yellow green blue indigo violet ROYGBIV
the color of an object is the color of light it reflects; other colors are absorbed
a red apple reflects red light and absorbs other colors
black absorbs all colors of light ~ white reflects all colors
transparent materials let almost all light pass through clear glass window water air ~ translucent let some light through frosted glass wax paper ~ opaque block light no light passes wood metal brick cardboard
shadows form when an opaque object blocks light
electromagnetic spectrum from longest to shortest wavelength: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, x-rays, gamma rays
radio waves communication radio television cell phones ~ microwaves cooking radar ~ infrared heat thermal imaging remote controls ~ ultraviolet sunburn tanning ~ x-rays see bones medical images ~ gamma rays most energy radioactive
visible light is the only part of the electromagnetic spectrum we can see
lasers concentrated light beam one color
fiber optics light through glass fibers communication
light from the Sun takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth
# natural resources environment
natural resources materials from Earth used by people: air, water, soil, minerals, plants, animals, fossil fuels, sunlight
renewable resources can be replaced naturally in a short time: sunlight solar energy, wind, water hydroelectric, trees wood, plants crops, biomass, geothermal, tides, animals ~ nonrenewable resources cannot be replaced quickly limited supply take millions of years to form: coal, oil, petroleum, natural gas, gasoline, uranium, minerals, metals
fossil fuels coal oil natural gas formed from remains of ancient plants and animals over millions of years nonrenewable
burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide greenhouse gases air pollution smog acid rain global warming
solar energy from the Sun solar panels photovoltaic cells change sunlight into electricity renewable clean no pollution
wind energy wind turbines windmills generate electricity renewable
hydroelectric power dams use flowing falling water to turn turbines generate electricity
geothermal energy uses heat from inside the Earth hot springs
biomass fuel from plants wood crops ethanol corn animal waste
nuclear power plants use uranium fission produce radioactive waste no air pollution
advantages of renewable energy clean does not run out ~ disadvantages solar does not work at night cloudy wind not always blowing
conservation using resources wisely: reduce, reuse, recycle
reduce use less ~ reuse use again ~ recycle make old materials into new products paper aluminum cans glass plastic
recycling aluminum cans paper saves energy resources reduces landfill trash
composting food scraps yard waste decomposes into fertilizer soil
landfills bury trash garbage solid waste
air pollution from cars factories burning fuels smoke exhaust smog causes breathing problems
water pollution from sewage, fertilizer runoff, pesticides, oil spills, chemicals, trash harms fish and wildlife
oil spills harm ocean birds animals coat feathers
acid rain caused by sulfur dioxide nitrogen oxides from burning coal harms lakes forests buildings
pesticides kill insects pests can harm other animals birds DDT eagles
thermal pollution warm water from power plants lowers oxygen harms fish
noise pollution light pollution
water treatment filtering chlorine cleans drinking water
conserve water: shorter showers, fix leaks, turn off faucet while brushing teeth
carpooling public transportation bikes walking reduce pollution save fuel
electric hybrid cars use less gasoline less pollution
deforestation cutting down forests causes habitat loss erosion more carbon dioxide
reforestation planting trees
strip mining damages land habitats
humans change environments: building cities dams roads farming mining clearing forests
# scientific inquiry measurement tools
scientific method: ask a question, research, form a hypothesis, test with an experiment, collect and analyze data, draw a conclusion, communicate results
hypothesis a testable prediction or possible explanation if then statement educated guess
experiment tests a hypothesis by changing one variable at a time
independent manipulated variable the one factor the scientist changes on purpose ~ dependent responding variable what is measured observed results ~ controlled variables constants kept the same
control group not changed used for comparison ~ experimental group receives the variable being tested
a fair test changes only one variable and keeps all other conditions the same
repeat trials many times to make results reliable valid reduce error
larger sample size more trials more reliable results
data are recorded in tables charts and shown in graphs
bar graph compares categories ~ line graph shows change over time continuous data trends ~ circle pie graph shows parts of a whole percent
x-axis horizontal independent variable ~ y-axis vertical dependent variable
observation using senses to gather information ~ inference conclusion based on observations explanation
qualitative observations descriptions color smell ~ quantitative observations numbers measurements
conclusion tells whether data support the hypothesis
a theory is a well tested explanation supported by much evidence ~ a law describes what happens always
scientists share results so others can repeat the experiment peer review replicate verify
models represent objects or processes too big small or complex globe map diagram
prediction what you think will happen based on evidence patterns
classify sort objects into groups by properties
safety in the laboratory: wear safety goggles to protect eyes, gloves, aprons, tie back hair, no eating or drinking food, wash hands, follow teacher directions, report spills accidents
safety goggles protect eyes from chemicals splashes heat
never taste or smell chemicals directly waft
fire extinguisher eye wash station safety shower
dispose of chemicals properly as teacher directs
metric system SI units: meter length, gram mass, liter volume, second time, Celsius temperature

1 kilometer equals 1000 meters ~ 1 meter equals 100 centimeters ~ 1 centimeter equals 10 millimeters
1 liter equals 1000 milliliters ~ 1 kilogram equals 1000 grams
tools: ruler meter stick measure length distance ~ balance scale measures mass grams ~ graduated cylinder measures liquid volume milliliters ~ thermometer measures temperature ~ stopwatch clock measures time ~ spring scale measures weight force newtons
hand lens magnifying glass makes small objects look larger ~ microscope views very tiny objects cells ~ telescope views distant objects stars planets ~ binoculars distant birds
