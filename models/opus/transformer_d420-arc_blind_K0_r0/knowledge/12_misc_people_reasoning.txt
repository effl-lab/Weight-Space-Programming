# reasoning about experiments and evidence
repeating an experiment several times makes the results more reliable trustworthy accurate
using a large number of plants or samples gives more reliable results
only one variable should be changed so the results can be explained by that variable
a control is used to compare results with the experimental group
keeping all conditions the same except one makes the test fair
the results of an investigation should be recorded accurately in a data table science notebook journal
scientists communicate their results by publishing reports so others can repeat replicate the investigation
when results do not support the hypothesis the scientist should revise the hypothesis and test again
graphs help scientists see patterns trends in data
evidence data observations support a conclusion
an opinion is not a scientific observation ~ a fact can be measured observed tested
scientific explanations must be based on evidence and can change with new evidence
new technology new tools such as better microscopes telescopes lead to new discoveries
scientists use models to study things that are too large too small too far away or too dangerous
computer models simulations predict weather climate
safety first follow directions handle glassware carefully report broken glass
students should wash hands after handling plants animals soil chemicals
heat liquids in heat-resistant containers point test tube away from people
do not touch eyes face during lab
living things in the classroom should be treated humanely with care food water
release animals back to where they were found
# living versus nonliving, needs of organisms
living things grow, reproduce, respond to the environment, need energy food water air, are made of cells, produce wastes
nonliving things do not grow reproduce or need food: rocks water air sunlight
all living things need water food energy air and space shelter to survive
animals need food water air oxygen and shelter
animals get energy by eating food plants or other animals ~ plants get energy from sunlight
offspring look like their parents inherited traits
young animals grow and change into adults
organisms depend on each other in an ecosystem
plants provide oxygen and food for animals ~ animals provide carbon dioxide and fertilizer for plants
bees pollinate flowers and get nectar food
birds and animals spread seeds by eating fruit
worms and bacteria in the soil decompose dead material and recycle nutrients for plants
if the producers in an ecosystem die the consumers lose their food source
changes in the environment like drought fire flood can cause organisms to move adapt or die
organisms that cannot adapt to environmental change may die out become extinct
some organisms survive harsh conditions by becoming dormant hibernating migrating
seeds can stay dormant until conditions are right for germination
populations grow when there is plenty of food and few predators
overcrowding causes competition for food water space
humans affect ecosystems by building, farming, polluting, hunting, introducing species, conserving
