# more life science facts
the cell is the smallest unit of life that can carry out all life processes
organ systems work together: muscles and bones move the body, lungs and heart deliver oxygen, stomach and intestines digest food
the heart is a muscle that pumps blood
the brain sends signals through nerves to muscles to move
lungs bring oxygen into the body and remove carbon dioxide
the stomach breaks down food with acid
the skeleton supports the body and protects organs
the skin protects the body is the largest organ
teeth break food into smaller pieces chewing begins digestion mechanical
saliva begins chemical digestion of starches
blood carries oxygen food nutrients and wastes throughout the body
red blood cells carry oxygen from the lungs to body cells
the immune system protects the body against disease germs viruses bacteria
fever helps the body fight infection
antibodies attack germs immunity
allergies immune system reacts to pollen dust
asthma airways narrow breathing difficult
a broken bone heals as new bone cells grow
a cut heals skin cells divide platelets form a scab clot
growth and repair of tissues by cell division mitosis
energy for the body comes from food broken down in cells with oxygen
cells get energy from glucose sugar by cellular respiration in the mitochondria
muscle cells need lots of energy many mitochondria
the body needs water to transport nutrients regulate temperature remove wastes
dehydration not enough water
during exercise muscles need more oxygen so breathing and heart rate increase
after exercise heart rate returns to normal resting
the body loses heat by sweating evaporation cools the skin
goose bumps shivering keep the body warm
the nervous system detects changes stimuli and responds
the eye receives light the ear receives sound vibrations the nose detects chemicals odors the tongue taste buds detect flavors the skin detects pressure heat cold pain
optic nerve carries signals from the eye to the brain
the cerebrum thinking memory ~ the cerebellum balance coordination ~ the brain stem breathing heartbeat
spinal cord injury can cause paralysis
reflexes protect the body from harm quick automatic
hormones are chemicals made by glands that travel in blood to target organs
insulin lowers blood sugar made by the pancreas
adrenaline increases heart rate in danger fight or flight response
growth hormone from pituitary gland controls growth
the reproductive system allows organisms to produce offspring
human babies develop inside the mother's uterus about nine months
inherited genes from both mother and father
genetic traits passed from parents to children through DNA in sex cells
children resemble their parents because they inherit genes
a dominant allele masks a recessive allele
two heterozygous parents Bb x Bb have a 25 percent chance of a recessive offspring bb
a Punnett square shows possible combinations of alleles from parents
tall pea plants T dominant over short t recessive
mutations can be caused by radiation chemicals
some traits are influenced by both genes and environment height weight
identical traits from asexual reproduction clones
variation from sexual reproduction helps species survive changing environments
organisms with traits best suited to their environment are more likely to survive and reproduce
over many generations helpful traits become more common in a population
a population of bacteria exposed to antibiotics survivors are resistant
the long neck of giraffes developed over many generations natural selection
different beaks of finches adapted to different food sources on islands
species change over time because of mutation variation and natural selection
fossils show that many organisms that lived long ago are now extinct
similar structures in different species suggest common ancestry
embryos of different vertebrates look similar early in development
DNA similarities show how closely related species are
classification is based on similarities in structure body parts characteristics DNA
a dichotomous key uses pairs of descriptions choices to identify organisms
scientists classify organisms into kingdoms based on cell type, number of cells, how they get food
plants are multicellular producers with cell walls ~ fungi are consumers decomposers with cell walls that absorb food ~ animals are multicellular consumers without cell walls
bacteria can be helpful: decomposers, making yogurt cheese, digestion in intestines, nitrogen fixing ~ bacteria can be harmful: cause diseases strep throat food poisoning
viruses are not cells they reproduce only inside living host cells
microorganisms include bacteria, protists, some fungi, and are seen with a microscope
yogurt cheese bread made using microorganisms bacteria yeast fermentation
algae protists produce oxygen by photosynthesis in oceans lakes
plankton float in water food for fish whales
a seed germinates when it gets water warmth and oxygen
seedlings need light to grow and make food
plants grown in the dark are tall pale yellow weak spindly
plants without water wilt and die
plants need nutrients from soil like nitrogen to grow healthy green leaves
leaves are green because chlorophyll reflects green light
plants release oxygen during the day photosynthesis
the main product of photosynthesis is sugar glucose food
oxygen is a product waste of photosynthesis
plants store energy in roots stems seeds fruits as starch sugar
animals get energy by eating plants or animals that ate plants
the energy in food originally came from the Sun
grass captures sunlight energy; a cow eats grass; a person drinks milk: energy flows from sun to plants to animals
when an animal dies decomposers return its nutrients to the soil
dead leaves decompose into humus nutrients for soil
earthworms bacteria fungi help decomposition
without decomposers dead material would pile up and nutrients would not be recycled
food chains begin with producers plants
a food web shows many feeding relationships
energy is lost as heat at each step of a food chain
a decrease in one population affects others in the food web
removing a predator can cause the prey population to grow and overgraze plants
introducing a new species can upset the balance of an ecosystem
a disease that kills producers would reduce the food for herbivores
an increase in the rabbit population leads to an increase in the fox population and a decrease in grass
pollinators bees butterflies birds are needed for many plants to produce fruits and seeds
loss of bees would reduce fruit crops
plants compete for sunlight water nutrients space
animals compete for food water territory mates shelter
organisms adapted to their environment: fish gills water, birds hollow bones flying, cactus desert, polar bear cold
organisms in the desert are adapted to conserve water
organisms in the arctic tundra are adapted to cold: thick fur, fat layers, small ears, white color
organisms in the ocean deep sea: bioluminescence, no light, high pressure, cold
organisms in rainforests: climbing, bright colors, many species, canopy layers
plants in wetlands have adaptations for wet soil
animals that live in groups herd school flock protect each other
animals use camouflage to hide from predators or prey
some animals use bright colors to warn predators they are poisonous
some animals play dead or use spines shells or speed for defense
some plants have thorns spines or poisons to protect themselves from animals
some animals store food for winter
some animals grow thicker fur in winter and shed it in spring
birds build nests to protect eggs and young
the temperature of the environment affects how fast cold-blooded animals move
lizards bask in the sun in the morning to warm their bodies
animals in hot climates rest in shade burrows during the hottest part of the day
seasonal changes: in fall trees drop leaves, animals store food, birds migrate; in spring plants bloom, animals have young
a habitat provides food water shelter and space for an animal
when a habitat changes organisms may move adapt or die
human activities such as cutting forests building dams draining wetlands destroy habitats
pollution can harm organisms in an ecosystem
clean water is needed by fish and other aquatic organisms
fish need dissolved oxygen in water
too many nutrients from fertilizer cause algae to grow and use up oxygen fish die
national parks protect habitats and endangered species
zoos and seed banks help preserve species
conservation efforts: protecting habitats, laws against hunting, captive breeding programs, reducing pollution
