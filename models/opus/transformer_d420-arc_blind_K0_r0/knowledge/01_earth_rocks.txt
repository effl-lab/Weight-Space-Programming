# rocks, minerals, rock cycle, soil, weathering, erosion, landforms
igneous rock forms when magma or lava cools and hardens solidifies crystallizes
igneous rocks: granite, basalt, obsidian, pumice, rhyolite, gabbro, andesite
intrusive igneous rock cools slowly underground inside the crust large crystals coarse grains granite ~ extrusive igneous rock cools quickly on the surface lava small crystals fine grains glassy basalt obsidian
obsidian volcanic glass cools very quickly no crystals smooth shiny
pumice lightweight volcanic rock full of holes gas bubbles floats on water
sedimentary rock forms from layers of sediments compacted and cemented together pressure
sedimentary rocks: sandstone, limestone, shale, conglomerate, coal, chalk, siltstone, breccia
sedimentary rocks form layers strata deposited in water lakes oceans
fossils are found mostly in sedimentary rock layers
limestone forms from shells skeletons of sea organisms calcium carbonate calcite fizzes bubbles in acid
sandstone is made of cemented sand grains quartz
shale forms from compacted mud clay silt fine particles
conglomerate made of rounded pebbles gravel cemented together
metamorphic rock forms when heat and pressure change existing rock deep underground without melting
metamorphic rocks: marble, slate, quartzite, gneiss, schist
limestone changes into marble under heat and pressure
shale changes into slate metamorphism
sandstone changes into quartzite
granite changes into gneiss banded foliated
rock cycle: rocks change from one type to another over long time igneous sedimentary metamorphic
rock cycle processes: weathering erosion deposition compaction cementation melting cooling heat pressure
melting rock forms magma; magma cools to form igneous rock
magma is molten rock below the surface ~ lava is molten rock on the surface erupted from a volcano
minerals are naturally occurring inorganic solids with a crystal structure and definite chemical composition
rocks are made of one or more minerals
mineral properties used to identify minerals: hardness, luster, streak, color, cleavage, fracture, crystal shape, density
hardness is resistance to scratching measured by Mohs scale scratch test
Mohs hardness scale from 1 talc softest to 10 diamond hardest
talc softest mineral hardness 1 ~ diamond hardest mineral hardness 10
quartz hardness 7 scratches glass common mineral in sand granite
gypsum hardness 2 fingernail scratches it
calcite hardness 3 copper penny fizzes with acid
fingernail 2.5, copper penny 3.5, glass steel nail 5.5, streak plate 6.5 hardness tests
streak is the color of a mineral's powder rubbed on a white unglazed porcelain tile streak plate
luster describes how a mineral reflects light: metallic shiny, nonmetallic glassy, dull, pearly, waxy
cleavage mineral splits along smooth flat planes ~ fracture mineral breaks with rough uneven or curved surfaces
color alone is the least reliable way to identify a mineral
magnetite is magnetic attracts a magnet iron ore
halite rock salt cubic crystals tastes salty
pyrite fool's gold metallic brassy yellow
mica splits into thin flexible sheets
feldspar common mineral in granite
graphite soft mineral used in pencils carbon
diamond carbon hardest mineral gems
gems precious stones cut polished crystals
ores contain metals mined from the earth iron copper aluminum gold
crystal size depends on how fast magma cools slow cooling large crystals ~ fast cooling small crystals
weathering breaks down rocks into smaller pieces sediments soil
mechanical physical weathering breaks rock without changing its composition: ice wedging, frost, roots, abrasion ~ chemical weathering changes minerals: acid rain, oxidation rust, dissolving limestone, carbonic acid
ice wedging frost action water seeps into cracks freezes expands breaks rock apart
plant roots grow into cracks and split break rocks apart weathering
acid rain dissolves limestone marble statues chemical weathering
water is the main agent of weathering and erosion
abrasion rocks rubbing grinding wearing away smooth rounded pebbles in rivers
lichens and mosses break down rocks and help form soil
erosion is the moving carrying transport of weathered rock sediment soil by wind water ice glaciers gravity
erosion agents: running water, rivers, streams, wind, glaciers, ice, waves, gravity
deposition sediments are dropped deposited settled when water or wind slows down
rivers erode valleys canyons and deposit sediment at deltas floodplains mouth
delta forms where a river deposits sediment entering an ocean or lake
Grand Canyon formed by erosion of the Colorado River over millions of years
V-shaped valleys carved by rivers ~ U-shaped valleys carved by glaciers
glaciers are huge moving masses of ice that carve valleys and carry boulders rocks till moraines
glaciers deposit moraines till erratics boulders when they melt retreat
glacier ice age covered land scratched striations on bedrock
wind erosion in deserts carries sand forms dunes sandblasting
sand dunes formed by wind deposition in deserts and beaches
waves erode beaches coastlines cliffs shoreline
beach erosion longshore currents move sand along coast
landslides mudslides rockfalls caused by gravity on steep slopes heavy rain
mass movement gravity pulls soil rock downhill
plant roots grass trees hold soil in place and prevent reduce erosion
ways to reduce soil erosion: plant trees grass cover crops, contour plowing, terraces, windbreaks, retaining walls
deforestation removing trees increases soil erosion and flooding
farming plowing leaves soil bare exposed to wind water erosion dust bowl
soil formation: weathered rock mixed with humus organic matter decayed plants animals
soil is made of minerals rock particles, organic matter humus, water, air
humus decayed organic matter from dead plants and animals makes soil fertile dark rich nutrients
topsoil is the top layer rich in humus nutrients ~ subsoil below has clay minerals less humus ~ bedrock solid rock at bottom
soil particle sizes: sand largest gritty, silt medium, clay smallest sticky
sandy soil drains water quickly large particles large pore spaces ~ clay soil holds water small particles drains slowly
loam soil mixture of sand silt clay humus best for growing plants
earthworms burrow mix aerate soil and add nutrients castings
decomposers break down dead matter adding nutrients to the soil
soil conservation keeps soil fertile prevents erosion
fertile soil has nutrients nitrogen phosphorus potassium for plant growth
landforms: mountains, valleys, plains, plateaus, canyons, hills, mesas, buttes, deltas, islands, peninsulas
plateau high flat land raised area
mesa flat topped hill with steep sides formed by erosion
canyon deep narrow valley with steep sides carved by a river
caves caverns form when groundwater dissolves limestone stalactites stalagmites
stalactites hang from cave ceiling ~ stalagmites grow up from cave floor
sinkholes form when underground limestone dissolves collapses
barrier islands sand bars along coasts
geologic time: earth is about 4.6 billion years old
law of superposition: in undisturbed rock layers the oldest layers are at the bottom and youngest at the top
relative dating compares rock layers older younger ~ absolute dating radioactive decay half-life radiometric exact age
index fossils lived a short time over wide area used to date rock layers
fossils form when remains of organisms are buried in sediment and harden into rock mineralization
fossil types: imprints, molds, casts, petrified wood, trace fossils footprints tracks burrows, amber, frozen in ice, tar pits
petrified wood minerals replace wood over time
amber fossilized tree sap resin traps insects
trace fossils footprints tracks burrows show behavior of ancient animals
fossils give evidence of past life environments and changes over time
fossils of sea shells marine organisms found on mountains show the area was once covered by ocean
fossils of tropical plants ferns found in cold polar areas Antarctica show climate was once warm or continents moved
coal formed from ancient swamp plants buried and compressed over millions of years
oil petroleum natural gas formed from remains of ancient tiny marine organisms plankton buried
dinosaurs became extinct about 65 million years ago possibly from asteroid impact
Mesozoic era age of dinosaurs reptiles ~ Cenozoic era age of mammals
Paleozoic era trilobites fish amphibians first land plants
Precambrian earliest longest time simple single celled life
radioactive elements decay at a constant rate half-life used to find age of rocks
carbon-14 dating used to find age of once living remains bones wood
