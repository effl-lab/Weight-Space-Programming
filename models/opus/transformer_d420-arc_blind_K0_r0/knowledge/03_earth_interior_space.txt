# earth interior, plate tectonics, earthquakes, volcanoes
Earth layers: crust, mantle, outer core, inner core
crust thin rocky outer layer we live on ~ core center of Earth made of iron and nickel very hot
inner core solid iron nickel extremely hot high pressure ~ outer core liquid molten iron nickel creates magnetic field
mantle thickest layer hot slowly flowing rock convection currents
lithosphere crust and upper mantle broken into tectonic plates ~ asthenosphere soft flowing plastic layer of mantle
continental crust thick less dense granite ~ oceanic crust thin denser basalt
plate tectonics: Earth's crust is broken into large plates that move slowly a few centimeters per year
convection currents in the mantle move tectonic plates
continental drift Alfred Wegener continents were once joined in one supercontinent Pangaea
evidence for continental drift: matching coastlines puzzle fit of South America and Africa, similar fossils and rocks on different continents, glacial evidence
plate boundaries: divergent move apart, convergent collide push together, transform slide past each other
divergent boundary plates move apart new crust forms rift valleys mid-ocean ridges seafloor spreading
convergent boundary plates collide subduction trenches volcanoes mountains form
subduction denser oceanic plate sinks under continental plate
mountains form when two continental plates collide push up fold Himalayas
transform boundary plates slide past each other earthquakes San Andreas fault California
faults cracks breaks in the crust where rocks move
earthquakes occur when rocks suddenly move along faults releasing energy seismic waves vibrations shaking
seismic waves: P waves primary fastest compression, S waves secondary shear only through solids, surface waves most damage
seismograph seismometer measures and records earthquake waves vibrations
Richter scale magnitude measures strength energy of earthquakes
epicenter point on surface directly above the focus where earthquake begins underground
most earthquakes and volcanoes occur along plate boundaries Ring of Fire Pacific Ocean
volcanoes form where magma rises through the crust erupts lava ash gases
volcanoes form at plate boundaries and hot spots Hawaii islands
hot spot magma rises in the middle of a plate forms chain of volcanic islands Hawaii
volcanic eruption releases lava ash gases rocks pyroclastic flow
volcanic ash blocks sunlight can cool climate
shield volcano gentle slopes runny lava Hawaii ~ composite stratovolcano steep explosive layers ash lava ~ cinder cone small steep
new land islands form from cooled lava volcanic eruptions
geysers hot springs groundwater heated by magma Yellowstone
folding rock layers bend under pressure ~ faulting rock layers break
uplift raises land mountains plateaus
earthquake safety drop cover hold on under a sturdy table
buildings designed to withstand earthquakes flexible foundations

# space: Sun Earth Moon
Earth rotates spins on its axis once every 24 hours causing day and night ~ Earth revolves orbits around the Sun once every 365 days one year
day and night are caused by Earth's rotation on its axis
the side of Earth facing the Sun has day ~ the side facing away from the Sun has night
the Sun appears to move across the sky from east to west because Earth rotates from west to east
the Sun rises in the east sunrise morning ~ the Sun sets in the west sunset evening
stars appear to move across the night sky because Earth rotates
shadows are longest in the morning and evening when the Sun is low ~ shadows are shortest at noon midday when the Sun is highest
shadows point away from the light source the Sun; morning shadows point west
a year is the time for Earth to revolve once around the Sun 365.25 days leap year
seasons are caused by the tilt of Earth's axis 23.5 degrees as Earth revolves around the Sun
summer: hemisphere tilted toward the Sun direct rays more daylight longer days warmer ~ winter: hemisphere tilted away from the Sun indirect slanted rays shorter days colder
seasons are not caused by distance from the Sun
northern hemisphere summer June July ~ northern hemisphere winter December January
summer solstice longest day June 21 ~ winter solstice shortest day December 21
equinox equal day and night spring March fall autumn September
seasons in the southern hemisphere are opposite those in the northern hemisphere Australia
near the equator day length stays about the same all year
the Moon revolves orbits around Earth about once every 27 to 29.5 days one month
the Moon rotates once for each orbit so the same side always faces Earth
the Moon reflects light from the Sun it does not make its own light
moon phases are caused by the Moon's position in its orbit how much of the lit half we see from Earth
moon phases order: new moon, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full moon, waning gibbous, third last quarter, waning crescent
new moon Moon between Earth and Sun dark side faces Earth ~ full moon Earth between Sun and Moon whole lit side faces Earth
waxing lit part growing getting larger ~ waning lit part shrinking getting smaller
moon phase cycle repeats about every 29.5 days a month
solar eclipse Moon passes between the Sun and Earth blocks Sun casts shadow on Earth new moon ~ lunar eclipse Earth passes between the Sun and Moon Earth's shadow falls on the Moon full moon
the Moon has craters formed by meteorite impacts no atmosphere no liquid water no weather
the Moon has no atmosphere so craters footprints last a very long time and temperatures are extreme
gravity on the Moon is about one sixth of Earth's gravity objects weigh less but mass stays the same
the Moon's gravity causes tides on Earth
the Moon is Earth's only natural satellite about 384,000 km away
astronauts on the Moon need space suits oxygen because there is no air
# solar system
the Sun is a star at the center of our solar system a ball of hot gases hydrogen helium
the Sun produces energy by nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium light heat
the Sun is the closest star to Earth that's why it looks bigger and brighter than other stars
the Sun is a medium sized average yellow star
the Sun's gravity holds the planets in their orbits around it
solar system: the Sun, eight planets, moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets, meteoroids
planets in order from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune
inner terrestrial planets small rocky dense Mercury Venus Earth Mars ~ outer gas giant planets large gaseous rings many moons Jupiter Saturn Uranus Neptune

Venus hottest planet thick carbon dioxide atmosphere greenhouse effect clouds sulfuric acid brightest planet
Earth third planet from the Sun only planet known to have life liquid water oxygen atmosphere
Mars red planet iron oxide rust dust thin atmosphere polar ice caps Olympus Mons largest volcano two moons

Neptune farthest planet from the Sun blue windy cold
Pluto dwarf planet beyond Neptune Kuiper belt
planets farther from the Sun take longer to orbit have longer years and are colder
planets closer to the Sun orbit faster shorter years
asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter rocky objects
comets balls of ice dust rock with long tails that point away from the Sun solar wind elongated orbits
meteoroid small rock in space ~ meteor streak of light shooting star burning up in the atmosphere ~ meteorite rock that hits Earth's surface
planets orbit in elliptical paths ellipses
gravity keeps planets in orbit around the Sun and moons in orbit around planets
inertia and gravity together keep objects in orbit
