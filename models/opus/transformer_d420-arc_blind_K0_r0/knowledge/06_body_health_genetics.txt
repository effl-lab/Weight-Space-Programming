# human body systems
skeletal system bones support the body give shape protect organs make blood cells in marrow allow movement with muscles
skull protects the brain ~ ribs rib cage protect heart and lungs ~ spine backbone vertebrae protects spinal cord
joints where bones meet allow movement hinge knee elbow ball and socket shoulder hip
ligaments connect bone to bone ~ tendons connect muscle to bone
cartilage flexible tissue cushions joints nose ears
calcium vitamin D build strong bones milk
muscular system muscles contract pull bones to move the body
skeletal muscles voluntary move bones ~ smooth muscles involuntary in stomach intestines blood vessels ~ cardiac muscle heart involuntary
muscles work in pairs biceps triceps one contracts other relaxes
circulatory system heart blood vessels arteries veins capillaries blood carries oxygen nutrients to cells removes carbon dioxide wastes
heart pumps blood through the body four chambers atria ventricles
arteries carry blood away from the heart oxygen-rich ~ veins carry blood back to the heart oxygen-poor valves
capillaries tiny blood vessels exchange oxygen nutrients wastes with cells
blood parts: red blood cells carry oxygen, white blood cells fight infection, platelets help clot stop bleeding, plasma liquid
pulse heartbeat rate increases during exercise to deliver more oxygen to muscles
exercise increases heart rate breathing rate sweating
respiratory system lungs breathe take in oxygen release carbon dioxide nose trachea windpipe bronchi alveoli diaphragm
alveoli tiny air sacs in lungs where oxygen enters blood and carbon dioxide leaves gas exchange
diaphragm muscle contracts to pull air into the lungs breathing inhale ~ exhale
respiratory and circulatory systems work together deliver oxygen to cells and remove carbon dioxide
digestive system breaks down food into nutrients absorbed into the blood mouth esophagus stomach small intestine large intestine
digestion begins in the mouth teeth chew saliva enzymes break down starch
esophagus muscular tube moves food from mouth to stomach peristalsis
stomach mixes food with acid and enzymes digestive juices breaks down proteins
small intestine absorbs most nutrients into the blood villi
large intestine colon absorbs water forms solid waste feces
liver makes bile breaks down fats filters toxins ~ pancreas makes enzymes and insulin
digestive and circulatory systems work together nutrients from food carried by blood to cells
excretory system removes liquid wastes kidneys filter blood make urine bladder skin sweat lungs
kidneys filter wastes from blood urine
skin integumentary system largest organ protects the body from germs injury water loss regulates temperature sweat senses touch
sweating cools the body by evaporation
nervous system brain spinal cord nerves control body sends receives messages signals electrical impulses
brain controls thinking memory senses body functions ~ spinal cord carries messages between brain and body
neurons nerve cells send electrical signals impulses
reflex automatic quick response without thinking pulling hand away from hot stove knee jerk
senses: sight eyes, hearing ears, smell nose, taste tongue, touch skin receptors
eyes detect light: pupil lets light in, iris colored part controls pupil size, lens focuses light, retina detects light sends signals optic nerve
pupils get smaller in bright light larger in dim light
ears detect sound vibrations eardrum vibrates cochlea
endocrine system glands make hormones chemical messengers carried in blood control growth development metabolism
hormones: insulin controls blood sugar, adrenaline fight or flight, growth hormone, thyroid
pituitary gland master gland ~ thyroid gland metabolism ~ adrenal glands adrenaline
immune system fights germs disease white blood cells antibodies
lymphatic system lymph nodes vessels returns fluid filters germs part of immune defense
reproductive system produces sex cells eggs sperm offspring
female ovaries produce eggs ~ male testes produce sperm
fertilization sperm joins egg forms zygote embryo develops in uterus
puberty adolescence body changes hormones
homeostasis keeping stable internal conditions body temperature water balance
body temperature about 37 degrees Celsius 98.6 Fahrenheit
shivering produces heat when cold ~ sweating releases heat when hot

# genetics heredity
heredity passing of traits from parents to offspring
inherited traits are passed from parents to offspring through genes: eye color, hair color, height, skin color, dimples, freckles, blood type, flower color, seed shape ~ acquired learned traits come from environment or experience: scars, language, reading, riding a bike, tattoos, haircut, injuries, skills, calluses, strong muscles from exercise
genes are sections of DNA on chromosomes that carry instructions for traits
DNA deoxyribonucleic acid genetic material double helix in the nucleus code instructions
chromosomes carry genes; humans have 46 chromosomes 23 pairs one set from each parent
sex cells egg sperm have half the chromosomes 23
offspring get half of their genes from each parent
alleles different forms of a gene
dominant trait allele shows if at least one present capital letter ~ recessive trait allele hidden shows only if two copies lowercase letter
genotype genetic makeup alleles BB Bb bb ~ phenotype physical appearance observable trait
homozygous purebred two same alleles ~ heterozygous hybrid two different alleles
Punnett square predicts probability of offspring traits genotypes
Gregor Mendel father of genetics pea plants experiments
mutation change in DNA gene can be harmful helpful or neutral
genetic disorders sickle cell anemia cystic fibrosis hemophilia
selective breeding artificial selection humans breed plants animals for desired traits dogs crops cows
genetic engineering modifies DNA genes GMO crops insulin bacteria
cloning makes genetically identical copy Dolly sheep
identical twins same DNA from one egg ~ fraternal twins different eggs
environment can affect traits plant growth sunlight nutrition height
variation differences among individuals of a species
# evolution
evolution change in species over long periods of time
natural selection: individuals with helpful adaptations traits survive and reproduce more and pass traits to offspring survival of the fittest Darwin
Charles Darwin studied finches on Galapagos Islands different beak shapes for different foods
variation in a population allows natural selection; organisms best suited to the environment survive
overproduction more offspring than can survive competition
peppered moths dark moths survived better on sooty trees during pollution industrial revolution
antibiotic resistance bacteria evolve resist antibiotics
fossil record shows how organisms changed over time evidence of evolution
homologous structures similar bones in arms of humans wings of bats flippers of whales common ancestor
species that cannot adapt to environmental changes become extinct
