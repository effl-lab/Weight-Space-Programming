# glossary: term followed by its meaning in plain words
atom: the smallest particle of an element
molecule: two or more atoms joined together bonded
element: a substance made of only one kind of atom
compound: a substance made of two or more elements chemically combined
mixture: two or more substances mixed together but not chemically combined
solution: a mixture in which one substance dissolves completely in another evenly mixed
solubility: how much of a substance can dissolve
density: how much mass is in a given volume compactness
mass: the amount of matter in an object
weight: the pull of gravity on an object
volume: the amount of space something takes up
matter: anything that has mass and takes up space
energy: the ability to do work or cause change
force: a push or pull
friction: a force that resists motion when surfaces rub together
gravity: a force that pulls objects toward each other
inertia: resistance of an object to a change in its motion
speed: how fast an object moves distance per time
velocity: speed in a particular direction
acceleration: the rate at which velocity changes speeding up slowing down turning
work: force applied over a distance
power: how fast work is done
conductor: a material through which heat or electricity flows easily
insulator: a material that slows or stops the flow of heat or electricity
circuit: a closed path through which electric current flows
current: the flow of electric charge
static electricity: buildup of electric charges on an object
magnet: an object that attracts iron and has north and south poles
wave: a disturbance that carries energy from place to place
wavelength: distance between one crest and the next crest of a wave
frequency: number of waves that pass a point each second
amplitude: height of a wave from rest position measure of energy
reflection: light or sound bouncing off a surface
refraction: bending of light as it passes from one material into another
absorption: taking in of light heat or sound energy
transparent: lets light pass through clearly
opaque: does not let light pass through
translucent: lets some light pass through but scatters it blurry
vibration: a rapid back and forth movement
pitch: how high or low a sound is
photosynthesis: process plants use to make food sugar from sunlight water and carbon dioxide
respiration: process cells use to release energy from food using oxygen
transpiration: release of water vapor from plant leaves
evaporation: change from liquid to gas
condensation: change from gas to liquid
precipitation: water falling from clouds as rain snow sleet hail
sublimation: change from solid directly to gas
melting: change from solid to liquid
freezing: change from liquid to solid
erosion: carrying away of rock and soil by water wind ice gravity
weathering: breaking down of rocks into smaller pieces
deposition: dropping of sediment in a new place
sediment: small pieces of rock shells and other material moved and deposited
fossil: preserved remains or traces of an organism that lived long ago
mineral: naturally occurring nonliving solid with a crystal structure
rock: solid mixture of one or more minerals
magma: melted rock below Earth's surface
lava: melted rock that flows onto Earth's surface
crust: Earth's thin outermost layer
mantle: thick hot layer of Earth below the crust
core: Earth's center made of iron and nickel
plate: large moving piece of Earth's lithosphere
fault: crack in Earth's crust where rocks move
earthquake: sudden shaking of the ground caused by movement along a fault
volcano: opening in Earth's crust where magma erupts
glacier: large slowly moving mass of ice
climate: average weather of a place over many years
weather: condition of the atmosphere at a certain time and place
atmosphere: layer of gases surrounding Earth
humidity: amount of water vapor in the air
air pressure: force of the air pressing on a surface
front: boundary between two air masses
orbit: path of an object around another object in space
rotation: spinning of an object on its axis
revolution: movement of one object around another orbit
axis: imaginary line through the center of a spinning object
satellite: an object that orbits a planet; the Moon is Earth's natural satellite
star: huge ball of hot glowing gases that gives off light and heat
planet: large body that orbits a star and does not give off its own light
moon: natural satellite that orbits a planet
galaxy: huge system of billions of stars gas and dust
solar system: the Sun and all the objects that orbit it
constellation: group of stars forming a pattern
light-year: the distance light travels in one year
eclipse: when one object in space blocks light from reaching another
cell: smallest unit of a living thing basic building block of life
tissue: group of similar cells that work together
organ: group of tissues working together to do a job
organism: a living thing
nucleus: part of a cell that controls its activities and holds DNA
chromosome: thread-like structure in the nucleus made of DNA carrying genes
gene: section of DNA that controls a trait
trait: characteristic or feature of an organism
heredity: passing of traits from parents to offspring
dominant: trait that shows whenever its allele is present
recessive: trait that is hidden when a dominant allele is present
mutation: change in the DNA of a gene
adaptation: trait that helps an organism survive in its environment
evolution: change in species over time
natural selection: organisms best adapted to their environment survive and reproduce
extinction: when all members of a species have died out
species: group of similar organisms that can reproduce together
population: group of the same species living in one area
community: all the different populations living in an area
ecosystem: living and nonliving things interacting in an environment
habitat: place where an organism lives
niche: role of an organism in its ecosystem
producer: organism that makes its own food
consumer: organism that eats other organisms
decomposer: organism that breaks down dead organisms and wastes
predator: animal that hunts other animals for food
prey: animal hunted and eaten by another animal
herbivore: animal that eats only plants
carnivore: animal that eats only other animals
omnivore: animal that eats both plants and animals
scavenger: animal that eats dead animals
parasite: organism that lives on or in a host and harms it
symbiosis: close long-term relationship between two different species
food chain: path of energy from one organism to another
food web: overlapping food chains in an ecosystem
biome: large region with a similar climate plants and animals
instinct: behavior an animal is born with
learned behavior: behavior an animal gets through experience
hibernation: deep winter sleep that saves energy
migration: seasonal movement of animals from one place to another
metamorphosis: major change in body form during an animal's life cycle
pollination: transfer of pollen from stamen to pistil
germination: when a seed begins to sprout and grow
vertebrate: animal with a backbone
invertebrate: animal without a backbone
mammal: warm-blooded vertebrate with hair that feeds young milk
reptile: cold-blooded vertebrate with dry scales
amphibian: cold-blooded vertebrate that lives part of its life in water and part on land
insect: invertebrate with six legs three body parts
bacteria: one-celled organisms without a nucleus
virus: tiny particle that can reproduce only inside a living cell
vaccine: substance that helps the body build immunity to a disease
hypothesis: possible explanation that can be tested
variable: factor that can change in an experiment
control: standard used for comparison in an experiment
conclusion: statement that tells what was learned from an experiment
observation: information gathered using the senses
inference: explanation based on observations and prior knowledge
prediction: statement about what will happen based on evidence
theory: well-tested explanation supported by evidence
law: statement that describes what always happens in nature
model: representation of an object or process
data: information facts measurements collected in an investigation
renewable resource: resource that can be replaced naturally in a short time
nonrenewable resource: resource that cannot be replaced in a short time
fossil fuel: fuel formed from remains of ancient organisms coal oil natural gas
pollution: harmful substances added to air water or land
conservation: wise use and protection of natural resources
recycling: processing used materials to make new products
