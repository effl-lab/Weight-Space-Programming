# cells
cells are the basic unit of structure and function of all living things organisms
cell theory: all living things are made of cells, cells are the basic unit of life, all cells come from existing cells
microscope used to observe cells and tiny organisms microorganisms magnify
Robert Hooke first observed cells in cork ~ Leeuwenhoek first observed living microorganisms bacteria
unicellular single-celled organisms bacteria amoeba paramecium yeast ~ multicellular organisms made of many cells plants animals humans
levels of organization: cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism
tissues are groups of similar cells working together
organs are made of different tissues working together heart lungs stomach brain
organ systems are groups of organs working together
nucleus control center of the cell contains DNA genetic information chromosomes directs cell activities
cell membrane thin flexible layer controls what enters and leaves the cell protects selectively permeable
cell wall rigid layer outside the cell membrane in plant cells gives support shape structure cellulose
cytoplasm jelly-like fluid inside the cell where organelles are found
mitochondria powerhouse of the cell release energy from food sugar glucose cellular respiration ATP
chloroplasts in plant cells capture sunlight energy for photosynthesis contain green chlorophyll make food
vacuole stores water food wastes; plant cells have one large central vacuole
ribosomes make proteins protein synthesis
endoplasmic reticulum transports materials proteins in the cell
Golgi apparatus packages and ships proteins
lysosomes break down digest wastes worn out cell parts
plant cells have cell wall, chloroplasts, large vacuole, rectangular shape ~ animal cells have no cell wall, no chloroplasts, small vacuoles, round irregular shape, centrioles
prokaryotic cells have no nucleus bacteria ~ eukaryotic cells have a nucleus plants animals fungi protists
bacteria are single-celled prokaryotes with no nucleus cell wall reproduce by binary fission
diffusion movement of particles from high concentration to low concentration
osmosis diffusion of water across a membrane
active transport uses energy to move materials across the cell membrane
cells need energy oxygen water nutrients and must get rid of wastes
mitosis cell division produces two identical daughter cells for growth and repair of body cells ~ meiosis produces sex cells gametes egg sperm with half the chromosomes
cells divide so organisms can grow and replace repair damaged cells
skin cells red blood cells nerve cells muscle cells specialized cells have different shapes for different functions
nerve cells neurons are long with branches to send messages signals
red blood cells carry oxygen hemoglobin no nucleus disk shaped
white blood cells fight infection germs disease
muscle cells contract to cause movement
stem cells can become many different types of cells
cancer uncontrolled cell division tumor
# plants
plants are producers that make their own food by photosynthesis
photosynthesis: plants use sunlight energy, carbon dioxide, and water to make glucose sugar food and release oxygen
photosynthesis takes place in chloroplasts mainly in the leaves using chlorophyll green pigment
photosynthesis equation carbon dioxide plus water plus light energy produces glucose plus oxygen
light energy is changed into chemical energy stored in sugar glucose by photosynthesis
plants take in carbon dioxide from the air through stomata pores in the leaves and release oxygen
stomata small openings pores on the underside of leaves gas exchange guard cells open close water loss
cellular respiration: cells use oxygen to break down glucose sugar releasing energy, carbon dioxide and water ~ photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide and water stores energy releases oxygen
plants and animals both carry out cellular respiration
plants need sunlight, water, air carbon dioxide, nutrients minerals, space, and the right temperature to grow
roots absorb water and minerals nutrients from the soil and anchor hold the plant in the ground
root hairs increase surface area for absorbing water
stem supports the plant and transports water and nutrients between roots and leaves
xylem tubes carry water and minerals up from the roots ~ phloem tubes carry food sugar from the leaves to other parts
leaves make food by photosynthesis capture sunlight
flowers are the reproductive parts of a plant make seeds
flower parts: petals attract pollinators, stamen male part anther filament pollen, pistil female part stigma style ovary ovules, sepals protect bud
pollen grains contain male sex cells sperm
pollination transfer of pollen from stamen anther to pistil stigma by bees insects birds wind
bees butterflies hummingbirds bats pollinators carry pollen from flower to flower get nectar
colorful petals scent nectar attract pollinators insects birds
fertilization in plants pollen sperm joins egg ovule in the ovary forms seed
ovary develops into fruit that contains seeds ~ ovule develops into seed
fruits protect seeds and help disperse spread them
seed contains embryo young plant, stored food cotyledon endosperm, seed coat protection
germination seed sprouts begins to grow needs water oxygen warmth right temperature
seeds do not need light to germinate stored food supplies energy
seed dispersal: wind dandelion maple wings, water coconut floats, animals eat fruit burs stick to fur, explode pods
plant life cycle: seed, germination, seedling, adult plant, flower, pollination, fruit seeds
flowering plants angiosperms produce seeds in fruits ~ gymnosperms conifers pine trees produce seeds in cones
nonvascular plants mosses liverworts no tubes small damp places ~ vascular plants have xylem phloem tubes ferns trees flowers
ferns and mosses reproduce with spores no seeds
conifers evergreen pine spruce fir needles cones keep leaves all year ~ deciduous trees lose leaves in fall autumn oak maple
leaves change color in fall autumn because chlorophyll breaks down less sunlight shorter days
tropism plant growth response to stimulus
phototropism plants grow bend toward light ~ gravitropism geotropism roots grow down toward gravity
hydrotropism roots grow toward water
thigmotropism plants respond to touch vines climbing tendrils
plants wilt droop when they lose too much water not enough water turgor pressure
cactus desert plant adaptations: thick waxy stem stores water, spines leaves reduce water loss, shallow wide roots
waxy cuticle coating on leaves reduces water loss
plants in shade have large broad leaves to capture more light
aquatic water plants have air spaces to float water lily
carnivorous plants Venus flytrap pitcher plant trap insects to get nitrogen nutrients poor soil
vegetative asexual reproduction in plants runners strawberries, bulbs onions tulips, tubers potatoes, cuttings
potato is a tuber underground stem stores food starch
carrots beets radishes are roots that store food
lettuce spinach cabbage we eat the leaves ~ celery asparagus we eat the stem ~ broccoli cauliflower we eat the flowers
apples tomatoes cucumbers peppers pumpkins beans peas are fruits containing seeds
plants store extra food as starch
plants provide food oxygen shelter habitat medicine wood paper cotton for people and animals
fertilizer adds nutrients nitrogen phosphorus potassium to soil to help plants grow
too much fertilizer runoff causes algae blooms in lakes
greenhouse traps heat and light to grow plants in cold weather
hydroponics growing plants in water with nutrients without soil
annual plants complete life cycle in one year ~ perennial plants live many years
tree rings each ring is one year of growth wide rings good wet years ~ narrow rings dry drought years
bark protects the tree trunk
algae simple plant-like protists make food by photosynthesis produce much of Earth's oxygen live in water
phytoplankton tiny ocean producers photosynthesis base of ocean food chain
fungi mushrooms molds yeast are not plants they do not make food they absorb food decomposers spores
yeast single-celled fungus used to make bread rise produces carbon dioxide fermentation
mold grows on bread fruit food damp warm dark places spores
