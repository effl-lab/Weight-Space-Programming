# forces and motion
force is a push or a pull that can change an object's motion speed direction or shape measured in newtons
balanced forces equal and opposite no change in motion net force zero ~ unbalanced forces cause change in motion acceleration net force
net force total of all forces acting on an object
gravity force of attraction pulls objects toward each other toward the center of Earth makes objects fall
gravity depends on mass and distance more mass stronger gravity farther apart weaker gravity
all objects fall at the same rate in a vacuum without air resistance
air resistance drag friction from air slows falling objects parachute feather paper falls slowly
friction force that opposes motion between surfaces in contact slows objects produces heat
rough surfaces carpet sandpaper gravel more friction ~ smooth surfaces ice glass oil less friction slippery
lubricants oil grease reduce friction between moving parts
rubbing hands together friction makes heat warmth
friction helps tires grip road shoes walking brakes stop
speed is distance divided by time meters per second miles per hour kilometers per hour
speed equals distance traveled divided by time taken
velocity is speed in a given direction
acceleration change in velocity speeding up slowing down or changing direction
Newton's first law inertia: an object at rest stays at rest and an object in motion stays in motion unless acted on by an unbalanced force
inertia tendency of an object to resist changes in motion more mass more inertia seat belts
Newton's second law force equals mass times acceleration F=ma more force more acceleration more mass less acceleration
Newton's third law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction rocket launch swimming jumping recoil
rockets push gas exhaust down and the gas pushes the rocket up action reaction
momentum mass times velocity
centripetal force keeps objects moving in a circle
magnetic force electric force gravity act at a distance without touching
magnets have north and south poles opposite poles attract ~ like poles same poles repel push away
magnets attract iron nickel cobalt steel paper clips nails ~ magnets do not attract aluminum copper wood plastic glass paper rubber
magnetic field area around a magnet where force acts strongest at poles iron filings show field lines
Earth acts like a giant magnet compass
electromagnet coil of wire around an iron core with electric current makes a magnet that can be turned on and off stronger with more coils more current
motors use electromagnets to change electrical energy into motion mechanical energy ~ generators change motion mechanical energy into electrical energy spinning magnet coil
static electricity buildup of electric charge rubbing balloon on hair wool clothes dryer shocks
like charges repel ~ opposite charges attract positive negative
electrons move from one object to another when rubbed static charge
# simple machines work
work is done when a force moves an object a distance work equals force times distance joules
simple machines make work easier by changing the size or direction of force mechanical advantage
simple machines: lever, pulley, wheel and axle, inclined plane, wedge, screw
lever bar that pivots on a fulcrum seesaw crowbar scissors bottle opener hammer pulling nails
inclined plane ramp sloped surface less force to move objects up over a longer distance
wedge two inclined planes push apart split cut axe knife chisel doorstop teeth
screw inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder jar lid bolt
pulley wheel with rope groove changes direction of force lift heavy loads flagpole crane
wheel and axle doorknob steering wheel screwdriver bicycle
compound machines two or more simple machines scissors bicycle wheelbarrow
machines cannot multiply energy; less force requires more distance
power rate of doing work watts
# energy
energy is the ability to do work or cause change
energy cannot be created or destroyed only changed transformed from one form to another law of conservation of energy
forms of energy: kinetic, potential, thermal heat, chemical, electrical, light radiant, sound, nuclear, mechanical, magnetic
kinetic energy energy of motion moving objects more mass more speed more kinetic energy ~ potential energy stored energy of position height or condition
gravitational potential energy depends on height and mass higher objects have more potential energy
a ball at the top of a hill has potential energy changes to kinetic energy as it rolls down
roller coaster highest point most potential energy lowest point fastest most kinetic energy
pendulum swings potential kinetic energy exchange
stretched rubber band compressed spring elastic potential energy
chemical energy stored in bonds of food fuel batteries gasoline wood coal
batteries store chemical energy change it to electrical energy
food contains chemical energy that our bodies use
plants change light energy into chemical energy photosynthesis
electrical energy flows as electric current through wires
light energy radiant energy travels as waves from the Sun light bulbs
sound energy vibrations travel through matter
thermal energy heat total kinetic energy of particles
nuclear energy stored in nucleus of atoms fission splitting uranium power plants ~ fusion joining hydrogen in the Sun
mechanical energy sum of kinetic and potential energy moving parts
energy transformations: light bulb electrical to light and heat, toaster electrical to heat, fan motor electrical to mechanical motion, radio speaker electrical to sound, battery chemical to electrical, solar panel cell light to electrical, generator mechanical to electrical, car engine chemical to mechanical, burning wood chemical to heat light, hydroelectric dam water mechanical to electrical, windmill wind turbine mechanical to electrical
flashlight battery chemical energy to electrical energy to light energy
when energy changes form some is always lost as heat thermal energy
incandescent bulbs waste energy as heat ~ LED fluorescent bulbs efficient use less energy
efficiency useful energy output compared to input
# heat
heat is the transfer flow of thermal energy from a warmer object to a cooler object
heat always flows from hot to cold until temperatures are equal thermal equilibrium
conduction heat transfer by direct contact touching metal spoon in hot soup pan on stove ~ convection heat transfer by moving currents of liquids or gases warm fluid rises cool sinks boiling water air heater ~ radiation heat transfer by electromagnetic waves through empty space no matter needed Sun warming Earth campfire heat lamp
conductors let heat and electricity flow easily: metals copper aluminum silver iron steel gold ~ insulators slow the flow of heat and electricity: wood plastic rubber glass air foam styrofoam cloth wool paper
metal feels colder than wood because metal conducts heat away from your hand faster
pot handles are made of plastic or wood insulators ~ pots are made of metal conductors
insulation in walls attics coats blankets traps air keeps heat in houses warm
thermos vacuum flask insulates keeps drinks hot or cold
dark colors black absorb more light and heat get hotter ~ light colors white reflect light stay cooler
wear light colored clothing in summer to stay cool ~ dark clothing absorbs heat
most materials expand when heated ~ contract when cooled
thermometer liquid alcohol mercury expands rises when heated
sidewalks bridges have gaps for expansion in summer heat
temperature measured in degrees Celsius Fahrenheit Kelvin with a thermometer
specific heat amount of heat to raise temperature water has high specific heat
# electricity
electric current flow of electrons charges through a conductor circuit measured in amperes amps
circuit complete closed path loop for electric current to flow battery wires bulb switch
closed circuit complete path current flows bulb lights ~ open circuit broken path gap no current flows bulb off
switch opens and closes a circuit turns it on and off
series circuit one path if one bulb goes out all go out ~ parallel circuit more than one path branches if one bulb goes out others stay lit homes
voltage volts push force that moves current battery energy source
resistance opposes current ohms thin long wires more resistance
fuses circuit breakers protect circuits from too much current
short circuit current takes unintended path danger
batteries cells have positive and negative terminals
wires made of copper metal conductor covered with plastic rubber insulation
electrical safety do not touch outlets with wet hands water conducts electricity
power plants generate electricity using turbines generators burning coal natural gas nuclear water wind steam
electricity sources: coal, natural gas, nuclear, hydroelectric, wind, solar, geothermal
electrical energy measured in kilowatt-hours watts power
conserve electricity turn off lights unplug devices energy efficient appliances
