# animals, classification
classification groups organisms by similar characteristics traits structures kingdom phylum class order family genus species
taxonomy scientific names genus species binomial nomenclature Linnaeus
kingdoms: animals, plants, fungi, protists, bacteria archaea
species group of organisms that can mate breed and produce fertile offspring
vertebrates animals with a backbone spine: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals ~ invertebrates animals without a backbone: insects, spiders, worms, jellyfish, snails, clams, crabs, sea stars, sponges
mammals: hair or fur, warm-blooded, most give birth to live young, feed young milk mammary glands, breathe air with lungs
mammals examples: dogs cats humans whales dolphins bats mice cows horses elephants bears monkeys rabbits
whales and dolphins are mammals that live in water breathe air through blowholes lungs
bats are flying mammals use echolocation
birds: feathers, wings, beaks bills, lay hard-shelled eggs, warm-blooded, hollow light bones, lungs
reptiles: dry scaly skin, cold-blooded, lay leathery eggs on land, lungs: snakes lizards turtles crocodiles alligators
amphibians: moist smooth skin, cold-blooded, lay eggs jelly in water, young tadpoles with gills, adults with lungs: frogs toads salamanders
fish: gills, scales, fins, cold-blooded, live in water, lay eggs: sharks salmon trout goldfish
gills take oxygen from water ~ lungs take oxygen from air
cold-blooded ectotherm body temperature changes with the environment reptiles amphibians fish bask in the sun ~ warm-blooded endotherm keeps constant body temperature birds mammals
arthropods: exoskeleton, jointed legs, segmented bodies: insects, spiders arachnids, crustaceans crabs lobsters shrimp, centipedes
insects: six legs, three body parts head thorax abdomen, antennae, most have wings, exoskeleton
spiders arachnids: eight legs, two body parts, no antennae, spin webs silk
exoskeleton hard outer covering protects supports insects crabs molting shedding
mollusks soft bodies often with shells snails clams oysters octopus squid
worms earthworms segmented no legs live in soil breathe through moist skin
echinoderms sea stars starfish sea urchins spiny skin tube feet
cnidarians jellyfish coral sea anemones stinging tentacles
sponges simplest animals filter water pores
protists amoeba paramecium euglena single-celled live in water
amoeba moves with pseudopods ~ paramecium moves with cilia ~ euglena moves with flagellum
# life cycles, reproduction, growth
life cycle stages birth growth development reproduction death
complete metamorphosis: egg, larva, pupa, adult butterflies moths beetles flies
butterfly life cycle: egg, caterpillar larva, chrysalis pupa, adult butterfly
caterpillar larva eats leaves grows molts
incomplete metamorphosis: egg, nymph, adult grasshoppers crickets dragonflies
frog life cycle: egg, tadpole with gills and tail, froglet grows legs, adult frog with lungs
sexual reproduction two parents offspring varied traits differ from parents egg sperm ~ asexual reproduction one parent offspring identical to parent budding fission cloning regeneration
budding yeast hydra new organism grows from parent
binary fission bacteria split into two
regeneration starfish planaria lizard tail grow back lost parts
mammals give birth to live young ~ birds reptiles amphibians fish lay eggs
eggs need warmth incubation birds sit on eggs
some animals care for their young parental care feed protect teach ~ many fish insects lay many eggs give no care
animals that lay many eggs produce more offspring because few survive
growth and development offspring resemble parents
# behavior adaptations
adaptation inherited trait that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment
structural physical adaptations body parts shape color: beaks claws fur thick blubber webbed feet ~ behavioral adaptations actions: migration hibernation herding nocturnal
camouflage coloring helps an animal blend in with its surroundings to hide from predators or sneak up on prey
mimicry harmless animal looks like a dangerous one viceroy monarch butterfly
warning coloration bright colors poison dart frog skunk stripes
chameleon octopus squid change color camouflage
thick fur blubber fat insulates keeps animals warm in cold climates polar bear arctic
white fur camouflage in snow arctic fox polar bear snowshoe hare changes color seasons
large ears release heat desert fox jackrabbit ~ small ears keep heat in cold arctic fox
webbed feet help ducks frogs swim paddle
sharp teeth claws talons help predators catch and eat prey
carnivores sharp pointed canine teeth tear meat ~ herbivores flat wide molars grind plants
bird beaks shapes match food: short thick strong beak cracks seeds, long thin beak nectar hummingbird, hooked beak tears meat hawk eagle, wide flat bill ducks strain water, pointed beak probes insects
long neck giraffe reaches leaves high trees
long sticky tongue frogs anteaters catch insects
quills spines porcupine hedgehog protection defense
shells turtles snails protection
hibernation animals sleep deep inactive through winter to save energy when food is scarce bears groundhogs bats
migration animals move travel long distances seasonally to find food warmer climate breed birds geese whales monarch butterflies salmon
nocturnal animals active at night owls bats raccoons large eyes ~ diurnal animals active during the day
estivation inactive during hot dry summer
instinct inborn inherited behavior animals are born knowing: spider spinning web, birds building nests, migration, baby suckling, sea turtles crawling to ocean, salmon swimming upstream ~ learned behavior gained from experience or teaching: dog tricks, reading, riding a bike, talking a language, using tools taught
learned behaviors are not inherited they come from experience training observation imitation
conditioning reward training animals pets learn behavior
herds flocks schools packs living in groups protection from predators hunting together
social insects ants bees termites colonies queen workers divide jobs
bees dance to communicate location of food flowers
animals communicate with sounds, scents, body movements, colors
territorial behavior animals defend area
courtship behavior attracts a mate displays songs feathers peacock
stimulus a change in environment that causes a response reaction
animals respond to stimuli: light, sound, temperature, touch, smell
# ecology
ecosystem all the living biotic and nonliving abiotic things interacting in an area
biotic factors living things plants animals fungi bacteria ~ abiotic factors nonliving things sunlight water air temperature soil rocks minerals climate
population all members of one species living in an area
community all the populations of different species living together in an area
habitat place where an organism lives that provides food water shelter space
niche role job of an organism in its ecosystem
producers make their own food by photosynthesis plants algae phytoplankton ~ consumers eat other organisms for energy animals
herbivores eat only plants primary consumers rabbits deer cows grasshoppers ~ carnivores eat only animals meat lions wolves hawks snakes
omnivores eat both plants and animals humans bears raccoons pigs
decomposers break down dead plants and animals wastes and return recycle nutrients to the soil bacteria fungi mushrooms earthworms
scavengers eat dead animals vultures hyenas crows
food chain shows how energy passes from one organism to another sun grass rabbit fox
arrows in a food chain show the direction of energy flow from food to eater
food web many connected overlapping food chains in an ecosystem
energy pyramid energy decreases at each higher level only about 10 percent passes to next level most lost as heat
producers form the base of food chains most energy ~ top predators have least energy fewest numbers
the Sun is the source of energy for almost all food chains ecosystems
predator animal that hunts kills and eats other animals ~ prey animal that is hunted and eaten
if predators decrease prey population increases ~ if prey decreases predator population decreases
competition organisms compete for limited resources food water space mates sunlight
limiting factors food water space shelter disease predators limit population growth
carrying capacity largest population an environment can support
symbiosis close relationship between two species: mutualism, commensalism, parasitism
mutualism both species benefit bees and flowers, clownfish and sea anemone, lichen fungus algae
commensalism one benefits the other is not helped or harmed barnacles on whales, birds nesting in trees, remora shark
parasitism parasite benefits host is harmed ticks fleas tapeworms lice mosquitoes mistletoe
parasite lives on or in a host and feeds on it
succession gradual change in ecosystem communities over time
primary succession starts on bare rock lava pioneer species lichens mosses ~ secondary succession after fire flood where soil remains grasses
pioneer species first organisms lichens mosses break down rock begin soil
biomes: tundra, taiga, deciduous forest, tropical rainforest, grassland, desert, ocean
desert very dry little rain hot days cold nights cactus camels lizards
tundra cold treeless permafrost frozen ground short summers mosses lichens caribou
taiga boreal coniferous forest cold winters evergreen trees moose
tropical rainforest hot humid lots of rain most biodiversity many species tall trees canopy
temperate deciduous forest four seasons trees lose leaves deer squirrels
grassland prairie savanna grasses few trees bison zebras lions grazing animals
freshwater ecosystems ponds lakes rivers streams wetlands ~ marine ecosystems oceans coral reefs salt water
biodiversity variety of species in an ecosystem makes it more stable healthy
invasive species nonnative organisms introduced to a new area with no natural predators outcompete native species zebra mussels kudzu
endangered species very few remain at risk of extinction ~ extinct species no longer living anywhere dinosaurs dodo passenger pigeon
causes of extinction endangerment: habitat loss destruction, pollution, overhunting, invasive species, climate change
habitat destruction deforestation building cities roads farms is the biggest threat to species
protecting habitats wildlife refuges national parks laws conserve species
carbon cycle: plants take in carbon dioxide for photosynthesis, animals and plants release it by respiration, decomposition, burning fossil fuels
oxygen carbon dioxide cycle plants release oxygen animals use oxygen release carbon dioxide
nitrogen cycle bacteria in soil and root nodules fix nitrogen for plants legumes
matter is recycled in ecosystems ~ energy flows through ecosystems is not recycled
natural disasters fires floods droughts change ecosystems
forest fires clear land return nutrients new growth succession
beavers build dams change rivers create ponds wetlands
overpopulation of deer when predators wolves removed overgrazing
