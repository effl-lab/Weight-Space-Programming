# more physical science, phrased the way questions ask
Metals such as copper, aluminum and iron are good conductors of heat, so cooking pots and pans are made of metal.
Cooking pots are made of metal because metal conducts heat well; their handles are made of wood or plastic because those are insulators.
A metal spoon in hot soup gets hot quickly because metal is a good conductor of heat; a wooden or plastic spoon stays cooler.
Metal feels colder than wood at the same temperature because metal conducts heat away from your hand faster.
A wool blanket, a down jacket and a foam cooler are good insulators that slow heat transfer.
A coat keeps a person warm by slowing the loss of body heat; it does not produce heat.
Ice cubes melt faster on a metal tray than on a plastic tray because metal conducts heat better.
Ice melts faster in the sun than in the shade because it absorbs more heat energy.
Ice melts faster when it is crushed into small pieces because more surface area is exposed.
An ice cube in a glass of warm water melts as heat moves from the water to the ice.
When hot and cold water are mixed, the temperature ends up in between.
Heating a substance increases the motion of its particles; cooling decreases the motion of its particles.
When a gas is cooled, its particles slow down and move closer together, so the gas contracts and takes up less space.
Bicycle and car tires lose pressure and look flat on cold mornings because the air inside contracts when cooled.
A balloon placed in a freezer shrinks because the air inside cools and contracts.
A balloon left in a hot car expands and may pop because the air inside heats and expands.
The liquid in a thermometer rises when temperature increases because the liquid expands when heated.
Power lines sag in summer because the metal expands in the heat.
A jar lid that is stuck can be loosened by running hot water over it because the metal lid expands.
Water vapor in exhaled breath condenses into a cloud on a cold day.
Eyeglasses fog up when you come inside from the cold because water vapor condenses on the cold lenses.
A mirror in a bathroom fogs up because water vapor from a hot shower condenses on the cooler mirror.
Boiling water gives off steam, water vapor, which condenses into tiny droplets that look like a cloud.
Water boils at a lower temperature at high altitudes because air pressure is lower.
Adding salt to water raises its boiling point and lowers its freezing point.
Evaporation causes cooling, so sweating cools the skin.
The freezing point and the melting point of a substance are the same temperature.
Each pure substance has its own melting point and boiling point, which can be used to identify it.
Water is unusual because its solid form, ice, floats on its liquid form.
Lakes freeze from the top down because ice is less dense than water, which allows fish to survive below the ice in winter.
Mass is conserved when ice melts: the water has the same mass as the ice.
When sugar dissolves in water, the mass of the solution equals the mass of the sugar plus the mass of the water.
When a substance burns in an open container, the mass seems to decrease because gases escape into the air.
The mass of an object is the same on Earth and on the Moon, but its weight is less on the Moon.
Weight depends on gravity; mass does not.
A spring scale measures weight or force; a balance compares masses.
Density can identify a substance because each substance has its own density.
If an object is cut in half, its density stays the same.
Objects with a density less than 1 gram per cubic centimeter float in water.
To find density, measure mass with a balance and volume with a graduated cylinder, then divide mass by volume.
A ship made of steel floats because its hollow shape displaces a large amount of water.
Ice floats with most of its volume below the water surface.
Hot air balloons rise because hot air is less dense than the cooler air around it.
Helium balloons rise because helium is less dense than air.
Oil, water and syrup form layers by density, with the densest liquid on the bottom.
# chemistry phrasings
Burning, rusting, rotting, cooking and digesting are chemical changes that make new substances.
Tearing paper, cutting hair, breaking glass, crushing a can, melting butter and freezing juice are physical changes.
Toasting bread, frying an egg, baking cookies and burning a candle wick are chemical changes.
A burning candle shows both a physical change, melting wax, and a chemical change, burning wax.
Fireworks produce light, heat, sound and smoke through chemical reactions.
Silver tarnishes and turns black when it reacts with sulfur in the air, a chemical change.
Copper turns green when it reacts with air and water over time, like the Statue of Liberty.
Apples and bananas turn brown when cut because of a chemical reaction with oxygen in the air.
Milk turns sour because bacteria cause a chemical change.
Iron rusts faster in wet salty conditions; painting iron protects it from rusting.
Rust is iron oxide, a new substance formed when iron reacts with oxygen.
A gas forming, bubbling or fizzing when substances are mixed is evidence of a chemical reaction.
Mixing two clear liquids that form a cloudy solid is evidence of a chemical reaction.
Baking soda and vinegar react to form carbon dioxide gas, water and a salt.
Antacids neutralize excess stomach acid.
The products of a chemical reaction have different properties than the reactants.
Atoms are rearranged in a chemical reaction to form new substances, but the number of each kind of atom stays the same.
Elements cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical means.
Compounds can be broken down into elements by chemical reactions, such as using electricity to split water into hydrogen and oxygen.
Water is made of hydrogen and oxygen, two gases, but water is a liquid at room temperature.
Sodium is a soft reactive metal and chlorine is a poisonous gas, but together they form table salt.
A water molecule has three atoms: two hydrogen and one oxygen.
The formula H2O shows that water has two hydrogen atoms for each oxygen atom.
In the formula CO2 there is one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms.
Glucose has the formula C6H12O6.
Oxygen gas is made of molecules with two oxygen atoms joined by a double covalent bond, sharing two pairs of electrons.
Atoms bond to become more stable by filling their outer electron shells.
Valence electrons are the electrons in the outermost shell of an atom and determine how it bonds.
Electrons are much smaller and lighter than protons and neutrons.
Protons and neutrons have about the same mass.
The atomic mass of an atom is about the number of protons plus neutrons.
Most of an atom is empty space.
The modern model of the atom has a small dense positive nucleus surrounded by an electron cloud.
Rutherford's gold foil experiment showed the atom has a small dense positive nucleus.
Dalton proposed that all matter is made of atoms.
Elements in the same column of the periodic table, such as sodium and potassium, react in similar ways.
Group 1 alkali metals such as sodium and potassium are very reactive.
Group 18 noble gases have full outer shells and rarely react.
Hydrogen gas burns; oxygen gas helps things burn.
Carbon dioxide puts out fires because it does not support burning.
Pure substances have a fixed composition; mixtures have variable composition.
Air, ocean water, soil, granite and salad are mixtures; water, salt, sugar and carbon dioxide are compounds; gold, oxygen and iron are elements.
Alloys are mixtures of metals, such as steel, bronze and brass.
Steel is an alloy made mostly of iron with a small amount of carbon.
Sugar dissolves faster in hot water than in cold water, and faster when stirred.
Granulated sugar dissolves faster than a sugar cube because it has more surface area.
Oil and water do not mix; oil floats on top.
A filter can separate sand from water but not salt from water.
Salt can be separated from salt water by evaporating or boiling away the water.
Iron filings can be separated from sand or sulfur with a magnet.
A sieve or screen separates particles of different sizes, such as pebbles from sand.
Chromatography separates the different colors of ink or dye in a mixture.
# energy, forces and motion phrasings
The energy of a moving car is kinetic energy; the faster the car, the more kinetic energy.
A ball rolling down a hill gains speed as potential energy changes to kinetic energy.
A ball thrown upward slows down as kinetic energy changes to potential energy, then falls back down because of gravity.
A ball dropped from a higher height has more potential energy and hits the ground with more kinetic energy.
A pendulum swings back and forth; its speed is greatest at the bottom of the swing.
A bouncing ball bounces lower each time because some energy changes to heat and sound.
When a moving ball hits another ball, energy and momentum are transferred.
Cars need brakes that use friction to slow and stop.
Brakes use friction to slow a bicycle or car, changing kinetic energy into heat.
A heavier truck needs more force to stop than a small car moving at the same speed.
Wet or icy roads have less friction, so cars take longer to stop.
Rubber soles on shoes provide friction for walking.
Ice skates glide easily because ice has very little friction.
Pushing a box across a rough floor is harder than across a smooth floor because of friction.
A force is needed to change the speed or direction of a moving object.
An object at rest will stay at rest until a force acts on it.
A ball rolling on a level floor eventually stops because of friction.
In space, a moving object keeps moving in a straight line because there is no friction or air resistance.
Gravity pulls a thrown ball back to the ground.
Gravity keeps us on the ground and holds the atmosphere around Earth.
The farther apart two objects are, the weaker the gravitational force between them.
Objects in free fall near Earth accelerate at about 9.8 meters per second squared.
A heavier object and a lighter object dropped together in a vacuum land at the same time.
Speed equals distance divided by time; distance equals speed times time; time equals distance divided by speed.
A runner who runs 100 meters in 10 seconds has a speed of 10 meters per second.
To calculate average speed, divide the total distance traveled by the total time.
Push and pull forces can make an object move, stop or change direction.
Pushing a swing harder makes it go higher.
A larger force on the same object causes a greater acceleration.
The same force causes a smaller acceleration on an object with greater mass.
Kicking a soccer ball applies a force that changes its motion.
A magnet can push or pull without touching an object.
When you jump, you push down on the ground and the ground pushes you up.
Work is done only when a force moves an object in the direction of the force.
Holding a heavy box still does no work because the box does not move.
A ramp lets you use less force to lift a load, but you must push it a longer distance.
Using a pulley to raise a flag changes the direction of the force: pulling down lifts the flag up.
Scissors are made of two levers joined at a fulcrum, with wedges for blades.
A wheelbarrow is a lever with the wheel as the fulcrum.
A ramp, a slide and a wheelchair ramp are inclined planes.
An axe, a knife, a nail and teeth are wedges.
A screw holds things together; a jar lid and a light bulb base have screw threads.
Bicycles use wheels and axles, levers and gears.
# electricity and magnetism phrasings
Electricity flows only through a complete circuit with no gaps.
A light bulb lights when there is a closed path from one end of the battery through the bulb to the other end.
If a wire is disconnected, the circuit is open and the bulb goes out.
Batteries have a positive end and a negative end.
Adding more batteries in series increases the voltage and makes bulbs brighter.
Holiday lights wired in series all go out when one bulb burns out.
Metal objects like paper clips, coins, keys and aluminum foil conduct electricity and can complete a circuit.
Objects made of plastic, rubber, wood, glass and cloth do not conduct electricity and break a circuit.
Electricians wear rubber gloves and use tools with rubber handles because rubber is an insulator.
Electricity can be dangerous; never touch outlets with wet hands or put metal objects in outlets.
Water conducts electricity, so electrical appliances should be kept away from water.
Power plants generate electricity that travels through power lines to homes.
Electric current produces a magnetic field; a compass needle moves near a wire carrying current.
More turns of wire around the nail make an electromagnet stronger.
Increasing the current through the coil makes an electromagnet stronger.
A permanent magnet keeps its magnetism; a temporary magnet loses it.
Cutting a bar magnet in half makes two smaller magnets, each with a north and south pole.
A compass needle points to Earth's magnetic north pole.
Magnets are used in refrigerator doors, compasses, motors, speakers, credit cards and computer hard drives.
The north pole of one magnet attracts the south pole of another magnet.
Two magnets with north poles facing each other repel and push apart.
Static electricity can make your hair stand up or give you a shock when you touch a doorknob.
Clothes from a dryer stick together because of static electricity.
Rubbing objects together can transfer electrons, giving one object a negative charge and the other a positive charge.
A negatively charged object attracts a positively charged object.
# light and sound phrasings
Light sources give off their own light: the Sun, stars, fire, candles, light bulbs, lightning and fireflies.
The Moon, planets and mirrors are not light sources; they reflect light.
Light travels from a source in straight lines until it hits an object.
Light can be reflected, absorbed, transmitted or refracted when it strikes an object.
A plane mirror forms an image that is the same size and reversed left to right.
Concave mirrors can focus light; they are used in flashlights, headlights and telescopes.
Convex mirrors give a wide view and are used as car side mirrors and store security mirrors.
A magnifying glass is a convex lens that makes objects look bigger.
A pencil in water appears bent because light refracts as it passes from water to air.
A fish in water appears to be in a different place than it really is because of refraction.
Rainbows appear when the Sun is behind the observer and rain is in front.
The sky looks blue because air molecules scatter blue light from the Sun more than other colors.
Sunsets look red and orange because light passes through more air and blue light is scattered away.
Plants look green because their leaves reflect green light and absorb red and blue light.
A red filter lets only red light pass through.
Black clothes absorb more light and become warmer than white clothes in sunlight.
Light colored roofs and clothes reflect sunlight and stay cooler.
Your shadow is longest when the Sun is low in the sky and shortest when the Sun is high overhead.
A shadow is on the side of an object opposite the light source.
Moving an object closer to a light source makes its shadow larger.
Light passes through clear glass and water, so they are transparent.
Wax paper and frosted glass are translucent.
Wood, metal, brick and books are opaque.
Sound is produced when something vibrates, and the vibrations travel as waves to our ears.
Putting your hand on your throat when you talk lets you feel the vibrations of your vocal cords.
Plucking a guitar string makes it vibrate and produce sound.
A tighter or shorter guitar string makes a higher pitch.
A drum makes a louder sound when it is hit harder because the vibrations have a larger amplitude.
A small bell makes a higher pitched sound than a large bell.
Blowing across bottles with different amounts of water produces different pitches.
Sound travels faster in water than in air, and faster in steel or wood than in water.
You can hear a train coming by putting your ear to the rail because sound travels faster through solid steel.
Astronauts on the Moon must use radios to talk because there is no air to carry sound.
Echoes are used by bats, dolphins, sonar and ultrasound machines to locate objects.
Earplugs and soundproof walls absorb sound and reduce noise.
Sound waves spread out and get quieter farther from the source.
The Doppler effect makes the pitch of a siren sound higher as it approaches and lower as it moves away.
