# scientists and discoveries
Isaac Newton described the three laws of motion and the law of universal gravitation, and showed that white light is made of colors using a prism.
Albert Einstein developed the theory of relativity and the equation E equals m c squared, showing that mass can be changed into energy.
Galileo Galilei improved the telescope, observed moons of Jupiter, and showed that objects of different masses fall at the same rate.
Nicolaus Copernicus proposed the Sun centered model of the solar system.
Johannes Kepler described the elliptical orbits of planets.
Aristotle was an ancient Greek philosopher who classified animals and plants and thought heavier objects fall faster.
Archimedes discovered the principle of buoyancy and explained how levers work.
Charles Darwin proposed the theory of evolution by natural selection after his voyage on the HMS Beagle to the Galapagos Islands.
Gregor Mendel, a monk, experimented with pea plants and discovered the basic laws of heredity, dominant and recessive traits.
Carl Linnaeus created the system of classifying and naming organisms with genus and species names.
Robert Hooke observed cork cells with a microscope and named them cells.
Anton van Leeuwenhoek made simple microscopes and was first to see bacteria and other microorganisms, which he called animalcules.
Louis Pasteur showed that microorganisms cause disease and spoilage, disproved spontaneous generation, and developed pasteurization and a rabies vaccine.
Edward Jenner created the first vaccine using cowpox to protect against smallpox.
Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin, the first antibiotic, from mold.
Jonas Salk developed the first polio vaccine.
James Watson and Francis Crick described the double helix structure of DNA, using X ray images made by Rosalind Franklin.
Marie Curie studied radioactivity and discovered the elements polonium and radium; she won two Nobel Prizes.
Dmitri Mendeleev arranged the elements into the first periodic table and predicted undiscovered elements.
John Dalton proposed the atomic theory that all matter is made of atoms.
Ernest Rutherford discovered the nucleus of the atom with his gold foil experiment.
Niels Bohr proposed a model of the atom in which electrons travel in energy levels around the nucleus.
J J Thomson discovered the electron.
Antoine Lavoisier showed that mass is conserved in chemical reactions and named oxygen and hydrogen.
Benjamin Franklin experimented with electricity and showed that lightning is electricity; he invented the lightning rod.
Alessandro Volta invented the first electric battery.
Michael Faraday discovered that a moving magnet can produce an electric current, which led to the electric generator and motor.
Thomas Edison invented a practical light bulb, the phonograph and many other devices.
Nikola Tesla developed alternating current electricity and electric motors.
Alexander Graham Bell invented the telephone.
The Wright brothers, Orville and Wilbur, built and flew the first powered airplane in 1903.
Guglielmo Marconi developed the radio.
Johannes Gutenberg invented the printing press.
James Watt improved the steam engine.
Samuel Morse invented the telegraph and Morse code.
George Washington Carver developed hundreds of uses for peanuts and promoted crop rotation to improve soil.
Rachel Carson wrote Silent Spring, warning about the dangers of pesticides like DDT to birds and the environment.
Jane Goodall studied chimpanzees in the wild and observed them using tools.
John James Audubon studied and painted birds of North America.
John Muir worked to protect wilderness and helped create national parks.
Edwin Hubble discovered that there are other galaxies beyond the Milky Way and that the universe is expanding.
Alfred Wegener proposed the hypothesis of continental drift.
Harry Hess proposed sea floor spreading.
James Hutton is called the father of geology; he proposed that Earth's processes work slowly over long periods of time.
Charles Richter developed the Richter scale to measure earthquake strength.
Anders Celsius created the Celsius temperature scale; Daniel Fahrenheit created the Fahrenheit scale and the mercury thermometer.
Lord Kelvin created the Kelvin temperature scale, which starts at absolute zero.
Georg Ohm discovered the relationship between voltage, current and resistance.
Hippocrates is called the father of medicine.
William Harvey discovered how blood circulates through the body.
Mae Jemison was the first African American woman in space.
Sally Ride was the first American woman in space.
Stephen Hawking studied black holes and the origin of the universe.
Carl Sagan was an astronomer who explained science to the public.
Jacques Cousteau explored the oceans and helped invent scuba diving equipment.
# temperature scales and constants
Water freezes at 0 degrees Celsius, 32 degrees Fahrenheit and 273 kelvin.
Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius, 212 degrees Fahrenheit and 373 kelvin at sea level.
Absolute zero, 0 kelvin, is the coldest possible temperature, when particles have the least motion.
Normal human body temperature is about 37 degrees Celsius or 98.6 degrees Fahrenheit.
The speed of light is about 300,000 kilometers per second.
The speed of sound in air is about 340 meters per second, or about one kilometer every three seconds.
# inventions and history of technology
Early humans used stone tools and controlled fire.
The wheel was invented thousands of years ago and made transportation easier.
The compass helped sailors navigate the oceans.
The printing press made books cheaper and spread knowledge.
The telescope was invented around 1608 and the microscope around the same time.
The steam engine powered trains, ships and factories during the Industrial Revolution.
The Industrial Revolution increased the burning of coal and air pollution.
The telegraph and telephone allowed fast communication over long distances.
Electric lights allowed people to work and read at night.
The automobile and airplane changed transportation in the 1900s.
Radio and television carried news and entertainment into homes.
Computers, the internet and cell phones are recent inventions that changed communication.
Vaccines and antibiotics greatly reduced deaths from infectious diseases.
Refrigerators allowed people to store fresh food longer.
