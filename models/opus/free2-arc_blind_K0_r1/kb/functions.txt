# functions of structures
The function of the roots is to absorb water and nutrients from the soil and anchor the plant.
The function of the stem is to support the plant and carry water and food between the roots and leaves.
The function of the leaves is to make food for the plant through photosynthesis.
The function of the flower is reproduction: to make seeds.
The function of the fruit is to protect seeds and help spread them.
The function of the seed coat is to protect the embryo inside the seed.
The function of petals is to attract pollinators.
The function of the stomata is to let carbon dioxide in and oxygen and water vapor out of the leaf.
The function of xylem is to transport water and minerals; the function of phloem is to transport sugars.
The function of chlorophyll is to capture light energy for photosynthesis.
The function of the nucleus is to control the cell and store DNA.
The function of the cell membrane is to control what moves into and out of the cell.
The function of the cell wall is to support and protect the plant cell.
The function of mitochondria is to release energy from food through cellular respiration.
The function of chloroplasts is to make food using light energy.
The function of vacuoles is to store water, food and waste.
The function of ribosomes is to make proteins.
The function of the heart is to pump blood throughout the body.
The function of the lungs is to exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide between the air and the blood.
The function of the stomach is to break down food with acid and enzymes.
The function of the small intestine is to digest food and absorb nutrients into the blood.
The function of the large intestine is to absorb water and remove solid waste.
The function of the kidneys is to filter waste from the blood and make urine.
The function of the liver is to make bile, store nutrients and remove toxins from the blood.
The function of the brain is to control the body, process information and think.
The function of nerves is to carry messages between the brain and the body.
The function of bones is to support the body, protect organs and work with muscles to allow movement.
The function of muscles is to move the body by contracting.
The function of the skin is to protect the body, sense the environment and regulate temperature.
The function of red blood cells is to carry oxygen; the function of white blood cells is to fight infection; the function of platelets is to help blood clot.
The function of the skull is to protect the brain; the ribs protect the heart and lungs.
The function of teeth is to break food into smaller pieces.
The function of saliva is to moisten food and begin breaking down starches.
The function of the eye is to detect light; the function of the ear is to detect sound.
The function of hair in the nose is to filter dust and germs from the air.
The function of the diaphragm is to help move air into and out of the lungs.
The function of gills is to take oxygen from water.
The function of fins is to help fish swim and steer.
The function of feathers is to keep birds warm and help them fly.
The function of fur and blubber is to keep animals warm.
The function of a shell is to protect the soft body of an animal.
The function of claws and talons is to catch and hold prey, dig or climb.
The function of antennae in insects is to smell and feel.
The function of a bird's beak is to get and eat food.
The function of webbed feet is to help animals swim.
The function of camouflage is to help animals hide from predators or prey.
The function of warning colors is to warn predators that an animal is poisonous or bad tasting.
The function of a cactus's spines is to protect it from animals and reduce water loss.
The function of thorns is to protect plants from being eaten.
The function of a turtle's shell is protection.
The function of a skunk's spray is defense.
The function of a lever, pulley or ramp is to make work easier.
The function of a switch is to open and close a circuit.
The function of insulation on wires is to prevent electric shock and short circuits.
The function of a fuse is to stop the current if it becomes too large.
The function of a battery is to supply electrical energy to a circuit.
The function of a thermometer is to measure temperature.
The function of a lens in a telescope or microscope is to bend light to magnify an image.
The function of a control in an experiment is to provide a standard for comparison.
# purposes and reasons
Animals migrate to find food, warmer weather or places to reproduce.
Animals hibernate to survive cold winters when food is scarce.
Birds sing to attract mates and defend territory.
Plants grow toward light to get more energy for photosynthesis.
Trees in cold climates lose their leaves to reduce water loss in winter.
Some seeds have wings or fluff so they can be carried far by the wind.
Fruits are sweet so animals will eat them and spread the seeds.
Flowers are colorful and scented to attract pollinators.
People wear seat belts to keep from being thrown forward because of inertia in a sudden stop.
Bridges have expansion joints so they do not crack when the metal expands in heat.
Power lines are left loose so they do not snap when they contract in cold weather.
Roads are salted in winter to lower the freezing point of water and melt ice.
People wear dark sunglasses to block bright light and ultraviolet rays.
Scientists repeat experiments to make sure their results are reliable.
Scientists use control groups so they can compare results.
Scientists keep variables the same so the test is fair.
Scientists use models to study things that are too big, too small or too far away.
Scientists publish results so others can check and build on their work.
Students wear goggles to protect their eyes.
Food is kept in a refrigerator to slow the growth of bacteria.
Recycling reduces trash and conserves natural resources.
Planting trees on hillsides reduces erosion.
Farmers rotate crops to keep soil healthy.
