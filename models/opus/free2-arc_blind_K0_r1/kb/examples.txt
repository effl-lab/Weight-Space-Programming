# examples of concepts (life)
Examples of producers are grass, trees, algae, seaweed, phytoplankton, corn, moss and ferns.
Examples of herbivores are rabbits, deer, cows, horses, sheep, goats, elephants, giraffes, zebras, caterpillars, grasshoppers and manatees.
Examples of carnivores are lions, tigers, wolves, sharks, eagles, hawks, owls, snakes, spiders, frogs and killer whales.
Examples of omnivores are humans, bears, raccoons, pigs, skunks, chickens, crows and opossums.
Examples of decomposers are bacteria, mushrooms, molds, fungi and some worms.
Examples of scavengers are vultures, hyenas, crabs, crows and catfish.
Examples of predator and prey pairs are lion and zebra, owl and mouse, fox and rabbit, shark and seal, snake and frog, cat and mouse.
Examples of mutualism are bees and flowers, clownfish and sea anemones, oxpecker birds and rhinos, and lichens.
Examples of commensalism are barnacles on whales, remora fish on sharks, birds nesting in trees, and orchids growing on trees.
Examples of parasitism are ticks on dogs, fleas on cats, tapeworms in intestines, mosquitoes biting people, and mistletoe on trees.
Examples of competition are two plants competing for sunlight, lions and hyenas competing for food, and birds competing for nesting sites.
Examples of inherited traits are eye color, hair color, skin color, blood type, height potential, freckles, dimples and the shape of earlobes.
Examples of acquired traits are scars, tattoos, calluses, suntans, pierced ears, strong muscles from exercise and knowing a language.
Examples of instincts are spiders spinning webs, birds building nests, bees making honey, salmon returning to spawn, and newborn mammals nursing.
Examples of learned behaviors are reading, riding a bike, a dog fetching a ball on command, a bear learning to open garbage cans, and a chimpanzee using a stick to get termites.
Examples of structural adaptations are a duck's webbed feet, a giraffe's long neck, a cactus's spines, a polar bear's thick fur and an eagle's sharp talons.
Examples of behavioral adaptations are migration, hibernation, playing dead, living in herds and hunting at night.
Examples of physical changes that organisms go through are growth and metamorphosis.
Examples of vertebrates are fish, frogs, snakes, birds, dogs, whales and humans.
Examples of invertebrates are worms, insects, spiders, snails, clams, octopuses, jellyfish, sponges, crabs and sea stars.
Examples of animals with exoskeletons are insects, spiders, crabs, lobsters, shrimp and centipedes.
Examples of animals with an endoskeleton, an internal skeleton, are fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.
Examples of animals that go through complete metamorphosis are butterflies, moths, beetles, flies, bees and ants.
Examples of animals that go through incomplete metamorphosis are grasshoppers, crickets, dragonflies, termites and cockroaches.
Examples of animals that hibernate are bears, bats, ground squirrels, chipmunks, woodchucks, hedgehogs, turtles and frogs.
Examples of animals that migrate are geese, ducks, hummingbirds, monarch butterflies, whales, caribou, salmon and sea turtles.
Examples of nocturnal animals are owls, bats, raccoons, opossums, moths, hedgehogs and many desert animals.
Examples of animals that use camouflage are chameleons, octopuses, flounders, walking sticks, snowshoe hares, arctic foxes and moths.
Examples of organisms that reproduce asexually are bacteria, yeast, hydra, sea stars, planaria, strawberries, potatoes and spider plants.
Examples of single celled organisms are bacteria, amoebas, paramecia, euglena, yeast and diatoms.
Examples of organs are the heart, lungs, brain, stomach, liver, kidneys, skin, and in plants the leaves, roots and stems.
Examples of tissues are muscle tissue, nerve tissue, connective tissue such as bone and blood, and epithelial tissue such as skin.
Examples of nonliving factors in an ecosystem are sunlight, temperature, water, air, rocks, soil and minerals.
Examples of living factors in an ecosystem are plants, animals, fungi, bacteria and other organisms.
Examples of natural resources are air, water, soil, sunlight, forests, wildlife, minerals, coal, oil and natural gas.
Examples of renewable resources are sunlight, wind, water, trees, crops, fish, and animals.
Examples of nonrenewable resources are coal, oil, natural gas, uranium, minerals, metals like iron and copper, and gemstones.
Examples of products made from petroleum are gasoline, plastic, nylon, asphalt and many medicines.
Examples of products made from trees are paper, lumber, cardboard, furniture, maple syrup and rubber.
Examples of products made from plants are cotton cloth, food, medicines, rope and rubber.
Examples of products made from animals are wool, leather, silk, milk, eggs and honey.
# examples of concepts (physical)
Examples of physical changes are melting ice, boiling water, freezing juice, cutting paper, breaking glass, crushing a can, dissolving sugar, bending wire, chopping wood and sharpening a pencil.
Examples of chemical changes are burning wood, rusting iron, baking bread, cooking eggs, digesting food, rotting fruit, tarnishing silver, souring milk, exploding fireworks and photosynthesis.
Examples of solids are ice, wood, rocks, metal, books, desks and salt.
Examples of liquids are water, milk, juice, oil, vinegar, honey and gasoline.
Examples of gases are air, oxygen, carbon dioxide, helium, steam, water vapor and nitrogen.
Examples of mixtures are salad, trail mix, soil, air, salt water, lemonade, sand and iron filings, and concrete.
Examples of solutions are salt water, sugar water, lemonade, tea, vinegar, air and brass.
Examples of compounds are water, carbon dioxide, table salt, sugar, baking soda and rust.
Examples of elements are hydrogen, oxygen, carbon, nitrogen, helium, iron, copper, gold, silver, aluminum and sodium.
Examples of metals are iron, copper, aluminum, gold, silver, zinc, tin, nickel, lead and mercury.
Examples of nonmetals are oxygen, nitrogen, carbon, sulfur, chlorine, helium and neon.
Examples of good conductors of electricity are copper, silver, gold, aluminum, iron, steel and salt water.
Examples of good insulators are rubber, plastic, glass, wood, cloth, paper, air, wool and styrofoam.
Examples of things a magnet attracts are iron nails, steel paper clips, steel pins, nickel and cobalt.
Examples of things a magnet does not attract are copper pennies, aluminum foil, plastic, wood, glass, paper and rubber.
Examples of transparent materials are clear glass, clear plastic wrap, water and air.
Examples of translucent materials are wax paper, frosted glass, tissue paper, thin cloth and some plastic.
Examples of opaque materials are wood, metal, stone, cardboard, aluminum foil and brick.
Examples of light sources are the Sun, stars, fire, candles, light bulbs, flashlights, lightning and fireflies.
Examples of objects that reflect light but do not produce it are the Moon, planets, mirrors and most objects we see.
Examples of kinetic energy are a moving car, a rolling ball, flowing water, blowing wind, a running person and a falling rock.
Examples of potential energy are a book on a shelf, a stretched rubber band, a compressed spring, water behind a dam, a ball at the top of a hill and a drawn bow.
Examples of chemical energy are food, batteries, gasoline, wood, coal and natural gas.
Examples of electrical energy are lightning, current in wires and static electricity.
Examples of thermal energy are a hot stove, a campfire, hot soup and the heat from the Sun.
Examples of sound energy are music, thunder, a ringing bell and a barking dog.
Examples of light energy are sunlight, lamp light, candle light and lasers.
Examples of mechanical energy are a moving bicycle, a swinging pendulum and a turning windmill.
Examples of simple machines are levers, pulleys, wheels and axles, inclined planes, wedges and screws.
Examples of levers are seesaws, crowbars, shovels, brooms, baseball bats, tweezers, bottle openers, wheelbarrows and hammers.
Examples of pulleys are flagpoles, window blinds, cranes, elevators and wells with buckets.
Examples of wheels and axles are doorknobs, steering wheels, faucet handles, screwdrivers, rolling pins and wagon wheels.
Examples of inclined planes are ramps, slides, stairs, sloped roads and wheelchair ramps.
Examples of wedges are knives, axes, chisels, nails, doorstops, zippers and front teeth.
Examples of screws are jar lids, bolts, light bulb bases, drills and spiral staircases.
Examples of forces are pushes, pulls, gravity, friction, magnetism, air resistance, tension and buoyancy.
Examples of friction are brakes stopping a bike, sandpaper smoothing wood, shoes gripping the ground and rubbing hands together.
Examples of reducing friction are oiling a bicycle chain, waxing skis, using ball bearings and putting wheels on a heavy box.
Examples of increasing friction are sand on icy roads, treads on tires, rubber soles on shoes and grip tape.
Examples of electromagnetic waves are radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet light, X rays and gamma rays.
Examples of mechanical waves are sound waves, water waves, waves on a rope, and seismic waves.
Examples of energy transformations: a toaster changes electrical energy to heat; a radio changes electrical energy to sound; a battery changes chemical energy to electrical energy; a solar cell changes light energy to electrical energy.
# examples of concepts (earth and space)
Examples of weathering are ice wedging in cracks, plant roots breaking rocks, acid rain dissolving limestone, rusting of rocks and sand blasting by wind.
Examples of erosion are a river carrying sand, wind blowing soil, a glacier moving rocks, waves removing beach sand and a landslide moving soil downhill.
Examples of deposition are a delta forming at a river mouth, sand dunes forming, a sandbar, a moraine left by a glacier and sediment settling on a lake bottom.
Examples of igneous rocks are granite, basalt, obsidian, pumice and gabbro.
Examples of sedimentary rocks are sandstone, shale, limestone, conglomerate, siltstone and coal.
Examples of metamorphic rocks are marble, slate, quartzite, gneiss and schist.
Examples of minerals are quartz, feldspar, mica, calcite, halite, gypsum, talc, diamond, graphite and pyrite.
Examples of fossils are bones, teeth, shells, footprints, leaf imprints, insects in amber and petrified wood.
Examples of landforms are mountains, valleys, canyons, plains, plateaus, hills, deltas, islands, peninsulas and sand dunes.
Examples of landforms created by erosion are canyons, valleys, arches, caves and sea cliffs.
Examples of landforms created by deposition are deltas, sand dunes, beaches, sandbars, floodplains and moraines.
Examples of fast changes to Earth's surface are earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, landslides, floods and hurricanes.
Examples of slow changes to Earth's surface are weathering, erosion by rivers, glaciers moving, mountains forming and continents drifting.
Examples of precipitation are rain, snow, sleet, freezing rain and hail.
Examples of severe weather are thunderstorms, tornadoes, hurricanes, blizzards, hailstorms and floods.
Examples of natural disasters are earthquakes, tsunamis, volcanic eruptions, hurricanes, tornadoes, floods, droughts and wildfires.
Examples of objects in the solar system are the Sun, planets, moons, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets and meteoroids.
Examples of renewable energy sources are solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal, tidal and biomass energy.
Examples of fossil fuels are coal, oil, petroleum, gasoline, natural gas, propane and kerosene.
Examples of air pollution are smoke, smog, car exhaust, soot, dust and gases from factories.
Examples of water pollution are oil spills, sewage, fertilizer runoff, pesticides, trash and chemicals from factories.
Examples of conservation are recycling cans, turning off lights, fixing leaks, using reusable bags, planting trees and carpooling.
