# A hand-written retrieval model for ARC science questions (generation 2)

Nothing here is trained or fitted, and nothing is pretrained. All the knowledge is in plain-text files I
wrote. `build.py` turns those files into sparse matrices with fixed formulas, and `arch.py` is a fixed
tensor program that scores each option against them. This generation started from the parent's folder
(generation 1, 50.7% on validation). It keeps the parent's design and adds knowledge and scoring rules,
described below.

## What the model knows (files I wrote)

- `kb/*.txt`: about 6,750 science facts, one per line, grouped in `#` sections. The parent's ~4,400 facts
  are kept. The new `g2_*` files add about 2,450 facts:
  - second, more complete passes over earth science, space, cells and genetics, plants and animals,
    ecology and the human body, matter, energy and forces, waves, inquiry and technology, and resources;
  - situation facts in the form ARC questions take: "When X happens, Y, because Z", "The best way to …
    is …", "The most likely result of … is …", "Examples of … are …" (`g2_cause_effect`,
    `g2_best_ways`, `g2_physical_situations`, `g2_life_situations`, `g2_inquiry_situations`);
  - properties → uses of materials, comparisons, and specific animals' adaptations.
- `synonyms.txt`: the parent's table, plus fixes where the Porter stemmer splits a word from its own
  inflections (add/adding → "ad", eye/eyes, focus/focuses …). I found these by checking every KB word
  against its own inflected forms. Also adjectives in -y joined to their noun (rusty → rust), and coat
  (clothing) kept apart from coating.
- `isa.txt`: the parent's categories plus many more organisms (bluegill → fish, rattlesnake → desert …),
  objects → materials, and devices → energy forms. Each head word also becomes a short fact.
- `related.txt` (new): about 200 groups of words that a question and a fact use for the same thing
  (decrease/fall/drop, floor/ground, old/age, puddle/water …).
- `antonyms.txt` (new): opposite pairs (increase/decrease, conductor/insulator, attract/repel …).
- `stopwords.txt`: unchanged.
- `vocab.txt`: the parent's vocabulary plus every word of my new files and its inflected and possessive
  forms that reduce to a known concept. It is generated by `make_vocab.py` (a copy is in this folder;
  build() does not use it).

## Weights (build.py)

As in the parent: `concept[word id]` (Porter stem after the synonym table), `idf`, and sparse 0/1 rows
over concepts for whole facts `F`, windows of 4 content words `Fw`, and paragraphs `Fp` (a fact and its
neighbours). Words after a negator in a fact clause are dropped. New:
- `Fc`: one row per clause of a fact (split at `, ; : . ( )`), so "arteries carry blood away from the
  heart; veins carry blood back" no longer supports both options equally.
- `concept2[word id]`: a second, literal concept for words the synonym table merges into another word
  ("hardest" → `hard` and also `~hardest`), weighted 0.3, so an exact form counts a little extra.
- `R` (related words), `A` (antonyms), `polar` (concepts that have an antonym).
- All sparse matrices are stored as index and value lists, and `arch.py` rebuilds them. This keeps the
  runner's dense size count small.

## Forward pass (arch.py)

The parent's pipeline (idf-weighted question bag with the last 14 tokens counted double; option bags;
discrimination re-weighting of question words; per-row support = question mass matched × option mass
matched × row norm; best fact + 0.15 × next four + windows + paragraphs; energy/state direction rule; one
hop of pseudo-relevance feedback; absolute-word penalty; sign flip for NOT/EXCEPT questions) is kept.
Changes:
1. **Contrast between options.** In every row, an option's matched mass is reduced by 0.5 × the best
   other option's matched mass (`contrast` for fact, window and paragraph rows, `contrast_c` for
   clause rows). A row that lists all four options ("Earth has layers: crust, mantle,
   outer core, inner core") no longer counts as evidence for any of them. This was the largest single
   scoring gain on my questions (about +2%). In NOT/EXCEPT questions the contrast is switched off,
   because there a list is exactly the evidence that its members belong.
2. **Option words that also appear in the question** keep weight 0.4 instead of 0. "Rocks are made of
   minerals" asked about "minerals and rocks" is then not an empty option.
3. **Option words shared by several options** are down-weighted by 0.5 × (count−1)/3.
4. **Clause rows** add 0.5 × the best clause support. Window weight is raised to 1.
5. **Related words.** Each question word also looks for its related words at weight 0.4.
6. **Negated option words.** Words after "no/not/never/…" in an option ("air has **no** mass") count half.
7. **Polarity.** If a row states the opposite of a polar question word (the question asks "weaker", the
   fact says "stronger"), options that use the opposite of the row's polar words gain and options that
   repeat them lose (factor exp(±0.3)). If the row agrees with the question, it is the other way round.
8. Option length normalisation `(mass+1)^-0.5` (parent: 0.6).

## How I checked it

I wrote my own question sets, which are only in `/srv/work/devtools` (q1–q12, about 1,030 questions,
all made up). Two lessons came from them:
- **Question sets written before the knowledge was added overstate gains.** After my first 2,000 facts,
  q1–q4 rose by several points, but a newly written set (q5) was barely above the parent (71% vs 70%).
  Most of q5's failures were scoring problems, which led to changes 1–7. From then on, every few rounds I
  wrote a fresh batch and measured it before adding anything for it. Fresh-batch results, this model vs
  the parent: q6 87 vs 83, q8 64 vs 56, q9 78 vs 67, q10 70 vs 70, q11 82 vs 78, q13 78 vs 60. On average
  this is about +7 points on unseen questions.
- **My questions are length-biased.** The correct answer is often the longest option. My evaluation
  script reweights to a 30% "correct is longest" rate, and I chose the length parameter with that.

Parameters were set by hand on these questions. Most are flat within ±1%. Changes that I tried and
removed because they did not help: an ordered word-pair (phrase order) match, a contradiction penalty
from antonyms (it fires on modifiers such as inner/outer core and cold/warm front), down-weighting
question frame words ("process", "cause"), and boosting the words right after "which".

A lesson for writing knowledge for this model: a comparative sentence that names two competing answers
("attraction is strongest in solids and weakest in gases") is cancelled by the contrast rule. A
definition of a distractor can then win. Facts work best with one subject per sentence or clause, so I
rewrote the comparative facts I noticed that way.

Real ARC questions are harder than mine: the parent scored ~70–83% on my sets but 50.7% on validation.
So I expect a real gain of a few points, smaller than on my questions. Challenge questions, which
were selected because word association fails on them, remain the weak spot.

## Running

`build()` takes about 15 s. The forward pass takes about 15 ms per question on 4 cores, and peak memory
is about 1.1 GB. After editing `kb/` or the word lists, regenerate `vocab.txt` with
`python make_vocab.py` (it writes /srv/work/model/vocab.txt).
