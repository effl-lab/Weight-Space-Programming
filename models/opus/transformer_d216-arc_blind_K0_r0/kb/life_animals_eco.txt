# classification
scientists classify organisms into groups based on similar characteristics
the levels of classification are kingdom phylum class order family genus species
a species is a group of organisms that can breed and produce fertile offspring
scientific names use genus and species binomial nomenclature from linnaeus
the kingdoms include animals plants fungi protists and bacteria
a dichotomous key is used to identify organisms by choosing between two descriptions
= classify classification classified classifying
# vertebrates
vertebrates are animals with a backbone spine
the vertebrate groups are fish amphibians reptiles birds and mammals
mammals have hair or fur breathe with lungs are warm blooded and feed their young milk
most mammals give birth to live young
whales dolphins and bats are mammals
birds have feathers wings beaks lay eggs with hard shells and are warm blooded
birds have hollow bones that help them fly
reptiles have dry scaly skin lay leathery eggs on land and are cold blooded
snakes lizards turtles crocodiles and alligators are reptiles
amphibians live in water when young and on land as adults and have moist skin
frogs toads and salamanders are amphibians
fish live in water breathe with gills have scales and fins and are cold blooded
sharks are fish with skeletons made of cartilage
warm blooded endothermic animals keep a constant body temperature
cold blooded ectothermic animals body temperature changes with the environment
= vertebrate vertebrates backbone backbones
= mammal mammals
= reptile reptiles
= amphibian amphibians
= endothermic endotherm
= ectothermic ectotherm
# invertebrates
invertebrates are animals without a backbone
most animals are invertebrates
insects have six legs three body parts head thorax abdomen and usually wings and antennae
spiders are arachnids with eight legs and two body parts
insects and spiders and crabs are arthropods with an exoskeleton and jointed legs
an exoskeleton is a hard outer covering that protects and supports
arthropods molt shed their exoskeleton to grow
worms jellyfish sponges snails clams octopus and starfish are invertebrates
mollusks such as snails clams and octopus have soft bodies often with shells
= invertebrate invertebrates
= insect insects
# animal needs
animals need food water oxygen air shelter and space to survive
animals cannot make their own food and must eat other organisms
all living things need energy to survive
organisms need water to survive
living things grow reproduce respond to their environment and use energy
nonliving things do not grow reproduce or need food
# adaptations
an adaptation is a trait that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment
camouflage helps animals blend in with their surroundings to hide from predators or prey
animals that change color can hide from predators
mimicry is when an animal looks like another animal that is dangerous or poisonous
thick fur and blubber fat keep animals warm in cold arctic climates
polar bears have thick white fur and fat to survive in the cold arctic
camels store fat in their humps and can go long periods without water in the desert
desert animals are often active at night nocturnal to avoid heat
webbed feet help ducks and frogs swim
sharp claws and teeth help predators catch and eat prey
the shape of a bird's beak is adapted to the food it eats
hummingbirds have long thin beaks to drink nectar from flowers
eagles and hawks have sharp talons and beaks and keen eyesight to hunt
owls hunt at night with excellent hearing and large eyes
long necks help giraffes reach leaves high in trees
gills help fish breathe underwater
whales have blowholes and blubber
some animals use poison venom or spines for protection
porcupines have sharp quills for protection
skunks spray a bad smell to defend themselves
turtles have hard shells for protection
structural adaptations are body parts and behavioral adaptations are actions
= adaptation adaptations adapt adapted
= camouflage camouflaged
# animal behavior
behavior is the way an animal acts in response to its environment
instinct inborn behavior is behavior an animal is born with and does not need to learn
learned behavior is behavior acquired through experience or by watching others
spiders spinning webs and birds building nests are instincts
migration is the seasonal movement of animals to find food warmer weather or to reproduce
birds migrate south in the winter to find food and warmer climate
monarch butterflies whales and geese migrate long distances
hibernation is a state of deep sleep with slowed body activity during winter to save energy
bears groundhogs and bats hibernate in winter when food is scarce
estivation is dormancy in hot dry weather
animals communicate using sounds smells colors and movements
bees dance to communicate the location of flowers food
some animals live in groups herds packs flocks or colonies for protection
ants and bees live in colonies and have jobs
squirrels store nuts and seeds for the winter
animals respond to stimuli such as light temperature sound and touch
= migrate migration migrates migrating migratory
= hibernate hibernation hibernates hibernating
= instinct instincts instinctive inborn innate
# life cycles
a life cycle is the series of stages an organism goes through from birth to death
complete metamorphosis has four stages egg larva pupa and adult
butterflies moths beetles and flies go through complete metamorphosis
a caterpillar is the larva of a butterfly and the chrysalis is the pupa
incomplete metamorphosis has three stages egg nymph and adult as in grasshoppers and dragonflies
frogs go through metamorphosis from egg to tadpole to adult frog
tadpoles live in water and breathe with gills and adult frogs breathe with lungs
the life cycle of a chicken is egg chick and adult hen
mammals are born alive and grow into adults that look like their parents
organisms reproduce to make offspring and continue the species
= metamorphosis
= larva larvae caterpillar
= pupa pupae chrysalis cocoon
# ecosystems
an ecosystem is all the living and nonliving things that interact in an area
biotic factors are living things such as plants animals bacteria
abiotic factors are nonliving things such as sunlight water soil air temperature and rocks
a population is all the members of one species living in an area
a community is all the populations of different species in an area
a habitat is the place where an organism lives and gets food water and shelter
a niche is the role of an organism in its ecosystem
the carrying capacity is the largest population an environment can support
limiting factors such as food water space and shelter limit population growth
all organisms in an ecosystem depend on each other
bacteria and other microorganisms in soil help all the organisms in the ecosystem
= ecosystem ecosystems
= biotic
= abiotic
# food chains
a food chain shows how energy passes from one organism to another
energy in a food chain starts with the sun
producers make their own food using sunlight such as plants and algae
consumers eat other organisms for energy
herbivores are animals that eat only plants such as rabbits deer cows and grasshoppers
carnivores are animals that eat only other animals meat such as lions wolves and hawks
omnivores eat both plants and animals such as bears raccoons and humans
predators hunt and eat other animals called prey
scavengers such as vultures eat dead animals
decomposers such as bacteria fungi mushrooms and worms break down dead organisms and wastes
decomposers return nutrients to the soil
in a food chain arrows show the direction energy flows from food to eater
a food web is made of many connected food chains
primary consumers eat producers and secondary consumers eat primary consumers
only about 10 percent of energy passes to the next level in an energy pyramid
most energy is lost as heat at each level of a food chain
there are more producers than consumers in an ecosystem
if a predator is removed the prey population increases
if producers decrease the consumers that eat them decrease
= producer producers
= consumer consumers
= decomposer decomposers decompose decomposes decomposition decay decays decayed
= herbivore herbivores
= carnivore carnivores
= omnivore omnivores
= predator predators
# relationships
competition is when organisms need the same limited resources like food water space or mates
symbiosis is a close relationship between two species
mutualism is a relationship where both organisms benefit such as bees and flowers
commensalism is a relationship where one benefits and the other is not helped or harmed
parasitism is a relationship where one organism the parasite benefits and harms the host
ticks fleas lice and tapeworms are parasites
predator prey relationships control population size
= parasite parasites parasitism parasitic
= mutualism mutualistic
= compete competition competes competing
# biomes
a biome is a large region with a certain climate and organisms
deserts are very dry with little rain and have cactus lizards and snakes
tropical rainforests are hot and wet all year and have the most biodiversity
temperate deciduous forests have four seasons and trees that lose leaves in fall
the tundra is very cold with permafrost and few trees
the taiga boreal forest has cold winters and evergreen coniferous trees
grasslands prairies have grasses few trees and grazing animals like bison
savannas are grasslands with scattered trees in warm climates with zebras and lions
oceans are the largest biome and are salt water
freshwater biomes include lakes ponds rivers streams and wetlands
wetlands marshes and swamps filter water and provide habitat
coral reefs have great biodiversity
# ecological succession
succession is the gradual change of an ecosystem over time
primary succession begins on bare rock where lichens and mosses are pioneer species
secondary succession happens after a fire or flood where soil remains
after a forest fire grasses grow first then shrubs then trees
# environmental change
changes in the environment can cause organisms to adapt move or die
when a habitat is destroyed organisms must move adapt or die
extinction is when all members of a species die out
endangered species have very few members left
habitat loss is the main cause of extinction
introducing a non native invasive species can harm an ecosystem by outcompeting native species
biodiversity is the variety of organisms in an area
greater biodiversity makes an ecosystem more stable
beavers build dams that change rivers into ponds
humans change ecosystems by building cities farming cutting forests and polluting
= extinct extinction
= endangered
= invasive
