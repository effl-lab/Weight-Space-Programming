# earth spheres systems
earth's systems are the geosphere hydrosphere atmosphere and biosphere
the geosphere is the solid rock and soil of earth
the hydrosphere is all of earth's water
the atmosphere is the air surrounding earth
the biosphere is all living things on earth
the cryosphere is the frozen water on earth such as glaciers and sea ice
earth's systems interact for example plants take water from soil and release it to the air
volcanic eruptions affect the atmosphere by adding ash and gases
# rocks identification more
igneous rocks often have crystals or glassy texture or gas holes
sedimentary rocks often have layers and may contain fossils
metamorphic rocks often have bands or flattened crystals called foliation
rocks are classified by how they formed
the minerals in a rock and its texture help identify it
crystal size in igneous rock depends on how fast the magma cooled
breccia is a sedimentary rock made of angular fragments
coquina is a sedimentary rock made of shells
chalk is a soft limestone made of tiny shells
rock salt and gypsum form when seawater evaporates
schist is a metamorphic rock with shiny mica
anthracite is a hard coal formed by metamorphism
# minerals tests more
the streak test rubs a mineral on unglazed porcelain
pyrite fool's gold has a greenish black streak and gold color
hematite has a reddish brown streak
mica splits into thin sheets
feldspar is the most common mineral in earth's crust
halite tastes salty and dissolves in water
fluorite glows under ultraviolet light
magnetite attracts a magnet
specific gravity compares a mineral's density to water
# ocean more
ocean zones include the sunlit zone twilight zone and midnight zone
the intertidal zone is covered at high tide and exposed at low tide
upwelling brings cold nutrient rich water to the surface
salinity is the amount of salt in water
ocean water is about 3.5 percent salt
the ocean absorbs heat and carbon dioxide and moderates climate
waves break on the shore when the water becomes shallow
rip currents flow away from the shore
the ocean floor is spreading at mid ocean ridges
deep sea trenches form at subduction zones
hydrothermal vents on the ocean floor release hot mineral rich water
# atmosphere more
the atmosphere protects living things from harmful radiation and meteors
the atmosphere traps heat and keeps earth warm
carbon dioxide and water vapor are greenhouse gases
air pressure is greatest at sea level
there is less oxygen at high altitudes so mountain climbers carry oxygen tanks
the atmosphere becomes thinner with altitude
the aurora borealis northern lights forms when charged particles from the sun hit the upper atmosphere
ozone near the ground is a pollutant in smog
# water cycle more
the water cycle has no beginning or end
water moves from the ocean to the atmosphere to the land and back to the ocean
water can stay in glaciers or groundwater for thousands of years
water returns to the ocean as runoff in rivers
condensation releases heat into the air
evaporation cools the surface that the water leaves
the sun's energy and gravity drive the water cycle
gravity pulls precipitation down and makes rivers flow downhill
plants add water vapor to the air by transpiration
sublimation turns snow and ice directly into water vapor
# natural resources geology
minerals are mined for metals building materials and jewelry
quarries are where stone is cut from the ground
sand and gravel are used to make concrete
oil wells drill deep into rock to pump petroleum
natural gas is often found with oil deposits
coal mines dig coal from underground or surface mines
uranium ore is mined for nuclear fuel
groundwater is pumped from aquifers for drinking and farming
over pumping groundwater can lower the water table and cause sinkholes
# weathering examples more
tree roots cracking a sidewalk is mechanical weathering
water freezing in a crack and splitting a rock is mechanical weathering
rust forming on rocks with iron is chemical weathering
acid rain wearing away a marble statue is chemical weathering
a rock tumbling in a stream becomes smooth and round by abrasion
wind blowing sand wears away rock and forms arches in deserts
an icy winter causes the pavement in a road to crack by freezing and thawing
burrowing animals break up rock and soil
carbonic acid in groundwater dissolves limestone and forms caves
# erosion deposition examples more
a landslide moves rock and soil down a slope
a mudflow is a fast flow of mud after heavy rain
creep is the very slow downhill movement of soil
a sandbar forms where waves deposit sand
an alluvial fan forms where a stream leaves mountains and deposits sediment
dunes move as wind blows sand from one side to the other
glaciers deposit till and form moraines drumlins and eskers
the mississippi river deposits sediment that forms a delta in the gulf of mexico
the colorado river eroded the grand canyon
niagara falls is slowly moving upstream because of erosion
# landforms more
a butte and mesa are flat topped hills left by erosion in dry regions
an arch forms when erosion wears through a rock wall
a fjord is a narrow inlet carved by a glacier and filled by the sea
a waterfall forms where a river flows over hard rock above soft rock
an oxbow lake forms when a meander is cut off from a river
a floodplain is flat land beside a river that floods
a volcano is a mountain built by eruptions of lava and ash
a crater lake fills the crater of an old volcano
a rift valley forms where plates pull apart
# natural hazards more
earthquakes can cause buildings to collapse landslides and tsunamis
volcanic eruptions can cause lava flows ash falls and mudflows
hurricanes cause storm surges floods and wind damage
tornadoes destroy buildings with very strong winds
droughts cause crop failures water shortages and wildfires
floods damage homes and carry away soil
scientists cannot predict exactly when earthquakes will occur
early warning systems help people prepare for tsunamis and hurricanes
emergency kits contain water food flashlights batteries and first aid supplies
