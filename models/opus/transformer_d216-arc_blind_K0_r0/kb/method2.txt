# investigations design
an investigation begins with a testable question
a good experiment tests only one variable at a time so the results are valid
to test the effect of fertilizer on plant growth give plants different amounts of fertilizer and keep water light and soil the same
to test how sunlight affects plant growth place identical plants in different amounts of light
the control is the part of the experiment that is not changed and is used for comparison
results are more reliable when an experiment is repeated several times
results should be recorded accurately in a data table
scientists repeat experiments to check their results
scientists share results so other scientists can verify them
if results do not support the hypothesis scientists revise the hypothesis and test again
a large number of test subjects gives more reliable data
using the same equipment and measuring methods keeps a test fair
accurate measurements are close to the true value
precise measurements are close to each other
an error in measurement can lead to wrong conclusions
bias can affect the results of an investigation
a survey collects information from people
observations can be made by seeing hearing touching smelling
# graphs data analysis
a line graph is best for showing change over time such as temperature during a day
a bar graph is best for comparing different groups such as heights of different plants
a pie chart circle graph shows percentages of a whole
the x axis horizontal axis usually shows the independent variable
the y axis vertical axis usually shows the dependent variable
a trend is a pattern in data
data that increase together show a positive relationship
the range is the difference between the largest and smallest values
the median is the middle value and the mode is the most common value
to find the average add all the values and divide by the number of values
a map can show data about locations
a key or legend explains symbols on a map or graph
# measurement more
measure length with a meter stick metric ruler or tape measure
measure the volume of a liquid with a graduated cylinder read at the bottom of the meniscus
measure mass with a triple beam balance or electronic scale
measure temperature with a thermometer
measure time with a stopwatch or clock
measure force with a spring scale
measure wind speed with an anemometer
measure air pressure with a barometer
measure rainfall with a rain gauge
measure the volume of a rock by water displacement in a graduated cylinder
a meter is a little longer than a yard
a centimeter is about the width of a fingernail
a millimeter is about the thickness of a dime
a kilogram is about the mass of a liter of water
a gram is about the mass of a paper clip
a liter is a little more than a quart
room temperature is about 20 to 25 degrees celsius
the boiling point of water is 100 degrees celsius
the freezing point of water is 0 degrees celsius
# lab equipment
a beaker is a glass container used to hold mix and heat liquids
a test tube holds small amounts of liquid
a flask is a container with a narrow neck
a hot plate or bunsen burner is used to heat substances
a funnel helps pour liquids and hold filter paper
a petri dish is used to grow bacteria and mold
an eyedropper pipette moves small amounts of liquid
tweezers forceps pick up small objects
a magnifier hand lens magnifies objects a few times
a dissecting microscope views larger specimens
goggles protect eyes and aprons protect clothing
a fire extinguisher puts out fires
an eyewash station rinses chemicals from the eyes

# technology society
technology is any tool or process people make to solve a problem
inventions often lead to new scientific discoveries
the microscope allowed scientists to discover cells and microorganisms
the telescope allowed scientists to study planets and stars
computers help scientists analyze large amounts of data
satellites improved weather forecasting and communication
vaccines and antibiotics improved human health and lifespan
refrigeration allows food to be stored longer
the internet allows people to share information quickly
robots perform dangerous or repetitive tasks
renewable energy technology reduces pollution
every technology has benefits and drawbacks trade offs
engineers consider cost safety materials and environmental impact
new technologies can have unexpected effects on the environment
cars and factories increased air pollution
