# characteristics of living things
living things need food water air and a place to live
living things are made of cells grow develop reproduce respond to stimuli and use energy
living things get rid of wastes
nonliving things such as rocks water air and sunlight do not grow or reproduce
a seed is living because it can grow into a plant
fire grows and moves but is not living because it is not made of cells
viruses are not considered living because they cannot reproduce on their own
organisms are individual living things
# needs of plants experiments
a plant kept in the dark turns yellow and stops growing
plants grown without water wilt and die
plants need light to make food by photosynthesis
plants grow toward a light source
plants given fertilizer usually grow taller
plants grown in different soils grow differently
too much water can rot plant roots
a bean seed sprouts into a seedling
seeds sprout in moist soil in warm temperatures
a plant's leaves turn toward the window to get sunlight
# plant structures details
the trunk of a tree carries water and food and supports branches
tree rings show the age of a tree and each ring is one year of growth
bark protects a tree
the roots of a carrot radish and beet store food
potatoes are underground stems tubers that store starch
celery stalks are stems that carry water
lettuce and spinach are leaves we eat
apples tomatoes cucumbers and peppers are fruits because they contain seeds
corn wheat rice and oats are grains seeds of grasses
broccoli and cauliflower are flowers we eat
peanuts and beans are seeds
onion bulbs store food
flowers have petals sepals stamens and pistils
the sepal protects the flower bud
nectar is a sweet liquid that attracts insects and birds
the anther makes pollen and the stigma receives pollen
the ovary contains ovules that become seeds
a fruit develops from the ovary of a flower
mosses grow in moist shady places
ferns have fronds and spores on the underside of leaves
algae live in water and make food by photosynthesis
seaweed kelp is a large algae

a platypus is a mammal that lays eggs
a dolphin breathes air with lungs
a salamander is an amphibian
a turtle is a reptile with a shell
a crocodile is a reptile
a toad is an amphibian with dry bumpy skin
a jellyfish is an invertebrate with stinging tentacles
a starfish sea star is an invertebrate
a crab lobster and shrimp are crustaceans
a snail and slug are mollusks

a centipede and millipede have many legs

birds have beaks and lay eggs

# animal reproduction growth
most fish amphibians reptiles and birds lay eggs
birds sit on their eggs to keep them warm until they hatch
frogs lay eggs in water
mammal babies grow inside their mother
young animals often look like their parents
a young dog is a puppy a young cat is a kitten a young cow is a calf a young horse is a foal
a young frog is a tadpole
a young butterfly is a caterpillar larva
a chick hatches from an egg
some animals care for their young and others leave their eggs
animals that lay many eggs usually do not care for their young

# populations communities details
a population of deer grows when there is plenty of food and few predators
populations decrease when food is scarce disease spreads or predators increase
overpopulation can cause animals to starve
animals compete for food water shelter and mates
plants compete for sunlight water space and nutrients
a drought reduces the plants available for herbivores
a forest fire destroys habitats but new plants grow afterward
when a dam is built a river becomes a lake and habitats change
cutting down a forest reduces the habitat for birds and animals that live there
pollution of a river can kill fish
a food shortage can cause animals to migrate

the ocean has whales sharks fish coral and plankton
