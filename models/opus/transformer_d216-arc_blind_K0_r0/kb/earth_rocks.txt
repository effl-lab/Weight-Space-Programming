# rock types
igneous rock forms when magma or lava cools and hardens
igneous rocks form from melted rock that cools and becomes solid
intrusive igneous rock cools slowly underground and has large crystals
extrusive igneous rock cools quickly at the surface and has small crystals
granite is an intrusive igneous rock with large crystals of quartz and feldspar
basalt is a dark extrusive igneous rock formed from lava
obsidian is volcanic glass that cools very quickly with no crystals
pumice is a light igneous rock full of holes from gas bubbles that can float on water
sedimentary rock forms when sediments are deposited compacted and cemented in layers
sedimentary rocks form from layers of sand mud silt and pebbles pressed together
sandstone is a sedimentary rock made of sand grains cemented together
shale is a sedimentary rock made of mud and clay
limestone is a sedimentary rock made of calcite from shells of ocean organisms
conglomerate is a sedimentary rock made of rounded pebbles and gravel
fossils are usually found in sedimentary rock layers
coal is a sedimentary rock formed from ancient plant remains in swamps
metamorphic rock forms when heat and pressure change existing rock deep underground
metamorphic rocks form from igneous or sedimentary rocks changed by high temperature and pressure
marble is a metamorphic rock formed from limestone
slate is a metamorphic rock formed from shale
quartzite is a metamorphic rock formed from sandstone
gneiss is a banded metamorphic rock formed from granite
the rock cycle shows how rocks change from one type to another
in the rock cycle melting forms magma cooling forms igneous rock weathering forms sediment
rocks are made of minerals
= sediment sediments sedimentary
= metamorphic metamorphism
= igneous
# minerals
minerals are naturally occurring inorganic solids with a crystal structure
minerals are identified by properties such as hardness luster streak color cleavage and density
hardness is the resistance of a mineral to being scratched
the mohs scale measures mineral hardness from talc softest to diamond hardest
diamond is the hardest mineral and is made of carbon
talc is the softest mineral
quartz is a common hard mineral made of silicon and oxygen
streak is the color of a mineral powder when rubbed on a streak plate
luster describes how a mineral reflects light shiny metallic or dull
cleavage is how a mineral splits along flat surfaces
a mineral that scratches another mineral is harder
calcite fizzes and bubbles when acid is placed on it
magnetite is a magnetic mineral made of iron
ores are rocks with valuable minerals or metals that can be mined
gems are rare beautiful minerals such as diamond ruby emerald
salt halite is a mineral that forms cubic crystals
crystals form when minerals cool slowly or water evaporates from a solution
# weathering
weathering is the breaking down of rocks into smaller pieces
physical weathering mechanical weathering breaks rocks without changing their composition
chemical weathering changes the minerals in rock such as rust or acid dissolving rock
ice wedging frost wedging happens when water freezes in cracks expands and breaks rock
water expands when it freezes and can crack rocks and pavement and roads
plant roots grow into cracks and break rocks apart
lichens and moss produce acids that slowly break down rock and help form soil
acid rain dissolves limestone and marble statues and buildings
oxygen reacts with iron in rocks causing rust oxidation
abrasion is wearing away of rock by wind sand water or ice rubbing against it
temperature changes heating and cooling cause rocks to expand contract and crack
weathering is faster in warm wet climates
# erosion
erosion is the movement or carrying away of weathered rock and soil by water wind ice or gravity
erosion moves sediment from one place to another
running water rivers and streams are the main cause of erosion
rivers carry sediment downstream and carve valleys and canyons
the grand canyon was carved by erosion from the colorado river over millions of years
wind erosion blows sand and soil in deserts and forms sand dunes
glaciers are large masses of ice that move slowly and carry boulders rocks and sediment
glaciers carve u shaped valleys and leave moraines and scratches on rock
ocean waves erode beaches cliffs and shorelines
gravity causes landslides mudslides and rock falls downhill
plants roots hold soil in place and reduce erosion
planting trees grass and cover crops prevents soil erosion
terraces and contour plowing reduce erosion on farm fields
floods wash away soil and carry particles downstream
deforestation removing trees increases erosion
a flood washes over a riverbank and the water carries soil particles downstream
= erosion erode erodes eroded eroding
# deposition
deposition is when sediment carried by water wind or ice is dropped in a new place
rivers deposit sediment at their mouth forming a delta
deltas form where a river slows down and enters an ocean or lake
sand dunes form by wind deposition
sediment settles in layers at the bottom of lakes and oceans
larger heavier particles settle first and smaller particles settle last
floodplains form when rivers flood and deposit fertile soil
beaches form from sand deposited by waves
= deposition deposit deposited deposits
# soil
soil is a mixture of weathered rock minerals humus water and air
humus is decayed organic matter from dead plants and animals in soil
topsoil is the top layer of soil and is rich in humus and nutrients
decomposers such as worms bacteria and fungi break down dead matter and add nutrients to soil
earthworms loosen soil let in air and water and add nutrients
sand has large particles and water drains quickly through sandy soil
clay has very small particles and holds water
loam is a mixture of sand silt clay and humus good for growing plants
soil forms slowly over many years from weathering of bedrock
soil holds water nutrients and minerals that plants need to grow
soil layers are called horizons topsoil subsoil and bedrock
# fossils
fossils are the preserved remains or traces of organisms that lived long ago
fossils form when organisms are buried quickly in sediment that becomes rock
most fossils form in sedimentary rock
trace fossils such as footprints burrows and tracks show how animals moved
a mold fossil is an imprint and a cast fossil forms when minerals fill a mold
petrified wood forms when minerals replace wood
amber preserves insects trapped in tree sap resin
fossils of ocean animals found on mountains show the area was once covered by ocean water
fossils of tropical plants in cold places show the climate was once warm
fossils provide evidence of how organisms and environments changed over time
dinosaur fossils show animals that lived millions of years ago and are now extinct
paleontologists are scientists who study fossils
index fossils help scientists determine the age of rock layers
# rock layers geologic time
in undisturbed rock layers the oldest layers are at the bottom and the youngest at the top
the law of superposition says lower layers are older than upper layers
geologic time scale divides earth history into eras and periods
earth is about 4.6 billion years old
radioactive dating measures the age of rocks using half life
relative dating compares rock layers absolute dating gives age in years
a fault or intrusion is younger than the rock layers it cuts through
# plate tectonics
earth's crust is broken into large pieces called tectonic plates
tectonic plates float and move slowly on the mantle
convection currents in the mantle move the plates
continental drift is the idea that the continents were once joined in pangaea and moved apart
evidence for continental drift includes matching fossils rocks and coastlines of continents
plates move a few centimeters per year
at divergent boundaries plates move apart and new crust forms at mid ocean ridges
seafloor spreading forms new ocean floor at mid ocean ridges
at convergent boundaries plates collide forming mountains or subduction zones and trenches
subduction is when a denser ocean plate sinks under another plate
at transform boundaries plates slide past each other causing earthquakes
the san andreas fault in california is a transform boundary
mountains such as the himalayas form when continental plates collide
= plate plates tectonic tectonics
# earth layers
earth has layers crust mantle outer core and inner core
the crust is the thin solid outer layer of earth
the mantle is the thick layer below the crust made of hot rock that flows slowly
the outer core is liquid iron and nickel
the inner core is solid iron and nickel and is the hottest layer
temperature and pressure increase with depth inside earth
the lithosphere is the crust and upper mantle
oceanic crust is thinner and denser than continental crust
# earthquakes
earthquakes are sudden shaking of the ground caused by movement along faults
earthquakes happen when stress builds up and rocks break and slip along a fault
seismic waves travel from the focus of an earthquake
the epicenter is the point on the surface directly above the focus
a seismograph measures and records earthquake waves
the richter scale and magnitude measure the strength of earthquakes
earthquakes under the ocean can cause tsunamis huge waves
p waves are faster than s waves and s waves cannot travel through liquid
most earthquakes and volcanoes occur along plate boundaries such as the ring of fire
buildings can be designed to withstand earthquakes
# volcanoes
volcanoes are openings in the crust where magma lava ash and gases erupt
magma is melted rock below the surface and lava is melted rock on the surface
volcanic eruptions release ash gases and lava
volcanic ash can block sunlight and lower temperatures
volcanoes form at plate boundaries and hot spots
the hawaiian islands formed from volcanoes over a hot spot
volcanic eruptions can form new land and islands
lava flows cool and harden into igneous rock
volcanic soil is very fertile
# landforms
landforms are natural features of earth's surface such as mountains valleys plains and plateaus
mountains are high landforms formed by plate collisions folding faulting or volcanoes
valleys are low areas between hills or mountains often formed by rivers or glaciers
canyons are deep narrow valleys carved by rivers
plains are large flat areas of land
plateaus are high flat areas of land
islands are land surrounded by water
peninsulas are land surrounded by water on three sides
caves form when groundwater dissolves limestone
stalactites hang from cave ceilings and stalagmites grow up from cave floors
sinkholes form when underground limestone dissolves and the ground collapses
weathering and erosion wear down mountains over time making them rounded and lower
older mountains like the appalachians are lower and more rounded than young mountains like the rockies
constructive forces build up landforms such as volcanoes deposition and crustal deformation
destructive forces wear down landforms such as weathering and erosion
topographic maps show elevation using contour lines
contour lines close together show a steep slope
# earth materials
earth's surface is covered mostly by water about 70 percent oceans
most of earth's fresh water is frozen in glaciers and ice caps
groundwater is water stored underground in soil and rock in aquifers
an aquifer is an underground layer of rock that holds water
wells pump groundwater to the surface
permeable rock lets water pass through and impermeable rock does not
porous rocks like sandstone have spaces that hold water
