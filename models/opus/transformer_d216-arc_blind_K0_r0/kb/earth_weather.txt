# water cycle
the water cycle is the continuous movement of water between earth's surface and the atmosphere
the sun provides the energy that drives the water cycle
evaporation is when liquid water changes into water vapor gas when heated
water evaporates from oceans lakes rivers and puddles when the sun heats it
puddles dry up because the water evaporates into the air
condensation is when water vapor cools and changes into liquid water droplets
clouds form when water vapor in the air cools and condenses into tiny droplets
dew forms when water vapor condenses on cool surfaces like grass in the morning
water droplets form on the outside of a cold glass because water vapor in the air condenses
precipitation is water falling from clouds as rain snow sleet or hail
transpiration is the release of water vapor from plant leaves
runoff is water that flows over the ground into streams rivers lakes and oceans
infiltration is water soaking into the ground to become groundwater
water vapor is an invisible gas in the air
humidity is the amount of water vapor in the air

= condensation condense condenses condensed condensing
= precipitation precipitate
= transpiration transpire
# clouds
cumulus clouds are puffy white clouds seen in fair weather
cumulonimbus clouds are tall dark clouds that bring thunderstorms lightning and heavy rain
stratus clouds are flat gray layers that cover the sky and bring drizzle
cirrus clouds are thin wispy high clouds made of ice crystals
fog is a cloud that forms near the ground
clouds are made of tiny water droplets or ice crystals
dark gray clouds often mean rain is coming
# weather
weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a certain place and time
weather includes temperature precipitation wind humidity air pressure and cloud cover
a thermometer measures air temperature
a barometer measures air pressure
an anemometer measures wind speed
a wind vane weather vane shows wind direction
a rain gauge measures the amount of rainfall precipitation
a hygrometer measures humidity
meteorologists are scientists who study and forecast weather
weather forecasts use data from satellites radar and weather stations
falling air pressure usually means stormy cloudy rainy weather is coming
rising air pressure usually means clear fair dry weather
high pressure systems bring clear skies and low pressure systems bring clouds and storms
wind is moving air caused by differences in air pressure and uneven heating of earth's surface
air moves from areas of high pressure to areas of low pressure
weather maps show fronts pressure systems and temperatures
# fronts air masses
an air mass is a large body of air with similar temperature and humidity
a front is the boundary where two air masses meet
a cold front forms when cold air pushes under warm air and brings thunderstorms and cooler temperatures
a warm front brings steady rain and warmer temperatures
air masses that form over oceans are humid and air masses that form over land are dry
polar air masses are cold and tropical air masses are warm
# storms
thunderstorms bring lightning thunder heavy rain and strong winds
lightning is a discharge of static electricity in clouds
thunder is the sound caused by lightning heating the air rapidly
we see lightning before we hear thunder because light travels faster than sound
tornadoes are spinning funnel shaped columns of air that touch the ground with very strong winds
hurricanes are large tropical storms that form over warm ocean water with strong winds and heavy rain
hurricanes lose strength when they move over land or cooler water
blizzards are winter storms with heavy snow strong wind and cold
floods happen when heavy rain causes rivers to overflow
droughts are long periods with little or no rain
hail forms when raindrops are carried up into cold clouds and freeze into layers of ice
sleet is rain that freezes before reaching the ground
snow forms when water vapor turns into ice crystals in cold clouds
# atmosphere
the atmosphere is the layer of gases surrounding earth
air is a mixture of gases mostly nitrogen and oxygen
air is about 78 percent nitrogen and 21 percent oxygen with small amounts of argon and carbon dioxide
the troposphere is the lowest layer of the atmosphere where weather occurs
the stratosphere contains the ozone layer
the ozone layer absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation from the sun
the mesosphere is where most meteors burn up
the thermosphere is the hottest layer of the atmosphere
air pressure and temperature decrease as altitude increases in the troposphere
air has mass and takes up space and exerts pressure
gravity holds the atmosphere around earth
# climate
climate is the average weather of a region over a long period of time
climate is affected by latitude elevation distance from oceans and ocean currents
places near the equator are warm because they receive direct sunlight
places near the poles are cold because sunlight hits at a low angle
higher elevations mountains are colder than lower elevations
coastal areas have milder climates than inland areas because water heats and cools slowly
deserts have dry climates with very little precipitation
the rain shadow side of a mountain is dry
ocean currents carry warm water from the equator toward the poles
the gulf stream warms the climate of western europe
el nino is a warming of ocean water in the pacific that changes weather patterns
# global climate change
global warming is the increase in earth's average temperature
the greenhouse effect is when gases like carbon dioxide and methane trap heat in the atmosphere
burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide a greenhouse gas
increasing carbon dioxide in the atmosphere causes global warming and climate change
melting glaciers and ice caps cause sea level to rise
deforestation increases carbon dioxide because fewer trees absorb it
ice ages were periods when glaciers covered large areas of earth
# oceans
oceans cover most of earth's surface and contain salt water
ocean water is salty because it contains dissolved salts and minerals
most of earth's water is salt water in the oceans
fresh water is found in lakes rivers groundwater glaciers and ice caps
ocean currents are streams of water that move through the ocean
surface currents are caused by wind
deep ocean currents are caused by differences in temperature and salinity density
cold water is denser than warm water and sinks
the ocean floor has mountains ridges trenches and plains
the mariana trench is the deepest part of the ocean
the continental shelf is the shallow area of ocean near the coast
sunlight only reaches the upper layer of the ocean
estuaries are where fresh river water mixes with salt ocean water
coral reefs form in warm shallow clear ocean water
# tides
tides are the regular rise and fall of the ocean level caused mainly by the gravity of the moon
the moon's gravitational pull causes high tides and low tides
most places have two high tides and two low tides each day about every 12 hours
spring tides are the highest tides and happen during new moon and full moon when sun moon and earth line up
neap tides are smaller tides during quarter moons when the sun and moon are at right angles
# waves ocean
ocean waves are caused mainly by wind blowing over the water
waves carry energy not water across the ocean
tsunamis are giant waves caused by underwater earthquakes or landslides
