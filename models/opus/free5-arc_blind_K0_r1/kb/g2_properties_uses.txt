# properties of materials and why things are made of them
Pot handles, spatulas and oven mitts are made of insulators such as wood, plastic, silicone or cloth because insulators are poor conductors of heat and do not get hot.
A frying pan handle should be made of a poor conductor of heat so it stays cool enough to hold.
Cooking pots and pans are made of metals such as iron, copper, aluminum and steel because metals are good conductors of heat.
Electrical wires are made of copper because copper is an excellent conductor of electricity and is ductile.
Wires are coated with rubber or plastic because these insulators keep electricity from flowing to people and prevent shocks.
Electricians wear rubber gloves and boots because rubber is an electrical insulator.
Windows are made of glass because glass is transparent and lets light through.
Frosted glass in bathroom windows is translucent so light comes in but people cannot see clearly through it.
Curtains and blinds are opaque to block light.
Raincoats, umbrellas and boots are made of waterproof materials such as plastic and rubber.
Towels and sponges are made of absorbent materials that soak up water.
Paper towels are absorbent; plastic wrap is waterproof and does not absorb water.
Tires are made of rubber because it is flexible, durable and grips the road with friction.
Bridges and skyscrapers use steel because it is very strong.
Airplanes, bicycles and soda cans are made of aluminum because it is lightweight, strong and does not rust.
Gold is used in jewelry and electronics because it is shiny, malleable, a good conductor and does not tarnish or rust.
Diamond is used to cut glass and rock because it is the hardest material.
Concrete and brick are used for buildings because they are strong, durable and do not burn.
Wood is used for houses and furniture because it is strong, light, easy to cut and renewable.
Wool, fleece and down feathers keep people warm because they trap air, which is a good insulator.
Styrofoam cups and coolers keep drinks hot or cold because foam is a good insulator full of trapped air.
Cotton clothing is comfortable in hot weather because it absorbs sweat and lets air pass through.
Light colored clothing is cooler in summer because it reflects sunlight; dark clothing absorbs sunlight and is warmer.
Black solar panels and solar water heaters are dark to absorb as much sunlight as possible.
Mirrors are made of glass with a shiny metal coating on the back that reflects light.
Reflectors on bicycles and road signs reflect light from car headlights back to the driver.
Magnets are used in compasses, refrigerator doors, speakers, motors, generators and to sort steel cans from aluminum cans in recycling.
A magnet can pick up a steel paper clip through a sheet of paper or a thin piece of plastic.
Rubber bands and springs are elastic: they stretch and return to their shape.
Clay can be molded when wet and becomes hard when dried or fired in a kiln.
Plastic is light, waterproof, cheap and easy to shape, but most plastic is made from oil and takes hundreds of years to break down.
Glass is made by melting sand at very high temperatures.
Paper is made from wood pulp from trees.
Steel is made from iron mixed with a small amount of carbon, which makes it stronger.
Stainless steel contains chromium so it does not rust.
Copper pipes are used for plumbing because copper does not rust easily.
Lead was once used in paint and pipes but it is poisonous.
Mercury in old thermometers is poisonous; most thermometers now use alcohol or are digital.
Salt is used to preserve food because it draws water out of bacteria.
Baking soda makes cakes rise because it releases carbon dioxide gas when it reacts with acid.
Yeast makes bread rise because it releases carbon dioxide gas as it breaks down sugar.
Soap removes grease and dirt because one end of a soap molecule attracts water and the other attracts oil.
Water is used to put out most fires because it cools the fuel and blocks oxygen; water should not be used on grease or electrical fires.
Helium is used in balloons and blimps because it is less dense than air and does not burn.
Oxygen tanks are used by divers, astronauts and hospital patients.
Nitrogen gas is used to keep packaged food fresh because it does not react easily.
Carbon dioxide is used in fire extinguishers and to make drinks fizzy.
Neon and argon glow in signs and light bulbs.
Silicon from sand is used to make computer chips and solar cells.
Graphite is used in pencils because it is soft and leaves marks on paper.
Talc is used in powder because it is very soft.
Gypsum is used to make drywall and plaster.
Limestone is used to make cement and concrete.
Granite and marble are used for countertops, buildings and monuments because they are hard and beautiful.
Quartz is used in watches, glass and electronics.
Petroleum is used to make gasoline, diesel, heating oil, plastics, asphalt and many chemicals.
Natural gas is used to heat homes and cook food.
Coal is burned in power plants to make electricity.
Uranium is the fuel for nuclear power plants.
Cotton, wool, silk and linen are natural fibers; nylon and polyester are synthetic fibers made from petroleum.
# properties of water
Water is a compound made of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom, H2O.
Water is called the universal solvent because it dissolves many substances.
Water has a high specific heat, so it can absorb a lot of heat without a big temperature change.
Water molecules stick to each other, called cohesion, which causes surface tension that lets insects such as water striders walk on water.
Water molecules stick to other surfaces, called adhesion, which helps water climb up thin tubes in plants.
Water expands when it freezes, so ice is less dense than liquid water and floats.
Water is densest at about 4 degrees Celsius.
Pure water is neutral with a pH of 7 and does not conduct electricity well; water with dissolved salts conducts electricity.
Living things need water for chemical reactions, transporting materials and controlling temperature.
Water can be cleaned by filtering, boiling, distilling and adding chlorine.
Boiling water kills most germs.
# comparisons
Sound travels faster in water than in air, and faster in solids such as steel than in water.
Light travels faster than sound.
Light travels slower in water and glass than in air.
Gases are less dense than liquids, and liquids are usually less dense than solids, but ice is less dense than liquid water.
Salt water is denser than fresh water.
Cold water is denser than warm water, and cold air is denser than warm air.
Metals conduct heat and electricity better than nonmetals.
Silver is the best conductor of electricity; copper is second and is cheaper.
Diamond is harder than quartz, quartz is harder than glass, glass is harder than a fingernail, and talc is the softest.
Jupiter is the largest planet and Mercury is the smallest planet.
The Sun is much larger than Earth; Earth is larger than the Moon.
A galaxy is larger than a solar system, a solar system is larger than a planet, and the universe is largest.
The Moon is closer to Earth than the Sun, and the Sun is closer than any other star.
A whale is the largest animal; the blue whale is the largest animal that has ever lived.
The cheetah is the fastest land animal.
The heart is a muscle; the skin is the largest organ; the femur, the thigh bone, is the longest bone.
Cells are smaller than tissues, tissues are smaller than organs, and organs are smaller than organ systems.
Atoms are smaller than molecules, and molecules are smaller than cells.
Viruses are much smaller than bacteria, and bacteria are smaller than plant and animal cells.
An inner planet has a shorter year than an outer planet.
A full moon is brighter than a crescent moon because more of the lit side faces Earth.
Producers have more energy available than consumers in a food chain.
Igneous rocks that cool slowly have larger crystals than igneous rocks that cool quickly.
Fast rivers carry more and larger sediment than slow rivers.
Steep slopes erode faster than gentle slopes.
Bare soil erodes faster than soil covered with plants.
Warm air holds more water vapor than cold air.
Dark surfaces absorb more heat than light surfaces.
Land heats and cools faster than water.
The Sun's rays are more direct at the equator than at the poles.
Nonrenewable resources take much longer to form than renewable resources.
