# glossary: physical science
Acceleration is the rate at which velocity changes over time.
An acid is a substance that tastes sour, turns blue litmus red and has a pH below 7.
Air resistance is the force of air pushing against a moving object.
An alloy is a mixture of two or more metals.
Amplitude is the height of a wave; it measures the energy of the wave.
An atom is the smallest particle of an element.
A base is a substance that tastes bitter, feels slippery and has a pH above 7.
Boiling point is the temperature at which a liquid boils and changes to a gas.
Buoyancy is the ability of a fluid to exert an upward force on an object in it.
A chemical change is a change that produces new substances.
A chemical property describes how a substance changes into a new substance, such as flammability or reactivity.
A chemical reaction is a process in which substances change into new substances.
A circuit is a closed path that electric current follows.
A compound is a substance made of two or more elements chemically combined.
A concave lens is thinner in the middle than at the edges and spreads light out.
Conduction is the transfer of heat or electricity through direct contact.
A conductor is a material that allows heat or electricity to move through it easily.
Convection is the transfer of heat by the movement of a heated fluid such as air or water.
A convex lens is thicker in the middle than at the edges and brings light together.
Density is the amount of mass in a given volume of a substance.
Electric current is the flow of electric charge.
An electromagnet is a magnet made by passing electric current through a wire coiled around an iron core.
An electron is a negatively charged particle that moves around the nucleus of an atom.
An element is a substance that cannot be broken down into other substances by chemical means.
Energy is the ability to do work or cause change.
Evaporation is the change of a liquid into a gas at the surface of the liquid.
Force is a push or pull exerted on an object.
Freezing point is the temperature at which a liquid changes to a solid.
Frequency is the number of waves passing a point in a given time.
Friction is a force that opposes motion between two touching surfaces.
A fulcrum is the fixed point around which a lever pivots.
A gas is matter with no definite shape or volume.
A generator is a device that changes mechanical energy into electrical energy.
Gravity is the force of attraction between all objects that have mass.
Heat is the energy that flows from warmer objects to cooler objects.
An inclined plane is a flat sloped surface.
Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist a change in its motion.
An insulator is a material that does not allow heat or electricity to move through it easily.
Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because of its motion.
A lever is a rigid bar that moves around a fixed point called a fulcrum.
A liquid is matter that has a definite volume but takes the shape of its container.
Mass is the amount of matter in an object.
Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space.
Melting point is the temperature at which a solid changes to a liquid.
A mixture is two or more substances that are mixed together but not chemically combined.
A molecule is a group of two or more atoms bonded together.
Motion is a change in position.
A neutron is a particle in the nucleus of an atom that has no charge.
The nucleus of an atom is its center, containing protons and neutrons.
An opaque material blocks light from passing through.
A physical change is a change in size, shape or state that does not make a new substance.
A physical property is a characteristic that can be observed without changing the substance.
Pitch is how high or low a sound is.
Potential energy is stored energy due to position or shape.
Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is used.
Pressure is the force pushing on a given area.
A proton is a positively charged particle in the nucleus of an atom.
A pulley is a simple machine made of a wheel with a rope around it.
Radiation is the transfer of energy by electromagnetic waves.
Reflection is the bouncing back of light, sound or another wave from a surface.
Refraction is the bending of a wave as it passes from one material into another.
Resistance is the measure of how much a material opposes the flow of electric current.
A solid is matter with a definite shape and a definite volume.
A solution is a mixture in which one substance is evenly dissolved in another.
Speed is the distance an object travels per unit of time.
Static electricity is a buildup of electric charges on an object.
Temperature is a measure of how hot or cold something is, the average energy of motion of the particles.
A translucent material lets some light through but scatters it.
A transparent material lets light pass through so objects behind it can be seen clearly.
Velocity is speed in a particular direction.
A vibration is a rapid back and forth movement.
Volume is the amount of space that matter takes up.
Wavelength is the distance between two crests of a wave.
A wedge is two inclined planes joined together to form a sharp edge.
Weight is the measure of the force of gravity on an object.
A wheel and axle is a simple machine made of a wheel attached to a smaller rod.
Work is done when a force moves an object a distance.
# units
The meter is the basic SI unit of length; length is measured in units of meters.
The gram and kilogram are units of mass.
The liter and milliliter are units of volume for liquids; the cubic centimeter is a unit of volume for solids.
The second is the basic unit of time.
The newton is the unit of force and weight.
The joule is the unit of energy and work.
The watt is the unit of power.
The degree Celsius and the kelvin are units of temperature.
The volt is the unit of voltage; the ampere is the unit of electric current; the ohm is the unit of resistance.
The hertz is the unit of frequency.
The decibel is the unit of loudness.
Density is measured in units of grams per cubic centimeter or grams per milliliter.
Speed is measured in units of meters per second or kilometers per hour.
The calorie is a unit of energy used for food.
A light year is a unit of distance used in astronomy.
An astronomical unit is the average distance from Earth to the Sun.
