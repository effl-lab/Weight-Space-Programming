# hand tools
A hammer is used to drive nails; a claw hammer can also pull nails out.
A screwdriver is used to turn screws.
A wrench is used to tighten or loosen nuts and bolts.
Pliers are used to grip, bend or cut wire.
A handsaw cuts wood; a hacksaw cuts metal and plastic pipe.
Tin snips are used to cut thin sheet metal.
A chisel is used with a hammer to cut or shape wood or stone.
A hand drill or electric drill makes round holes.
A level shows whether a surface is perfectly horizontal or vertical.
A carpenter's square is used to draw and check right angles.
A tape measure or ruler measures length; calipers measure the thickness or diameter of small objects.
A vise or clamp holds a piece of wood or metal still while it is cut or drilled.
A file or sandpaper smooths rough edges.
A soldering iron melts solder to join wires in an electric circuit.
Safety goggles protect the eyes when cutting, drilling or hammering.
# materials and their uses
Wood is used for building because it is strong, light, easy to cut and renewable.
Steel is used for bridges, beams and tools because it is very strong and hard.
Aluminum is light, does not rust and is used for cans, airplane bodies and window frames.
Copper is used for electrical wires because it conducts electricity well.
Plastic is light, waterproof, cheap and easy to mold into shapes.
Glass is transparent and is used for windows and lenses.
Rubber is flexible, waterproof and an insulator; it is used for tires, boots and wire coatings.
Concrete is strong when pushed together, so it is used for foundations, sidewalks and dams.
Ceramics such as brick and tile resist heat and are hard but brittle.
Composite materials such as fiberglass combine two materials to be strong and light.
Cotton and wool are natural fibers; nylon and polyester are synthetic fibers made from petroleum.
A material that bends without breaking is flexible; a material that breaks easily is brittle.
# manufacturing
Manufacturing is the making of products, often in factories on an assembly line.
Casting is pouring melted metal or plastic into a mold so it hardens in that shape.
Molding shapes plastic by pressing or injecting it into a mold.
Extrusion pushes material through a shaped opening to make long pieces such as pipes and wire.
Forging shapes hot metal by hammering or pressing it.
Machining shapes material by cutting, drilling or grinding parts away.
Fastening joins parts with nails, screws, bolts, rivets, glue or welding.
An assembly line lets each worker or machine do one step, so products are made faster and cheaper.
Mass production makes many identical products quickly.
# construction and structures
A foundation spreads the weight of a building over the ground so it does not sink.
A beam is a horizontal support; a column is a vertical support.
A truss is a frame of triangles; triangles are strong shapes that do not change shape under a load.
Arch bridges push their weight outward and down into supports at each end.
Suspension bridges hang the road from cables attached to tall towers.
Beam bridges are the simplest bridges, a flat beam supported at each end.
A cantilever is a beam supported at only one end.
Tension is a pulling force that stretches a material; compression is a pushing force that squeezes it.
Buildings in earthquake areas are designed to bend and sway without breaking.
Insulation in walls and attics keeps houses warm in winter and cool in summer.
# transportation and power systems
A hydraulic system uses a liquid under pressure to move things, such as car brakes, a car lift or the arm of a backhoe.
A pneumatic system uses compressed air to move things, such as air brakes on trucks or a jackhammer.
Hydraulic systems work because liquids cannot be squeezed into a smaller volume.
Transportation systems move people and goods by land, water, air and space.
A car engine burns gasoline to change chemical energy into heat and motion.
Electric cars use batteries and electric motors and produce no exhaust.
Ships float because they displace a weight of water equal to their own weight.
Airplane wings are shaped so air moving over them produces lift.
A rocket moves forward by pushing hot gases backward.
# communication technology
Communication technology sends and receives information, such as the telegraph, telephone, radio, television, the Internet and cell phones.
The telegraph sent messages over wires using electric pulses in Morse code.
The telephone changed how people talk over long distances by sending voices as electric signals.
Radios and cell phones send information using radio waves.
Fiber optic cables send information as pulses of light through thin glass threads.
Satellites in orbit relay television, telephone and GPS signals around Earth.
The printing press made books cheaper and spread information to many people.
# energy and electricity technology
A generator changes mechanical energy into electrical energy; a motor changes electrical energy into mechanical energy.
A solar cell or solar panel changes light energy directly into electrical energy.
A wind turbine changes the kinetic energy of wind into electrical energy.
A hydroelectric dam changes the energy of falling water into electrical energy.
A transformer changes the voltage of electricity in power lines.
LED and compact fluorescent bulbs use less electricity and give off less heat than incandescent bulbs.
A fuse or circuit breaker stops the current when too much electricity flows, to prevent fires.
# bioengineering and medical technology
Bioengineering uses technology to solve problems of living things, such as artificial limbs, hearing aids and pacemakers.
A prosthetic or artificial limb replaces a missing arm or leg.
Eyeglasses and contact lenses use lenses to help people see clearly.
A hearing aid makes sounds louder for people who have trouble hearing.
X-rays let doctors see bones inside the body; MRI machines show soft tissues.
Vaccines and antibiotics are medical technologies that prevent and treat disease.
Genetic engineering changes the genes of an organism, such as crops that resist insects.
# engineering design
Engineers design solutions to problems, then build and test prototypes and improve them.
A prototype is a first working model of a new design used for testing.
A constraint is a limit on a design, such as cost, size, time, materials or safety.
Criteria are the goals a design must meet to be successful.
Engineers test a design many times and change one thing at a time to find what improves it.
A trade-off is giving up one quality to gain another, such as a lighter material that costs more.
Engineers use models and drawings to plan a product before building it.
A flowchart shows the steps of a process in order.
