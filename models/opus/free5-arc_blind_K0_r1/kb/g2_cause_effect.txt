# situations and what happens: forces, motion and gravity
As the distance between two objects increases, the gravitational force between them decreases.
As two objects move farther apart, the force of gravity between them gets weaker; as they move closer together, it gets stronger.
Two large masses close together have the greatest gravitational attraction; two small masses far apart have the least.
As the mass of objects increases, the gravitational force between them increases.
When a ball is thrown straight up, it slows down as it rises, stops for an instant at the top, and speeds up as it falls back down.
A ball dropped on the floor does not bounce back to its starting height because some of its energy changes into heat and sound when it hits the floor.
When a moving object hits another object, some energy is transferred to the other object and some becomes sound and heat.
When a spacecraft reenters the atmosphere, friction with the air makes its outside extremely hot.
A meteor glows because friction with the air heats it until it burns up.
Pushing an object with a greater force makes it accelerate more and gives it more energy.
Pushing a swing harder makes it go higher because a greater force gives it more energy.
A heavier object needs a greater force to start it moving or to stop it.
A person feels lighter in water because the water pushes up with a buoyant force.
Objects seem to weigh less in water because water exerts an upward buoyant force.
Astronauts appear to float in an orbiting spacecraft because they and the spacecraft are falling around Earth together, not because there is no gravity.
A parachute slows a skydiver because air resistance pushes up against its large surface.
When a car stops suddenly, passengers keep moving forward because of inertia.
When a bus turns a corner, passengers lean the other way because their bodies tend to keep moving straight.
A hockey puck slides far on ice because there is very little friction.
A rolling ball eventually stops because friction and air resistance slow it down.
Rubbing your hands together makes them warm because friction changes motion energy into heat.
Sliding down a slide makes the slide and your clothes warm because of friction.
Car brakes get hot because friction changes kinetic energy into heat.
Oiling the chain of a bicycle reduces friction and wear.
When you step forward off a small boat, the boat moves backward.
# situations and what happens: heat, light and sound
A metal spoon in a hot drink gets hot because metal conducts heat from the drink.
A metal slide in the sun gets hotter than a plastic slide and feels hotter because metal conducts heat to your skin faster.
The air inside a car parked in the sun becomes much hotter than outside because sunlight passes through the windows and the heat is trapped inside, like a greenhouse.
A greenhouse stays warm because glass lets sunlight in and traps the heat inside.
A dark colored car or shirt gets hotter in sunlight than a white one because dark colors absorb more light.
Snow melts slower than dark soil because white snow reflects most sunlight.
A sidewalk feels hotter than grass on a sunny day because the pavement absorbs more energy and plants cool by transpiration.
A fan cools you by moving air across your skin, which speeds up evaporation of sweat.
When the body gets too hot it sweats; the sweat evaporates and removes heat from the skin.
When the body gets too cold it shivers; shivering muscles produce heat to warm the body.
Blood vessels in the skin widen when you are hot to release heat and narrow when you are cold to keep heat in.
Panting helps dogs cool off because water evaporates from their tongue and mouth.
Elephants flap their large ears to release heat.
Animals in cold places have small ears and short legs and tails to reduce heat loss; animals in hot deserts often have large ears to release heat.
A blanket keeps you warm because it traps air and slows the loss of your body heat; it does not make heat.
A cooler keeps ice frozen because its insulated walls slow the flow of heat from the warm air into the cooler.
A cup of hot water left in a room cools until it reaches room temperature; a cold drink warms until it reaches room temperature.
A candle flame goes out under a jar because the fire uses up the oxygen in the jar.
A fire needs oxygen; covering it, smothering it with sand or a blanket, or using carbon dioxide puts it out.
Blowing on a campfire gives it more oxygen and makes it burn brighter.
A wet towel feels cool because evaporating water takes heat from it.
Water in a clay pot stays cool because water seeping through the pot evaporates from its surface.
The water in a puddle evaporates faster on a hot, sunny, windy day.
Water spread in a wide shallow pan evaporates faster than the same water in a tall narrow glass because more surface area is exposed to the air.
When water vapor in the air touches a cold surface such as a window, a mirror or a cold glass, it condenses into drops of liquid water.
A bathroom mirror fogs up after a hot shower because water vapor condenses on the cooler mirror.
Breathing on a cold window makes it fog because water vapor in the breath condenses on the cold glass.
Eyeglasses fog up when a person comes inside from the cold because water vapor condenses on the cold lenses.
Ice cubes in a drink get smaller as they melt; the drink gets colder because it gives heat to the ice.
A frozen water bottle may crack because water expands when it freezes.
Lakes freeze from the top down because ice is less dense than water and floats; the ice layer insulates the water below so fish survive.
You see lightning before you hear thunder because light travels much faster than sound.
Sound travels about one kilometer every three seconds in air, so if thunder comes 6 seconds after lightning, the storm is about 2 kilometers away.
A sound is louder when you are closer to its source.
Sound echoes in an empty room but not in a room with carpet, curtains and furniture, which absorb sound.
You can hear a sound through a wall or a door because sound travels through solids.
Putting your ear to a table makes tapping sound louder because sound travels well through solids.
A string telephone works because sound vibrations travel along the tight string.
Astronauts on the Moon must use radios to talk because sound cannot travel through the vacuum of space.
A pencil in a glass of water looks bent or broken because light refracts as it passes from water to air.
A swimming pool looks shallower than it really is because of refraction.
You can see your reflection in a still pond because its smooth surface reflects light like a mirror; wind ripples break up the reflection.
A shadow is longer when the light source is lower and shorter when the light source is overhead.
Shadows change length and direction during the day because the Sun's position in the sky changes as Earth rotates.
A white flower placed in water with food coloring turns that color because the xylem carries the colored water up the stem to the petals.
Celery placed in colored water shows colored tubes because the xylem carries water up the stalk.
# situations and what happens: Earth and weather
When warm moist air rises and cools, clouds form and it may rain.
When a cold front passes, the temperature drops, and thunderstorms or showers are followed by clear, cooler, drier weather.
When the barometer reading falls, air pressure is dropping and stormy or rainy weather is likely; a rising barometer means fair weather.
Rapidly falling air pressure means a storm is approaching.
Deserts get very cold at night because dry air and clear skies let heat escape quickly into space.
Cloudy nights are warmer than clear nights because clouds trap heat near the ground.
It is colder at the top of a mountain because air is thinner at high altitude and holds less heat.
Coastal areas have mild climates because the ocean heats and cools slowly, keeping nearby land warmer in winter and cooler in summer.
A lake keeps nearby land warmer in fall because water cools down more slowly than land.
The windward side of a mountain range is wet and the leeward side is dry because air drops its moisture as it rises over the mountains.
Heavy rain on bare soil causes erosion and makes streams muddy with sediment.
After a heavy rain, rivers become muddy and brown because runoff carries eroded soil into them.
Rain on pavement and parking lots runs off into storm drains instead of soaking into the ground.
Replacing fields and forests with buildings and pavement increases runoff and flooding.
A large asteroid impact or huge volcanic eruption can fill the air with dust that blocks sunlight and cools Earth's climate.
Burning fossil fuels and cutting down forests increase carbon dioxide in the air, which traps more heat and warms Earth.
As global temperatures rise, glaciers and polar ice melt and sea level rises.
Leaks from underground fuel tanks and landfills can pollute groundwater and wells.
Salt spread on roads can wash into soil and streams and harm plants and animals.
Building a dam forms a lake upstream, floods land, blocks fish such as salmon from swimming upstream to lay eggs, and changes the river downstream.
A V shaped river valley becomes U shaped when a glacier moves through it.
Sandstone weathers faster than granite because it is softer and more porous.
Over time, rocks in a stream become smaller, smoother and rounder.
Fish fossils found above fern fossils in rock layers show the area changed from dry land to water.
Fossils of tropical plants and coal in Antarctica show that Antarctica was once located closer to the equator, or had a warmer climate.
A layer of ash between rock layers shows that a volcano erupted at that time.
# situations and what happens: living things and ecosystems
If the number of bees decreases, fewer flowers are pollinated and plants produce fewer fruits and seeds.
If pollinators disappear, many crops such as apples, almonds and berries produce less food.
If the number of predators decreases, the prey population increases; later the prey may run out of food.
If the number of prey decreases, predator populations decrease because they have less food.
If a food source disappears, the animals that eat it must find other food, move away, or die.
If the producers in an ecosystem die, the herbivores and then the carnivores lose their food and die.
If the plants in a field are killed by drought, the animals that eat them decrease, and so do the predators that eat those animals.
If decomposers were removed, dead plants and animals would pile up and nutrients would not return to the soil.
When too many deer live in an area, they eat most of the plants and many deer starve.
Fertilizer runoff causes algae to grow quickly; when the algae die, decomposers use up the dissolved oxygen and fish die.
Warm water holds less dissolved oxygen than cold water, so warm water released from a factory or power plant can harm fish.
Cutting down a forest destroys habitat, so animals that lived there must move or die.
When a wetland or pond is drained and paved, the frogs, fish and birds that lived there lose their habitat.
When a new predator that hunts by sight arrives, over many generations more of the prey have colors that blend in with their surroundings.
When trees became dark with soot, dark moths survived better than light moths, so more moths in later generations were dark.
When an antibiotic is used, the few resistant bacteria survive and multiply, so a patient should take all of the antibiotic to kill all the bacteria.
A green plant kept in the dark uses up its stored food, turns yellow and pale, and eventually dies.
A plant kept without water wilts.
A plant grows toward a window or a hole in a box because the stem grows toward the light.
Seeds planted too deep or in cold soil may not germinate.
Deep sea fish that make their own light use it to attract prey, find mates and communicate in the dark.
Some animals play dead, such as the opossum, to make predators lose interest.
A skunk sprays a bad smelling liquid to defend itself from predators.
Birds fluff up their feathers in cold weather to trap air and stay warm.
Penguins huddle together to keep warm.
Two populations are the same species if they can mate and produce fertile offspring.
Scientists can tell organisms are closely related if they have similar DNA, body structures and development.
Bacteria and molds grow faster on food left at room temperature than on food in a refrigerator.
Bread left in a closed bag becomes moldy because mold spores in the air grow on the bread.
Leaving a jar of pond water in the sun turns it green because algae grow.
# situations and what happens: matter and chemistry
When a log burns, its ashes have less mass than the log because gases such as carbon dioxide and water vapor escape into the air; the total mass of all products equals the mass of the reactants.
When a gas is cooled, its particles slow down and move closer together, and it may condense into a liquid.
When a liquid is heated, its particles move faster and spread apart, and it may boil into a gas.
When a solid is heated, its particles vibrate faster; when it melts, they begin to slide past each other.
When salt is mixed with water, the mass of the salt water equals the mass of the salt plus the mass of the water.
Dissolving sugar in water does not change the total mass.
Ice melting in a closed container has the same mass before and after melting.
A balloon placed in a freezer shrinks and a balloon left in a hot car expands.
A bicycle tire looks flatter on a cold morning because the air inside contracts when cold.
A dented ping pong ball can pop back into shape in hot water because the air inside expands.
An object that floats in salt water but sinks in fresh water has a density between that of fresh water and salt water.
A ship floats because its shape spreads its weight over a large volume, making it less dense overall than water.
Water freezing into ice and ice melting are physical changes that can be easily reversed.
Burning, rusting and cooking are chemical changes that cannot be easily reversed.
A green leaf soaked in alcohol turns the alcohol green because chlorophyll dissolves in alcohol.
Rubbing a plastic ruler, comb or balloon with wool or hair gives it an electric charge, so it attracts small pieces of paper.
A charged object can attract a neutral object, such as a balloon sticking to a wall.
Clothes cling together after a dryer because of static electricity.
A nail left outside in the rain rusts because iron reacts with oxygen and water.
Iron can be heated until it glows and hammered into shapes such as horseshoes because it is malleable and has a high melting point.
Copper is used for electrical wires because it conducts electricity well and can be drawn into wires.
Aluminum foil keeps food warm and is used for cans because it is light, does not rust and can be made into thin sheets.
# situations and what happens: space
The Moon appears to move across the sky during the night because Earth rotates.
The Moon rises about 50 minutes later each day because it moves along its orbit around Earth.
We always see the same side of the Moon because it rotates once in the same time it takes to orbit Earth.
The Moon has many craters because it has no atmosphere to protect it from meteoroids and no weather to erase the craters.
Mercury and the Moon have huge differences between day and night temperatures because they have almost no atmosphere to hold heat.
Earth's surface is warmer than the Moon's average temperature because Earth's atmosphere traps heat.
Venus is hotter than Mercury even though it is farther from the Sun because its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat.
Planets closer to the Sun have shorter years because they travel a shorter path and move faster.
A planet that can support life as we know it needs liquid water, an atmosphere, and a temperature that is not too hot or too cold.
The Sun is a star because it produces its own light and heat by nuclear fusion.
Stars are not visible during the day because sunlight scattered by the atmosphere makes the sky too bright.
Earth is not flat: ships disappear bottom first over the horizon, Earth casts a round shadow on the Moon during a lunar eclipse, and photos from space show a sphere.
Tides are caused mainly by the Moon's gravity, so tidal energy depends on the Moon's pull.
