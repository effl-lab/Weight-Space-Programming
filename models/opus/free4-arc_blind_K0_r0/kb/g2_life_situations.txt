# living things: situations and explanations
Leaves help a tree survive by making food through photosynthesis using sunlight, water and carbon dioxide.
Roots help a plant survive by holding it in the soil and absorbing water and minerals.
A cactus survives with little rain because it stores water in its thick stem, has spines instead of leaves and a waxy coating to reduce water loss.
Plants in dry places often have small, thick or waxy leaves to reduce water loss.
Grass under a tree stays green longer in a drought because the shade reduces evaporation of water from the soil.
A plant watered with salt water wilts because water moves out of its cells by osmosis.
Seeds stored in a cool dry place stay dormant until they get water.
A seed and an egg both contain stored food for the developing young organism.
A moth is usually active at night and rests with its wings flat; a butterfly is usually active during the day and rests with its wings folded up; both are insects.
Moths have feathery antennae and form cocoons; butterflies have thin antennae with knobs and form a chrysalis.
Snowshoe hares and arctic foxes have brown fur in summer and white fur in winter so they stay camouflaged from predators in each season.
Polar bears have white fur for camouflage on snow and ice and black skin to absorb heat.
Animals that can go long periods without water, such as camels and kangaroo rats, survive well in hot deserts.
Many desert animals are active at night when it is cooler, so they lose less water.
Animals that live in groups, such as wolves in packs, can hunt larger prey and protect each other.
A dog panting on a hot day, a person shivering in the cold, and a plant bending toward light are examples of organisms responding to their environment.
Organisms respond to stimuli such as light, temperature, touch, sound, smell and gravity.
A stimulus is a change in the environment that causes a response.
Growth and development are life processes: a puppy grows into a dog, a seed grows into a plant, a tadpole develops into a frog.
All living things grow, reproduce, use energy, respond to their environment, get rid of wastes, and are made of cells.
Nonliving things such as rocks, water, air and sunlight do not grow, reproduce or need food.
A bird building a nest the same way as others of its species, without being taught, is an inherited behavior or instinct.
A child learning to read and a dog learning tricks are learned behaviors.
Birds with longer or stronger beaks that can reach more food survive and have more offspring, so over generations more birds have those beaks.
A mushroom cell has a cell wall but no chloroplasts because fungi cannot make their own food.
Cells with cell walls and chloroplasts come from plants; cells with a cell wall but no chloroplasts may come from fungi or bacteria; cells with no cell wall come from animals.
Onion skin cells have cell walls but no chloroplasts because they grow underground away from light.
Cheek cells are animal cells: they have a nucleus, cytoplasm and cell membrane but no cell wall.
When you exercise, your muscles need more oxygen and produce more carbon dioxide, so breathing becomes faster and deeper and the heart beats faster.
Breathing in smoke damages the lungs and the respiratory system.
The small intestine has a very large surface area because of folds and villi, so more nutrients can be absorbed into the blood.
Eating a balanced diet and exercising keep the heart, bones and muscles healthy.
A broken bone heals because bone cells divide to make new bone.
A cut heals because skin cells divide to replace damaged cells, and platelets help the blood clot.
Fever is the body raising its temperature to fight infection.
Sneezing and coughing remove irritants from the nose and airways but can spread germs.
Tears and mucus trap germs, and stomach acid kills many germs in food.
Vaccines cause the body to make antibodies so it can fight a germ quickly if it is exposed later.
The body gets energy from food through digestion and cellular respiration.
Oxygen from the lungs is carried by red blood cells to all the cells of the body.
Carbon dioxide made by cells is carried by the blood to the lungs and exhaled.
Kidneys filter wastes from the blood and make urine.
The liver removes poisons from the blood and makes bile.
Bones and muscles work together to move the body: muscles pull on bones at joints.
When the biceps contracts, the arm bends; when the triceps contracts, the arm straightens.
The brain and nerves control the muscles.
Touching something hot causes a reflex in which the hand pulls away before the brain feels the pain.
In bright light the pupil gets smaller to let in less light; in dim light the pupil gets larger to let in more light.
Most of a household's water is used for flushing toilets, showers and baths, and washing clothes.
# ecosystems: situations and explanations
There are fewer hawks than mice in a meadow because energy is lost at each level of a food chain, so less energy is available for top predators.
In an energy pyramid there are many more producers than top predators.
If hawks decrease, the mice and snakes they eat increase at first.
If snakes decrease in a food web where owls eat snakes and mice, owls eat more mice.
If mice decrease, animals that eat mice, such as owls, hawks and snakes, have less food and may decrease.
A long drought in a grassland means fewer plants grow, so herbivore populations decline, and then predator populations decline.
A forest fire clears trees so more sunlight reaches the ground and grasses and shrubs grow first.
Mushrooms growing on a dead log are decomposers breaking down the wood and recycling nutrients into the soil.
Earthworms loosen the soil so air and water can reach plant roots, and their wastes add nutrients.
Bees, butterflies, hummingbirds and bats pollinate flowers while feeding on nectar.
Animals such as squirrels and birds spread seeds by burying nuts or eating fruit.
Oak trees provide food such as acorns and shelter for many animals.
Coral reefs provide food and shelter for many kinds of fish.
Wetlands filter water, prevent floods and provide habitats for birds, fish and frogs.
Kelp forests are underwater forests of large algae that provide habitats for many sea animals.
Sea otters eat sea urchins, which eat kelp, so without sea otters the kelp forest is destroyed.
Lichens are a partnership of a fungus and an alga; they can grow on bare rock and are pioneer species.
Bacteria in the human intestine help digest food and make vitamins, an example of mutualism.
Cattle egrets eat insects stirred up by grazing cattle, an example of commensalism.
Mistletoe and ticks are parasites that take nutrients from their hosts.
Ladybugs eat aphids, so farmers use ladybugs to control aphid pests without chemicals.
Introducing a natural predator to control a pest is called biological control.
A food web is more stable when it has many different species, because if one species decreases, others can take its place as food.
Animals compete for food, water, shelter, space and mates; plants compete for sunlight, water, space and nutrients.
When two species compete for the same food, the better adapted species may survive and the other may move or die out.
Cows, horses, sheep and deer are herbivores with flat teeth for grinding grass.
Lions, wolves and sharks are carnivores with sharp teeth for tearing meat.
Bears, raccoons and humans are omnivores.
